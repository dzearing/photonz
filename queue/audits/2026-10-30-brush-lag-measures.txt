Brush time per pointer move, p95 in ms, measured in the probe at Next defaults on this Mac (M4 Max,
Retina screen at 2x), 10 pt a move, 120 moves a drag, before -> after this change
(every-brush-follows-the-pointer-with-no-lag-even, 2026-10-30). Same walks both columns:
2026-10-30-brush-lag-m-*.walk (made by 2026-10-30-brush-lag-walks.py), run with
Scripts/playtest.sh <file> --no-build. "Before" is the code at the start of the task
(dc527729; the empty-layer chalk/crayon/marker/airbrush before was not measured).

Why it lagged: a hand sends a move every 8 to 16 ms and a big brush lays a dab only every
few moves (one every 50 pt at 500 pt), so the cores sat idle between dabs and the system
slowed them down. The same 500 pt oil dab took 4.8 ms with moves back to back, 10.7 ms with
8 ms gaps between them, and 5.3 ms with one core kept running beside it (release build,
bench linked against PhotonzRender). So most of the lag was not the medium at all.

What changed:
- One core is kept running while a stroke is under way (CoresReady): every move asks for a
  quarter second more, it stops by itself after the last move. One thread, never more.
- Pixel work split across all performance cores but one (11 here, was 8).
- Oil: per-dab scratch (13 MB at 500 pt on a screenshot) kept from dab to dab instead of
  allocated and zeroed every dab; what the bristles picked up summed across cores, each core
  its own bristles in the original order; each bristle's flow and tint once a dab, not once
  a pixel; the bristle under a pixel found by stepping from its neighbour's.
- Dry media: value-noise corners read from a lattice worked out once a dab (same numbers);
  a tip's box cut to where it can lay anything (chalk scanned 1.6 radii, can lay to 1.1).
- Byte for byte the same pixels: BigBrushKeepsItsPixelsTests (every medium, 160 pt at 1.5x,
  landed and as drawn) and PaintKeepsItsPixelsTests hold fingerprints taken before the change.

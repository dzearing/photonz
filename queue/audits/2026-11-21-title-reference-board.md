# Title reference board

For task `keynote-clean-soft-gradient-glass-kinetic-bold-t`: what refined title
slides and title cards actually do with colour and type, written down before
any redesign. Each entry names the source, its palette, its typeface and size
ratio, its tracking, its layout grid and the one typographic idea it carries.

**How this was made, honestly.** No frames were downloaded or copied into the
repo (they are other people's copyrighted work). Each entry is described from
the published work and, where the owner publishes them, from its brand colours
(Stripe, Linear, Vercel, Apple's web palette). Hex values without "(brand)" are
approximations of what the frame reads as, not samples, and a few are marked
"approx." where the memory is loose. Ratios are headline cap height to the
secondary line, rounded. The rules at the bottom are what the board agrees on;
they are copied into `docs/design/video-titles-polish.md` ("Colour and type
rules for title pages") for every later title.

## What the board agrees on

Out of 28 entries, 27 stand on a near-black, an off-white or a warm paper; one
(Stripe Sessions, #13) stands on a deep navy, and even there the violet is a
glow, not the ground. None stands on a saturated purple or an electric blue.

### Colour

1. **The ground is a neutral with a temperature.** Warm black (`#0B0A09`),
   cool black (`#08090A`), ink navy (`#0A2540`), off-white (`#F5F5F7`), warm
   paper (`#F4F1EC`), stone. A saturated hue never fills the frame.
2. **One accent at most, and it takes the ground's temperature:** brass on warm
   black, vermilion on ink, sage on stone, amber on graphite, blush on oxblood.
   It covers a tenth of the frame or less, and it is used for one job (a rule,
   a caret, a kicker, the second line).
3. **Type is never pure white on pure black.** Ivory (`#EFE8DC`), Apple's
   `#F5F5F7`, Vercel's `#EDEDED`; on light grounds ink (`#1D1D1F`), not black.
4. **The secondary line is the same family of colour, lower in value**, about
   half to two thirds of the headline's lightness. A new hue for the sub is
   the template tell.
5. **A headline reads at 7:1 or better against its ground; small type at 4.5:1
   or better.**
6. **Violet and electric blue appear only as light**, a glow at a fifth of
   its strength over a deep ground, never as the field the words stand on.
7. **A moving ground is light, not pattern.** If a person can count the specks
   or see the waves, it is too strong; it should read as dust in a beam, a
   slow change of light, a grid you notice on the second look.

### Type

1. **One typographic idea per title**, named: Didone hairlines at display size,
   roman against italic, a caps stack at tight leading, a monospaced system, a
   serif over tracked caps. Plain centred SF Pro with a grey line under it is
   not an idea.
2. **Tracking moves against size.** Display sizes are set tight (-1% to -3%,
   condensed caps about 0); small labels and kickers open up (+10% to +35%,
   caps).
3. **Scale contrast of about 3:1 to 8:1** between the headline and its small
   line (board median about 4.5:1). Two sizes per title.
4. **Two families at most, and when two, contrasted in structure**: serif with
   sans, mono with proportional, roman with italic. Never two similar sans.
5. **Display leading 0.85 to 1.05**; a caps stack as low as 0.85.
6. **Layout on a grid**: dead centre and symmetric (film, Apple) or flush left
   at about an eighth of the width (editorial, Stripe, Vercel). Words are not
   "centred in a box" with nothing else holding them.
7. **Ornament is structural**: a rule, a caret, a number, a crop. No rounded
   squares, blobs or stars standing in for a logo.

### Faces that ship with every Mac and serve these ideas

Didot and Bodoni 72 (Didone, with italics), New York (Apple's serif),
Baskerville, Big Caslon, Iowan Old Style, Helvetica Neue (every weight),
Avenir Next and Avenir Next Condensed, Futura (with condensed cuts), DIN
Condensed, SF Pro, SF Mono and Menlo. All are in `/System/Library/Fonts` or
`/System/Library/Fonts/Supplemental` on macOS 26, so a title made with them
opens the same on any Mac. An italic is named by its face (`Didot-Italic`,
`BodoniSvtyTwoITCTT-BookIta`), which the renderer finds by name.

small window with a player, a floating dark bar and a strip of blocks, all behind
`next-cut-a-recording`, which is on by default in Next, so the user already has

into pieces, click a piece, Delete to drop it, drag the trim handles, Command to
free a handle from a cut. All of that is real and now proven on screen. What is

### Information architecture
- **Saving a recording somewhere else is the one export in the app with no
  sheet.** Every picture and icon leaves through `ExportDialog`, which offers the
  format, the quality, and what the file will weigh (two tasks landed that
  deliberately: `da51700a` for PNG, `a4a7dbf7` for SVG). `AppCoordinator.saveRecording`
  at `AppCoordinator.swift:389` builds a bare `NSSavePanel`, with the format
  already chosen by which of three menu items you picked and no weight at all.
  MP4 has no quality control: `VideoExporter.exportMP4` is pinned to
  `AVAssetExportPresetHighestQuality`. A screen recording is the file most likely
  to be too big to send, and the user's whole sentence ended in "send in teams".
  Filed: `saving-a-recording-somewhere-else-goes-through-t`, dependent on the p0
  because the p0 uses that same path as its workaround.
- **The recording window's bar teaches two vocabularies.** Idle it is nine
  unlabelled icon glyphs with no grouping; in trim mode most of them vanish and
  three text buttons appear with two icon buttons beside them. And the accent
  outline means "this is the piece you are holding" on the strip and "this is the
  part the handles keep" one keystroke later in trim mode: same drawing, opposite
  senses of keep and drop. Not filed, folded with the pictures into
  `refine-the-video-design-so-it-reads-as-the-same`, which exists to answer
  exactly this and whose surface is retired by the foundation task anyway.

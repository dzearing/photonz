# Should the app ask what you are making?

## What this is about

When you make something new in Photonz today, the app asks one question: **how
big?** A list of five screen sizes and a Custom row. It never asks what the
canvas is for.

That has a cost you can see straight away. Somebody sitting down to draw a 24
point icon has to already know that 24 by 24 is the answer, and that they will
want to be zoomed right in, and that they want a pixel grid up. And once they
are drawing, the toolbar offers them the measure tool, the arrow, the
highlighter, the zoom callout and the blur lens, none of which has anything to
do with drawing a glyph. One day it would offer them a video timeline too.

So: **should the app ask what you are making, and should the answer decide what
you get to see afterwards?** Those are two separate questions and the study
found they have different answers.

Study page: <http://127.0.0.1:8791/index.html#project-kinds>
(Icons ▸ "Does the app need project types?"). It is mostly pictures, including
the New sheet drawn as it would look, and the same editor drawn twice under two
different kinds with everything the kind removes still shown, struck through, so
the removal is visible.

## What you would see under each answer

### A · Ask, then keep everything (recommended)

File ▸ New shows four starting points: **Icon**, **Screen**, **Capture**,
**Recording**, each with what it is for written underneath. Picking Icon makes a
24 point frame, zooms you in far enough to draw, puts keylines up and readies an
SVG export. That is the whole of what the choice does. It is a setup, not a
setting: the toolbar keeps every tool, and the panel on the right changes
because of what is on the canvas, not because of what you said at the start.

You would notice it as the app knowing how to start something, and never as the
app having opinions about what you are allowed to do.

### B · Ask, and show only what that kind needs

The same sheet, but the answer sticks. Drawing an icon, five of the thirteen
tool slots leave the bar. Editing a recording, seven leave. There is a
"Show everything" switch for when you need one back.

You would notice it as a quieter window, and — sooner or later — as a tool that
is not where you left it. Section 3 of the study walks five ordinary moments
where this happens; the one that costs most is silent: you paste a screenshot
into an icon document to trace from, look for the measure readout, do not find
it, and conclude the app cannot measure.

### C · Change nothing at the start

New keeps asking only how big. All the work goes into the right hand panel
following what you are working in, so a 24 point frame brings the icon size
previews and a 1440 screen brings layout and measurements, changing as you move
around the same document.

You would notice the panel getting smarter and the opening moment staying
exactly as blank as it is now.

### D · Leave it alone

Nothing changes. Worth picking if this is not what you want the next stretch of
work spent on. **Choosing this retires the task rather than sending it back.**

## The thing that decided it

One Photonz document can hold a **24 point icon frame beside a 1440 point
screen**, today, and both are real work: it is how you check that an icon reads
at the size it will actually be seen. The study draws that document.

Ask that document what kind it is and there is no true answer. The app is
already right about both halves at the same moment — it knows the 24 is an icon
and the 1440 is not, and it starts a fresh line at 2 pixels in one and 4 points
in the other — because the context follows the **frame you are working in**
rather than a label on the file. A kind chosen at the start is one answer for
the whole document forever, decided at the moment you know least.

## Two things worth knowing before you choose

**The crowding is not really in the toolbar.** A separate study measured the
right hand panel asking for **1052 points of the 968** the largest window can
give, with three layers and one piece of text selected. Hiding five tool buttons
does not touch that.

**Hiding helps the redlining case not at all.** Counted honestly, a Capture
document loses zero of the thirteen tool slots, because measuring and annotating
a screenshot wants nearly everything. Capture and redline is the main way this
app gets used.

## What happens either way

Under A, C or D nothing is stored in a document, so a file made today opens the
same in any build and a file made last year gets every improvement since. Under
B the kind lives in the file, which is a promise every document has to keep from
then on, including the ones that already exist and have no kind to give.

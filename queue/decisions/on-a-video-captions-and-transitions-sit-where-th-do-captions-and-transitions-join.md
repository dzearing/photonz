# Do Captions and Transitions join the lists pane on a video?

## What this is about

On 2026-10-13 you chose how the right hand panel splits: **what you picked on
top, and the document's lists in a pane at the bottom** that keeps its height
whatever you pick, with a grip between the two (Photoshop's Properties over
Layers). That is built. On a picture the pane holds Layers, Measurements and
the Library.

On a video the timeline already stands in for Layers, so the lists are
**Captions** (where you make captions) and **Transitions** (the tiles you drag
onto a cut). The brief for that card said they would join the pane. This card
asks whether they still should, now that it has been tried.

## What happened when they joined the pane

Both are forms rather than lists: they cannot be shortened the way the Layers
list can, so the pane has to hold their whole height or scroll. On a laptop
sized window over a video, with a piece of music picked:

| | Pick's part | Sections you can see without scrolling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One column (today) | about 395pt | Properties, Channel, Fades |
| Pane at 40 per cent | 235pt | Properties, Channel; Fades starts at 244pt, just under the line |

The audio mock (`http://127.0.0.1:8791/index.html#video-audio`) shows Fades on
screen with a sound picked, so for now they stay at the foot of the pick's
column on a video, and this card asks what you want.

## The options

### A. Keep them under the clip, no pane on a video (recommended)

One column on a video, as today: the pick, then Captions and Transitions at the
foot. The pane appears on a video only if you fold the timeline away and the
Layers list comes back.

### B. Move them into the pane, which takes 40 per cent

Same layout as a picture. On a laptop the pick's part is short, and a sound's
Fades sits just below the line until you drag the grip down (the grip
remembers).

### C. Move them into the pane, folded to their headers

They sit in the pane but start folded, so the pane is two header rows and the
pick keeps the height. Opening one grows the pane to show it.

# Putting the pieces of a recording in a different order

## What exists today

Photonz records your screen and opens the recording in its own window. There is
a strip under the picture showing the whole recording end to end. You can:

- **Cut it** where the playhead is, with the B key or the scissors button. The
  strip then shows two blocks instead of one.
- **Throw a piece away** by clicking it and pressing Delete. What is left closes
  up by itself, so there is never a hole to drag shut.
- **Trim the ends** by dragging the two handles in.
- **Crop the picture**, and save or export what is left.

What you cannot do is change the ORDER. The pieces play in the order they were
recorded, always. If the good take is at the end, it stays at the end.

## What the question is about

Adding one gesture to that strip: pick a piece up and drop it somewhere else.

Nothing new appears on screen until you drag. The strip looks exactly as it does
now; the difference is that a block on it can be moved, the way a row in the
layers list can be moved.

## What you would see under each answer

**Drag a piece to move it.** You hold a block for a moment, it lifts, and as you
move it the other blocks open a space where it would land. Let go and the
recording plays in the new order at once: the playhead, the strip and anything
you export all agree. Undo puts it straight back.

**Finish what cutting does first.** The strip stays as it is. The next stretch of
video work goes on the pieces you can already make: a trim handle that catches
on a cut as it goes past, so trimming exactly to a join is not a steady hand; the
piece you picked staying visible when you go into trim mode, which it does not
today; a clearer sign of what is about to be thrown away. All three are already
written up and waiting.

**Somewhere else entirely.** Video stops here for now and the loop spends the
next stretch on icons, redlining or building UI instead. What is built stays
built and stays behind its own switch.

## One thing worth knowing before you answer

Every bit of video work so far has been built without anybody seeing it run.
This Mac's screen has been locked since 2026-09-15, and while it is locked the
app cannot be driven by a script or photographed, so the strip, the blocks and
the readouts have been checked by tests and by reading the code and by nothing
else. That is true of the cutting you have already got, and it would be true of
anything built next.

If seeing it matters more than adding to it, unlocking the Mac for a while is
worth more than any of the three answers here.

## Where to try what exists

In the **Next** release, turn on "Cut a recording into pieces and drop the one
you do not want" in the Experiments window. Record something, press B in the
middle of it, click a block, press Delete.

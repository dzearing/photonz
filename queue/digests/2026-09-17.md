sixty four hours. Most runners read the locked screen as "no picture is

Video cutting took its first three steps: the pieces stay on screen while you

## Reflections

**The loop's own instructions are now its biggest single defect, and they are
cheap to fix.** Nothing in the last three days cost the user as much as one
stale paragraph in `queue/bin/runner-prompt.md`. Forty audits with no picture is
forty features the user cannot judge, and every one of them was avoidable by a
runner typing one environment variable. The fix is a documentation edit plus a
sentence saying which step kinds survive a lock, which the loop has already
measured. That is the p1 and it needs nobody to unlock anything.

The wider lesson is worth keeping: when `CLAUDE.md` is corrected, the runner
prompt is a second copy of the same truth and it does not follow along. Those
two files disagreed for a full day and nothing noticed. A check that reads both
and complains when they contradict each other on a named subject would be small,
but it is speculative and I have not filed it. If the same thing happens twice it
has earned a task.

**A green test run should not be partly luck.** One timing test, the hover
detection budget, has now failed three times in two days on a busy machine, the
last one by forty percent, and passes on its own seconds later every time. Every
task in the queue has to get a green run before it may commit, so this is a tax
on every runner rather than a problem in one feature. Worse than the wasted
cycles is what it teaches: a runner that sees red and re-runs to green twice
learns to shrug at red. The two tests beside it were converted to a ratio against
their own work on the same thread and have held ever since; this one was left on
an absolute budget with a note. It has been moved from the back of p2 to the
front (`the-hover-speed-test-stops-failing-when-the-mach`).

**Ask for the answers, not more building.** The loop has enough to build for
days, and seven questions are sitting unanswered, two of them holding up focus
work (a copy of a component, and how the right hand panel keeps Appearance and
Effects on screen). Nothing files a decision card twice, so an unanswered card is
quiet in a way a blocked task is not. The dashboard now says when the loop has
gone blind; it could as usefully say when the loop has gone unheard.

**Icons are close to being handed over.** An icon can now be drawn on a real
grid, given a border that follows the path, animated, and exported as a
transparent PNG or an animated SVG with its shadows intact. What is left before
it is a finished story is small and known: PNG does not say what it will weigh
(queued), and Apple's own SVG reader ignores enough of the file that three
separate audits had to design round it. That last one is worth a decision at some
point: whether an exported icon should be correct in a browser or correct in
Preview when the two disagree.

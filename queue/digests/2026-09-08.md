# Daily digest 2026-09-08

## Summary

The biggest thing: the right hand panel stopped being a pile of rows and became
two lists with a rule behind them. Appearance now holds what a shape simply is
(opacity, fill, outline, a corner radius only where there are corners) and
Effects is a list you add to, starting empty, holding as many shadows, glows,
blurs and borders as you like. That split landed with the sentence that decides
it, so the next thing to be added knows which side it goes on, and it dragged a
week of loose ends in with it: blur now actually softens a shape instead of
doing nothing, a border follows an oval instead of drawing a square round it,
corner radius rounds every ring on the shape rather than just the fill, an
effect can glow as well as darken, and an effect's colour moved out of the row
header into the effect's own settings where it picked up the saved colours menu
every other colour in the app has. The tick that switches a row on now leads
every row in both lists, from one shared row head, so a list added later cannot
come out reversed.

Fifty two tasks finished in the last 24 hours across 138 commits. Beyond the
panel, three threads:

- **The marquee grew up.** It answers to M and a second press swaps the box for
  the ellipse. It is part of the undo history, so drawing, nudging, dragging and
  clearing one can all be taken back. It keeps the layer you had picked when it
  catches nothing, and it now says which of the two boxes you are drawing while
  you are still drawing it: a band that has caught something closes into one
  unbroken blue line and washes what it would take. Cut and delete over a
  marquee on a shape refuse in one line that says why, and Turn Into Picture is
  the way out they name, so the dead end has an exit.
- **Components learned to say which version they are.** A version lands in free
  space rather than on top of whatever was there, the canvas travels to it, a
  copy prints the version it is showing under its name in the layers list, and
  four versions standing side by side read as Default, Disabled, Loading and
  Pressed instead of four labels fighting for the same line.
- **The dock got a height budget.** Forms are drawn whole and paid for first,
  lists share what is left and scroll inside themselves, and a body that has
  been shortened fades at its cut edge so a clipped control does not read as a
  rendering fault.

Two things worth knowing that are not features: the design dashboard was
re-downloading the whole queue fifteen times a minute (3.2MB a poll became 62KB,
and the node process went from 52% of a CPU to about 3%), and thirteen scripted
walks that had gone red are green again, with a new `reveal` step that scrolls
until a control is pressable instead of the hand-tuned scroll distances that go
stale every time the dock grows.

Three decisions were opened and three resolved. Two are still waiting on the
user: what drawing a shape should give you now that Outline is leaving
Appearance, and where a picked thing's settings should sit in the panel. Five
runner failures, all recovered: three were network blips, two were runners
killed waiting on a forty minute walk sweep.

## Reflections

**The refusal is becoming a real surface, and it needs a rule.** Three commands
learned to say why they cannot act this week, each answering it on its own, and
the fourth (Option Backspace over a shape) still says nothing at all. The
existing rule covers a button that cannot act, and a key press has nothing to
dim, so there is no row in the table for it. Queued as
`the-rules-say-what-a-key-press-does-when-it-cann` (p3, design system) and, for
the thing it blocks, `a-canvas-notice-can-carry-the-way-out` (p2), so the pill
that names Turn Into Picture can offer it rather than send you hunting.

**Undo hands back the drawing and drops the picking.** This turned up in three
separate audits in one day, each one saying the same sentence and each one
leaving it alone because it was not that task's job. It is the most common step
in the app and every undo currently costs a re-pick, so the task that owned one
case has been broadened to the rule behind it and raised to p1
(`undo-brings-the-band-back-without-bringing-back`, reproduced on HEAD, evidence
in its notes).

**Process: the loop is now good at catching itself lying.** Three tasks in the
last day were closed with "the bug as reported did not exist", each proved by
running the thing rather than reading the code: the measurement walk, the
version labels that were said to print on top of each other, and the tick order
said to have reversed. The rule added on 2026-09-07 requiring a filed bug to
carry its reproduction is doing exactly what it was meant to. The remaining hole
is at the other end: two runners were killed waiting on the full walk sweep,
which takes forty minutes against a ten minute ceiling, and both were focus
tasks. Queued as `a-long-walk-sweep-does-not-cost-a-task-its-runne`.

**A feature idea worth having, not queued yet.** Turn Into Picture asks before
it bakes a shape, with a Do not ask again box, and that is the first question of
its kind in the app. It will not be the last: flattening, exporting a selection
and merging down all want one. Worth deciding once whether Photonz is an app
that asks or an app that trusts undo, rather than one command at a time. Folded
into the rules task above as the second half rather than queued separately.

## Design review

**Is the IA sound?** One rule bent and it bent honestly. Building Turn Into
Picture, the runner wanted to put the way out inside the refusal pill and
stopped, because section 3 of UX-PATTERNS says a canvas notice "takes no input,
has no close control". That is the rule working: a runner met the vocabulary,
did not invent local chrome, and wrote down what it cost (three steps instead of
one). It is a finding all the same, because the rule is now making the product
worse in a case nobody had in mind when it was written. Queued as
`a-canvas-notice-can-carry-the-way-out`, which is explicitly required to settle
the rule in the audit before building anything.

The Appearance and Effects split, by contrast, conformed exactly: new capability
is a new group, and it got one.

**Are we missing components or guidance?** One extraction was done right without
being asked: `PanelRowHead` is now the only thing that orders a tick against a
name, so the two lists cannot drift, and the settings indent is derived from the
same value rather than typed twice. That is the pattern to keep.

Two gaps in guidance rather than components. The suppressible question
(RasterizePrompt) invented its wording, its suppression key and its storage
locally because there was nothing to copy. And the key-press refusal has been
hand-rolled three times. Both queued into
`the-rules-say-what-a-key-press-does-when-it-cann`.

**What UX issues keep recurring?** Three patterns, each with more than one
sighting:

1. *Things fall below the fold in the right hand panel.* The dock height budget
   shipped, and it still bit twice afterwards: two glows push Position and Size
   out of view (`2026-09-08-glow.json`), and picking a text layer leaves its
   settings below the fold, which is the one walk still red
   (`picking-a-text-layer-leaves-its-settings-below-t`, blocked on a user
   decision). Five separate fixes in two days each trimmed one section instead
   of adopting the rule, which is what the queued doc scrub
   `the-dock-rules-say-plainly-that-they-govern-the` exists to stop.
2. *Undo restores the thing but not the picking.* Named in
   `2026-09-08-stack-band-undo.json`, `2026-09-08-sweep-delete-undo.json` and
   `2026-09-07-marquee-keeps-your-layer.json`. Now one p1.
3. *A key press that cannot act answers for itself.* Cut, delete and fill, three
   answers, one of them silence. Covered above.

**Do the documented rules need a scrub?** Two queued, both named above:
`the-rules-say-what-a-key-press-does-when-it-cann` (UX-PATTERNS sections 3 and
4) and the already-open `the-dock-rules-say-plainly-that-they-govern-the`
(section 3 and D9, whose rule keeps being violated because it does not say
plainly that it binds the app and not only the mock pages).

**Judging the focus feature as a user.** Reading the component and layout audits
cold, the flow holds up until you type a number into Room, and then it does two
opposite things for a reason you cannot see. On a loose drawing the box grows
outward and nothing moves. On a drawing where a piece has been named the
surface, the pieces move and the box stays pinned. Whether a group has a surface
is invisible, and the audit that shipped the first behaviour says so itself:
"two different answers to the same field, and it is only obvious once you know a
surface is what makes the difference". The same audits ask whether Padding is
even the right word for a row sitting next to a Layout section that already says
Padding. Filed as `room-on-a-group-means-one-thing-whether-or-not-a` (p2,
ui-layout), which is not allowed to close by picking one behaviour quietly: if
the two answers stay different, the group has to say which one you are about to
get before you type.

## Ready to try

Everything below is playtestable in `dist/Photonz Dev.app` on the Next release,
and each audit says what to do, step by step.

**Appearance is what it is, Effects is what you add**
(`queue/audits/2026-09-07-appearance-effects.json`). Draw a shape and read the
panel: what the shape simply has sits under Layers, and an empty Effects list
sits under that with a plus. Add two shadows, an inner shadow and a border, drag
them into a different order, switch one off. This is the change most worth
arguing with, because it decides where everything added later goes.

**An effect can glow** (`queue/audits/2026-09-08-glow.json`). A soft coloured
halo outside the edge, or a lit edge inside it, more than one at a time, in
different colours. The starting size and softness were raised once already
because the first defaults read as a smudge on white, so judge the default hard.

**A box says what it picks** (`queue/audits/2026-09-08-a-box-says-what-it-picks.json`).
There are two boxes you can drag over the picture and until now they looked
identical. A band that has caught nothing keeps crawling black dashes; one that
has caught something stops dead, closes into one unbroken blue line and washes
what it would take. The wash only lives while the button is down, deliberately,
so it never lies over a screenshot you are redlining.

**Turn a shape or a piece of text into a picture**
(`queue/audits/2026-09-08-turn-into-a-picture.json`). Marquee half a rectangle,
press Delete, read the line that comes back, and follow it. Two questions in
there for you: is Turn Into Picture the right name, or would you rather it said
Rasterize, and is being asked first worth the click.

**The marquee answers to M** (`queue/audits/2026-09-08-marquee-answers-to-m.json`)
and **undo puts back a marquee you lost**
(`queue/audits/2026-09-08-selection-undo.json`). M from any tool, a second press
to swap box for ellipse, and Command Z now takes back drawing, nudging, dragging
or clearing one. The deliberate departure from Photoshop parity is in the first
audit's rough notes: we swap without Shift.

**The panel reads the marquee**
(`queue/audits/2026-09-08-panel-reads-the-marquee.json`). Sweep a box round a
button in a screenshot and Position and Size becomes Selection, giving you the
exact size and offset. This is redlining work that landed inside a
component-focused week, and it is the fastest way to check the split panel did
not cost the measuring flow anything.

**Version labels on the canvas**
(`queue/audits/2026-09-08-version-labels.json`) and **a copy says which version
it is** (`queue/audits/2026-09-08-copy-version-row.json`). Four versions of a
button side by side now read Default, Disabled, Loading, Pressed. The trade is
in the audit and it is the first thing to argue with: a component with two or
more drawings no longer shows its name on the canvas at all.

**Room on a loose drawing grows the box outward**
(`queue/audits/2026-09-08-room-grows-outward.json`). Group two shapes, type 40
into Padding, and nothing moves while the box closes 40 further out on every
side. Read this one next to
`queue/audits/2026-09-07-room-lands-on-something.json`, because between them
they are the finding in the design review above.

Also playable, in one line each: blur that actually softens
(`2026-09-07-blur-softens-a-shape.json`), a border that follows an oval
(`2026-09-08-ellipse-border.json`), corner radius that rounds every ring
(`2026-09-08-corner-radius-rounds.json`), an effect's colour with the saved
colours menu (`2026-09-08-effect-colour.json`), the tick leading every row
(`2026-09-08-tick-leads.json`), the fill keys saying which colours they will use
(`2026-09-08-fill-colours.json`), dropping a picture handing you the pointer
(`2026-09-08-drop-hands-you-the-pointer.json`), one undo for a sweep and delete
(`2026-09-08-sweep-delete-undo.json`) and for a stack made from a band
(`2026-09-08-stack-band-undo.json`), effects remembered for the next shape
(`2026-09-07-effects-remembered.json`), grid settings on a narrow window

opens on cards rather than three hundred file names

## Reflections

filed the batch the loop then burned through in a day. The queue came into this
digest with 2 ready tasks against a low water mark of 3, so a refill was already
due on the next tick. That is the system working, but it does mean the day's
whole direction is set by one pass and then executed without re-aiming. Worth
considering: let a task that finds three follow-ups file them itself with an
epic, which several runners already do informally in their rough lists.

**Rough lists are the best queue input we have.** Every one of the seven tasks
filed today came out of a "rough" line an earlier runner wrote about its own
work. Those lines are specific, honest and already scoped. They are better task
seeds than anything a fresh reader of the code produces.

**Task titles are drifting into two styles.** Roughly half name the fix ("A
second paste lands where you can see it") and half name the defect ("The panel
takes a file the app cannot use and does nothing with it"). Both read fine
alone; together the dashboard is hard to scan, because you cannot tell at a
glance whether a row is a want or a complaint. Not worth a rename pass over 250
finished tasks, worth agreeing on one style for new ones.

**Where the app should go next, beyond the queue.** A stack that can be a fixed
size, hold its rows, and re-measure its text is most of a real layout engine. The
piece that would make it feel like one is room on the four sides of a container
(queued today as `a-stack-can-be-given-room-on-each-of-its-four-si`). After that
the honest gap is that you still cannot say "this row is 2 of 3 columns", which
is what the pending column grid decision is really about.

## Design review

**Is the IA sound? Two rules bent, both in the panel vocabulary.**

The first: the right hand dock became a document drop target
(`2026-09-04-panel-takes-picture.json`) while its own rows and sections still own
their reordering drags. UX-PATTERNS section 3 describes the dock's surfaces and
says nothing about what any of them do with a file held over them, so each
surface answered separately and the panel now reads as two surfaces. The audit
says this itself: a picture let go on a layer row lands on top of the stack, not
at that row, and there is no highlight, no drop line and no glow the whole time
you are holding it. Queued as
`a-picture-held-over-the-right-hand-panel-shows-w`, with adding the missing rule
to section 3 as part of the same sitting.

The second: UX-PATTERNS section 6 says tool options are shown "in the options bar
or the top of the Inspector". There is no options bar. So every tool setting
lives in the Inspector, and hiding the Inspector, which is a normal thing to do,
takes away the zoom callout's shape, the wand's tolerance and crop's aspect with
no way back. `2026-09-04-callout-shape.json` calls this "the house rule" and
follows it rather than fixing it, which is the sound of a rule nobody believes.
`2026-09-04-callout-one-home.json` is the same story from the other side: the
popover that used to hold tool settings turned out to be unreachable code,
because picking up a drawing tool clears the layer selection. Queued as
`the-settings-for-the-tool-in-your-hand-are-reach`, which must either build the
strip the doc promises or change the doc, not leave them disagreeing.

**Are we missing components or guidance? Yes, three, all now queued.**

*Drop feedback.* Built once for the canvas (`2026-09-03-drop-preview.json`,
`2026-09-04-drop-lands-here.json`) and never for the panel or the window chrome,
so five separate tasks in two days each decided locally what a drop looks like.
Extraction candidate, queued above.

*A read-only number.* `2026-09-04-text-height-readout.json` says it plainly in
its own rough list: the H field "wears the same rounded box as the fields you can
type in. Only the grey tells you apart, and a box that looks like an input but
refuses the keyboard is the clumsiest part of this". Clicking it gives no
feedback at all. The same change creates more read-only fields for locked and
stacked layers, so this is not one field. Queued as
`a-number-you-cannot-type-into-stops-looking-like`; losing the border is a look
change, so it should come to the user as a decision card with two shots.

*Mixed.* `2026-09-04-type-menu-labels.json`: the word sits at full strength in a
menu and dimmed on an Effects slider, and the alignment rows just go blank, which
is a third answer to the same question. UX-PATTERNS line 244 says a row answers
"Mixed" and never says what that looks like. Queued as
`mixed-reads-the-same-way-in-every-control`.

**What UX issues keep recurring? One pattern dominated the day.**

*A control that cannot do anything.* Six fixes, six different answers. Locked
layers stopped drawing resize handles, so the control was removed
(`2026-09-04-locked-layer-handles.json`). The Position and Size caption kept its
control and explained instead (`2026-09-04-locked-caption.json`). A stacked
layer's axis row was replaced by the sentence "Set by the stack"
(`2026-09-04-stack-owns-axis.json`). A placement rule that stopped mattering was
made deliberately invisible (`2026-09-04-inert-placement-rule.json`). Stretch on
text was kept and taught to do something (`2026-09-04-text-fills-height.json`).
The text height field was kept, dimmed, and left looking editable
(`2026-09-04-text-height-readout.json`). Two of the six also note that their
explanation only appears on hover and say that is too quiet. This is not six bugs,
it is one missing rule, queued as
`one-rule-for-a-control-that-cannot-do-anything` against UX-PATTERNS section 4.

*Drops answer differently depending on where you are over the window.* Covered
above; five tasks, converging but not yet one thing.

*Audits hand back a photograph where they should hand back a press.* Three
audits in one day said the scripted walk could not click the control it was
testing, because the panel is drawn by SwiftUI and swallows the synthesized
event: `2026-09-04-inert-placement-rule.json` ("The Clear button was not clicked
by the scripted walk"), `2026-09-04-panel-arms-tool.json` and
`2026-09-04-typed-width-floor.json`. Almost everything in the focus feature lives
in that panel, so this is the loop's own blind spot sitting exactly on top of the
thing it is building.

**Rule scrubs queued.** UX-PATTERNS section 4 (no rule for a control that cannot
act) as its own task; section 3 (no rule for what a drop target looks like) and
section 6 (the options bar the doc promises and the app does not have) folded
into the two feature tasks that have to answer them anyway, so the doc and the
code move together.

**The focus feature, judged as a user.** Read from
`2026-09-03-auto-layout.json` and `2026-09-03-ui-components-placement.json`, both
of which are genuinely followable: setup, numbered steps, real screen captures,
and questions that ask what felt wrong rather than what worked. Someone could sit
down with either and get somewhere. What is still clumsy: a stack has one gap
number and no room on its sides, so any real card is still built by nudging
pieces the stack then undoes; and a locked layer inside a stack is still picked
up and moved by the stack, so the one thing you locked is the one thing that
keeps moving, which is the last place lock does not mean lock after three fixes
elsewhere today. Both are queued. One ceremonial edge worth watching but not
queued: X and Y stop taking typing for a layer inside a stack, so the only way to
move one row is to drag it or change the gap. The audit calls that honest, and it
is, but it is the kind of honest that a first-time user reads as broken.

## Ready to try

**One colour picker, and gradients everywhere a colour lives.** Every place you
choose a colour is now the same designed picker, a colour slot can hold a
gradient rather than only a flat colour, the picture follows the slider while you
drag instead of waiting for you to let go, the tool in your hand can be armed
with a saved colour by name so editing that colour later moves everything you
drew, and a gradient can be saved in the Library under a name.
`queue/audits/2026-09-04-one-color-picker.json`,
`2026-09-04-gradient-paint.json`, `2026-09-04-live-color-drag.json`,
`2026-09-04-tool-holds-saved-colour.json`, `2026-09-04-gradient-styles.json`.

**Stacks that behave like layout.** A stack can be given a size and its rows fill
it, a label re-measures itself when you change its width or its words so nothing
is cut off, text told to stretch down actually fills the space it is given, and
the direction the stack owns is no longer offered as a menu you can set and watch
do nothing. `queue/audits/2026-09-03-stack-size.json`,
`2026-09-03-label-grows.json`, `2026-09-04-text-fills-height.json`,
`2026-09-04-stack-owns-axis.json`.

**Sharp where it counts.** Zoom past 100 percent and the measurements, captions,
labels and shape strokes you placed are redrawn at the size you are looking at
them rather than enlarged, while the screenshot underneath stays honestly blocky
so you can still count pixels. Exporting at 2x now draws the whole document at 2x
instead of finishing at document size and blowing it up.
`queue/audits/2026-09-04-crisp-zoom.json`, `2026-09-04-crisp-shapes.json`,
`2026-09-04-sharp-2x-export.json`.

**Zoom callouts you can keep working on.** A callout now has a Zoom Callout
section in the panel for how much it magnifies and whether it is a box or a
circle, neither of which could be reached before; the tool remembers your shape
choice between callouts and across relaunches; and a new callout steps clear of
the ones already on the picture instead of landing on the last one.
`queue/audits/2026-09-04-callout-one-home.json`,
`2026-09-04-callout-shape.json`, `2026-09-04-callout-crowding.json`.

**A layers list that does not charge you for rows you cannot see.** A hundred
layer document used to start a hundred renders to fill a list showing five. It
now asks for about ten, and clicking a layer no longer stalls the window for
three frames. `queue/audits/2026-09-04-layers-lazy-rows.json`,
`2026-09-04-layer-thumbnails.json`, `2026-09-04-layers-row-count.json`.

**Drags that tell you the truth.** A capture dropped on the picture lands where
you let go of it and shows you where that is before you do; a file the app cannot
use says no while you are still dragging it rather than promising a copy and then
doing nothing; and a picture is now taken anywhere on the right hand panel
instead of only in the empty space below the last section.
`queue/audits/2026-09-04-drop-lands-here.json`,
`2026-09-04-refused-file.json`, `2026-09-04-panel-refuses-file.json`,
`2026-09-04-panel-takes-picture.json`.

  user on 2026-09-06 and built the same day: every part has a tick, switching one

  tool bar is a handle: pick a colour up off one and drop it on another, drop it

  paints and keeps its name. Saving a colour under a name also stopped being
  silently dead, which was one root cause behind eleven failing walks.

  radius, thickness, gap, room inside), room on four sides as one knob, its own
  outermost room, a named version straight off the shelf, and one change carried
  to every version at once.

  adjustable caption corner, a one character caption reads as a badge rather than
  a circle, a caption can hold more than one line, and clicking blank canvas next
  to a label no longer selects its arrow.
- **Auto layout.** Words wrap inside their container instead of hanging out,
  a piece stretched along the way a stack runs becomes the surface behind the
  rest, a drop lands in the slot the pointer is over with the neighbours opening
  a gap for it, gaps come out even, and a layer its container cut off can be
  brought back in one press.
- **The loop itself.** A full walk run went from about an hour to 40m 21s by
  waiting on the editor rather than on the clock; walks can now open and
  photograph a right click menu; and a press now requires the whole control to be
  showing, which caught a walk that had been reporting a pass while clicking on

## Design review

**Is the IA sound? One rule bent, and it is the important one.**
UX-PATTERNS section 3 says a panel group holds itself to its own bounded height
with its own scroller, and exactly one group takes the leftover room, "so a
60-layer stack scrolls inside Layers and never pushes Effects or Library off
screen". The app does this for one group out of six: the Layers list got its own
bounded scroller on 2026-09-07 (`LayersPanel.swift:1493-1526`). Every other
section lives in the single outer scroller at `LayersPanel.swift:99`, which is
why they push each other off the bottom. The rule did not bend by accident, it

p3-low.

## Ready to try

**The Appearance list on a shape.** Draw a rectangle and the settings panel now
offers Fill, Outline and Shadow as one list, each with a tick, a colour and its
own settings underneath. Take the outline off a box, which was never possible
before, and it stays off for the next box you draw. Drop a colour on a part that
is switched off and it comes on wearing that colour.
`queue/audits/2026-09-06-shape-parts.json`, then
`2026-09-06-parts-settings-inline.json`, `2026-09-06-parts-off-is-remembered.json`,
`2026-09-06-switch-says-mixed.json`, `2026-09-07-colour-onto-an-off-part.json`.

**The grid, on the tool bar.** A switch, a size button that opens a vertical
slider of real UI sizes, and a gear for adjusting where the grid starts. The size
you pick is the spacing you get at every zoom, and in adjust mode a click pins a
guide onto a grid line that then catches drags, resizes, arrows, lines and
caliper feet. `queue/audits/2026-09-06-grid-tool-bar.json`,
`2026-09-06-grid-chosen-size.json`, `2026-09-06-grid-guides.json`,
`2026-09-06-guides-catch-marks.json`.

**Colour you can pick up and carry.** Every swatch in the panel and on the tool
bar is now a drag handle. Move a colour from one row to another, carry one to the
Library to save it under a name, drag a saved colour back out onto a row and it
paints and keeps its name. Saving a colour under a name also works again.
`queue/audits/2026-09-06-colour-swatch-drag.json`,
`2026-09-06-toolbar-colour-swatch.json`, `2026-09-06-shelf-color-drop.json`,
`2026-09-06-colour-off-the-shelf.json`, `2026-09-07-save-colour-as-a-style.json`.

**Components that hand you a number.** A component can expose one of its numbers
to every copy: a corner radius, a thickness, a gap, the room inside, or all four
sides of that room as one knob. A copy sets it in a normal number field. You can
also place a particular version straight off the shelf and carry one change to
every version at once. `queue/audits/2026-09-06-a-knob-can-be-a-number.json`,
`2026-09-06-uneven-room-knob.json`, `2026-09-06-component-own-room.json`,
`2026-09-06-place-a-version.json`, `2026-09-06-apply-to-versions.json`.

**Measuring a layer you drew.** A caliper foot now catches the exact edge of any
layer on the canvas, not just edges found in the picture underneath, so a box you
set to 128 reads 128 wherever your hand lands.
`queue/audits/2026-09-07-caliper-layer-edges.json`.

**Arrows that look like annotations.** Five endings to choose from, a caption
corner from square to full pill, a one character caption that draws as a badge
instead of a circle, and captions that hold more than one line.
`queue/audits/2026-09-06-arrow-endings.json`,
`2026-09-06-caption-badge.json`, `2026-09-06-arrow-caption-lines.json`.

**Picking things on a screen.** Dragging across a screen's empty background now
sweeps a selection band over what is on it instead of picking the screen up, and
while you sweep inside a group a blue line lights round the group you are picking
in. `queue/audits/2026-09-07-band-inside-a-screen.json`,
`2026-09-07-band-level.json`.

**The panel toggle in the title bar.** The show and hide button now sits at the
trailing end of the window's own title bar, level with the traffic lights, in the
same place whether the panel is open or shut.
`queue/audits/2026-09-07-panel-toggle-titlebar.json`.

One caution that applies to all of the above: the panel is currently taller than
a laptop screen allows, so Corner Radius and the rest of Effects need a scroll.
That is now the first task in the queue.

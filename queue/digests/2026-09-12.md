## Reflections

**The loop has no way to reach a person who is not looking at it.** This is the
one thing worth fixing from today. Two separate symptoms, one cause:

- Sixty two hours of stall that only a person could clear, announced in a
  terminal banner, a window title and a dashboard hero, and none of those reached
  anybody.
- Six decision cards waiting for an answer. Five of them have been sitting since
  2026-09-08 and 2026-09-09, right through the stall, and they freeze five of the
  thirty six open p2 tasks.

Queued as **a-loop-stalled-on-the-spend-limit-reaches-the-pe** (p2), scoped to
the stall only: one notification per stall, not one per retry, another if the
stall outlives a day, proved by the existing failure drill rather than by hand.
A question that has waited too long is the obvious next thing to carry, and the
task says so, but it is deliberately not in this one.

**The audits are the best task source in the system, and it is not close.**
Seventeen tasks were filed in the last day and nearly all of them came out of an
audit's own rough notes, written by the runner who had just shipped the thing and
knew exactly what it still got wrong. "The half of this that is NOT fixed", "that
one is verified by reading, not by a walk", "the trade was deliberate and here is
what it cost". Nothing in this process produces honesty like asking somebody to
write down what is still rough about work they just finished. Worth protecting if
the audit format is ever trimmed.

**A closed task is not always a built task, and that is fine.** One of today's
eleven shipped no code: the walk passed four times of four and the runner wrote
that down and closed it. That is the right outcome and the second time this week
triage has confirmed a bug that stopped reproducing. The evidence rule in the
runner prompt is earning its place.

**Worth watching, not yet worth a task.** Blending is a chooser that previews
each option on the canvas as you move down it, so it is a panel of its own rather
than a pop-up menu. The Lens adjustment in the same section is a plain menu.
Two choosers, one section, two controls, because one of them needs to preview and
the other does not. One instance is not a pattern; a second one is, and then the
previewing chooser should be a named control rather than something each feature
builds.

# Daily digest 2026-09-13

## Summary

**A screenshot is no longer a picture.** In six slices over about eleven hours
the loop shipped Separate into Layers: right click a capture and every run of
text and every box in it becomes its own layer, arranged the way the screen was,
with the space each piece came from filled in behind it. Then it went one
further and made the pictures of text into text: Turn into Text reads a label off
a screenshot in the face, size and colour it was set in and puts the words back
exactly where the old ones were, so you can retype them.

That is the biggest single thing this app has learned to do. For the person who
uses Photonz to redline other people's UI, it turns "here is a picture I can draw
on" into "here is a screen I can take apart", and every one of the six slices has
an audit with real captures and measured numbers behind it.

The day's other half was **Tutorials**: a framework where a guide is data
pointing at a real control, a Help menu organised in tracks with a window that
counts your progress, an offer on first launch, and then six of the seven tracks
landing one after another. Twenty six tasks finished since the last digest.

## Reflections

**The app crossed a line today and the queue has not noticed yet.** Every task in
the focus epic before this week was about making something easier to draw or
measure. Separate into Layers is the first thing in Photonz that reads a picture
and tells you what is in it, and the next obvious step is the one the audits keep
circling: it can find the words but it cannot say them, so a hundred and forty two
layers arrive called Text 1 to Text 142. Turn into Text proves the app CAN read
the characters when it wants to. Filed
`find-out-whether-separating-should-hand-you-text` to settle whether separating
should just hand you text where it is confident, and
`a-text-layer-s-row-says-the-words-that-are-in-it` to stop the rows being numbers
once it does.

**Six tracks of tutorials landed in a day, and nobody has walked one as a
person.** Each track has an audit, each guide is proved by a walk, the times are
measured rather than claimed. What is not proved is the thing the tracks exist
for: doing four guides in a row and coming out able to use the app. Three of the
follow-ups filed this cycle are about exactly that seam rather than about any one
guide (`running-a-whole-tutorials-track-should-not-leave` on the five identical
windows, `the-tour-leaves-you-sitting-in-its-sample-pictur` on where you end up,
`a-tutorial-card-about-the-picture-stops-covering` on the card in the way). Worth
one task at the end of the set that does a whole track start to finish and reports
as a person, not as a builder. Not filed yet: the Video track and the anchor check
are still ahead of it, and filing it now would only park it.

**The loop is filing good follow-ups faster than it clears them.** Seventeen tasks
were created in this window and twenty six closed, which sounds healthy until you
notice that most of what closed was planned work from the two feature sets and
most of what was created was a finding. The p2 backlog is holding around fifty and
almost all of it is real. That is not a problem to fix today, but it is the reason
triage now drops `later`-epic work rather than parking it.

**Process, one thing worth changing.** Two of the seven questions on the dashboard
have been there eleven days. A question that old has usually been answered by the
code moving on underneath it, and there is already a p3 task
(`a-decision-card-that-no-longer-needs-an-answer-c`) for taking a card down without
pretending somebody answered it. Triage should also be the thing that re-reads old
cards and says whether they still mean anything. Adding that to the triage pass is
a change to `queue/bin/` and this prompt, not a queue task, so it is noted here
rather than filed.

## Design review

**Is the IA sound?** One rule bent, and the audit that bent it said so itself.
`2026-09-13-tutorials-building-ui-track` rough note one: a guide step that talks
about the whole picture draws its card across the picture, because the canvas has
no side with room for a card. It was worked around by authoring the SAMPLES around
the callout, starting every screen 120 points down and sweeping from the left in
the lining-up guide, and the audit calls that backwards, correctly. UX-PATTERNS
D14 rule 2 says prefer the empty side; it does not say what happens when there is
no empty side, and D16's own test makes a guide card an annotation rather than a
guide, which D14 then governs. The behaviour is filed as
`a-tutorial-card-about-the-picture-stops-covering`.

Nothing else bent. The Tutorials window took the Experiments window's shape rather
than inventing a surface (`2026-09-12-tutorials-ia`), the first launch offer went
into the setup window that already owns first launch
(`2026-09-12-first-run-offer`), and Separate into Layers added no chrome at all: it
lives on the layers row menu and in the Layer menu, which is where every other
command about a layer lives.

One observation that is not a finding, recorded so the next person does not file
it as one. Separate into Layers is reached by right clicking a row in a list or by
opening a menu, never by right clicking the picture it acts on. That reads a step
removed from the work, but the canvas has no context menu anywhere in the app
(`contextMenu` appears in exactly three files: `LayersRow`, `EffectsListInspector`,
`MeasurementsListView`), so this is a foundation question for the
`interaction-foundations` epic, not a gap in the focus feature. It waits until that
epic is staged.

**Are we missing components or guidance?** Two things got hand-rolled that the
system should own, and both are already filed. The guide's highlight ring is
always a rounded rectangle with one corner radius, so it does not fit the pill
shaped tool bar it is drawn round (`the-ring-a-guide-draws-matches-the-shape-it-is-d`),
and it lands half off the window when the thing it is ringing is at the bottom of a
tall panel (`a-guide-pointing-at-the-library-rings-a-shelf-wi`). Those are one
component's problem, and the second one is D14 wearing a different hat: a ring that
runs off the window is a callout that has covered its subject with the window edge.
No extraction task queued, because both already point at the same single ring
component rather than at two hand-rolled copies.

**What UX issues keep recurring?** One pattern, and it is loud. **A pill that
fades is carrying things a person has to act on.** Four audits on the same day each
stop to ask whether the pill is enough: `2026-09-13-separate-into-layers` ("the only
thing that tells you is a pill that fades"), `2026-09-13-separate-whole-screenshot`
("the pill ends with run it again for more rather than offering a button"),
`2026-09-13-separate-boxes`, and `2026-09-13-turn-into-text` ("is the pill enough to
tell you it worked"). On the dashboard capture the number it is carrying is 580
pieces still in the picture, and a few seconds later nothing on screen says so.
Queued as `what-separate-left-behind-is-still-there-after-t`.

The second, quieter pattern is naming. `2026-09-13-separate-into-layers` calls it
"the weakest part"; `2026-09-13-turn-into-text` notes that retyping a label does not
rename its row; `2026-09-13-separate-hierarchy` notes a separated box's body is
called Fill or Picture, "the app naming a thing it has no word for". Three audits,
one cause: a layer's name does not follow its content. Queued as
`a-text-layer-s-row-says-the-words-that-are-in-it`, with
`find-out-how-a-hundred-and-forty-new-rows-should` for the separate question of how
a hundred and forty of them should arrive in a list that shows five at a time.

**Do the documented rules need a scrub?** Yes, one document, two rules. Queued as
`two-rules-in-ux-patterns-get-scrubbed-a-callout` (p3, design-system epic):

- **D14 and D16.** D14 must say what a callout does when its subject is the whole
  picture and there is no empty side, and the answer has to be something the
  callout does rather than something the content is authored around. D14 and D16
  must also say which of the two a tutorial guide card is, since by D16's own test
  it explains the picture and is therefore an annotation.
- **Section 3, Modal and toast.** It files a pill of this kind under transient
  confirmations, giving "Saved" and "42 instances updated" as the examples.
  Separate into Layers uses the same pill to say 580 pieces are still in the
  picture and that running the command again takes the next batch. That is not a
  confirmation, and the rule should say when a result may live only in something
  that fades.

**Judging the focus feature as a user.** The six Separate audits are the best set
this loop has written: real captures, measured numbers on five genuinely different
screenshots, and each one naming its own worst part before being asked. Somebody
could try the whole thing from them alone. Three things are clumsy rather than
unfinished, and all three are now queued. Taking a screenshot apart is two commands
where a person would expect one, and the app teaches the ceremony in its own
refusal copy (`TextReading.swift:318` returns "More than one run of text here.
Separate into Layers first, ") — filed as
`find-out-whether-separating-should-hand-you-text`. The count of what was left
behind fades. And a hundred and forty two rows called Text N is a long scroll in a
panel that shows five. The honest good news is the one the audits keep proving:
the repair is invisible. Worst channel difference 0 out of 255 on every one of nine
runs, and the magnified before and after of the patched button is one flat colour.

## Ready to try

opening a real screenshot for. Right click a picture's row in the layers list, or
Layer then Separate into Layers, and the words and boxes come out arranged the way
the screen was, with the picture repaired behind them. It is one undo press however
many layers come out. Start with
[the first slice](../audits/2026-09-13-separate-into-layers.json), then

answered are whether 150 pieces is the right ceiling, and whether stopping at text
and boxes is the right first bite or a half feature.

**Turn into Text** is the one to try on a screenshot of your own. Separate a
capture, pick a run of text, and Layer then Turn into Text makes it the actual
words in the actual face, sitting exactly where they were, so you can retype a
label instead of covering it up.
[Audit](../audits/2026-09-13-turn-into-text.json). The thing to look at: put a
retyped label next to the untouched one beside it and see whether you can tell
which one was set again. It is worth trying on something that is not a Mac app, a
web page in a serif or a terminal, because the candidate list is five families and
anything else is refused.

## Design review

**Is the IA sound? One rule bent, in the tool slot.** Trim moved into Crop's slot
and that slot's flyout now stacks three different kinds of row in one flat list:
the Crop and Trim family at the head, then Crop's four aspect locks, then Resize
Image (`2026-09-20-trim-is-a-tool.json`, rough item 4). UX-PATTERNS has a
vocabulary for a tool family in a slot and one for a tool's options, and none for
a list that is both plus a command. Nothing else bent: the timeline is a dock,
the trim capsule reuses Crop's glass capsule shape, the transport is D8's row,
and the Sound section is an ordinary panel section. Folded into the trim task's
log rather than filed, since that task is already open on the same surface and is
where the answer has to be written down.

**Missing components or guidance: nothing was hand-rolled this cycle.** The level
line with draggable points is new, but it is one control replacing four the mock
asked for (mute, solo, fade in, fade out, curve picker), which is a reduction
rather than an invention, and the audit asks you directly whether it is one
control too few. No extraction candidates. The one thing the design system does
not own and should is the timeline zoom, which `video-surface.md:111` says lives
in `.tlbar` and which no surface draws except `video.html`.

**Recurring UX issue: the app goes quiet when it will not do something.** Five
separate instances in one cycle, in four unrelated areas:

- a sound or a video file dropped on the window: `EditorState.swift:1077` is a
  bare `return` when ImageIO cannot read the file, so nothing lands and nothing
  is said (found by reading the code during this pass, filed)
- a colour dropped on a switched-off effect: the app says "Paints Border with
  this colour", takes the drop, and the border stays off
  (`a-colour-dropped-on-a-switched-off-effect-paints`)
- Backspace after turning a shape into a picture and undoing twice: the key stops
  answering, silently (`a-shape-that-was-turned-into-a-picture-and-undon`)
- a marquee across two picked pictures, and Command G on one layer: both refuse
  with no words (`2026-09-20-marquee-and-refusals.json`, left in that task's log)
- a recording the app cannot read: an empty window and no explanation
  (`2026-09-20-a-recording-is-a-document.json`)

The rule is not missing and it is not unclear. UX-PATTERNS:670 says "A refusal
speaks in the same place a yes would have, and it says WHY, and it names the one
thing to do", and :676 names a surface that refuses by going quiet as the thing
it exists to stop. This is conformance, not guidance, so no rule scrub was
queued: two of the five already have tasks, one is newly filed, and the other two
are recorded in the task logs where a later pass will find them.

**Rule scrubs queued: none, and one already open.** No document has been overtaken
by reality this cycle. `the-fourteen-video-pages-still-contradict-the-vi` (p2) is
already open against the video clickthroughs, which still show the blade as a
tool and a gap you have to close by hand, neither of which is what shipped.

**Judged as a user, the sound feature holds up and the audit is followable.** The
setup names both flags, the eight steps are concrete, and the questions asked are
the right ones. Two things in it are clumsy rather than wrong. Add Sound in the
Video menu is the only way to bring a file in, and dropping one on the window
does nothing at all, which is the first thing anybody will try and contradicts
the app's own thesis that everything is a layer (filed). And the Sound section
sits at the bottom of the panel under Motion, so the level fader, the most
reached control a sound layer has, is the lowest thing in the dock. That is the
same shape as the open Appearance task: whatever ships last lands lowest,
whatever it is used for. Folded into that task, with the note that the fix has to
be a rule about what a layer is being used for rather than a hand-ordered list,
or the next feature repeats it.

## Ready to try

Sound, and the audio lands on a layer of its own directly above the picture: cut
the picture with B and the voiceover over it is untouched. Video then Add Sound
brings music in as a third layer. Click the white level line on a sound's bar to

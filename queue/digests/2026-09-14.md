# Daily digest 2026-09-14

## Summary

**Photonz can now draw an icon.** Twenty four hours ago the app had no way to
make a shape that was not a rectangle and nowhere small enough to draw an icon
in. It now has a Pen that places corners and pulls curves, paths you can reshape
point by point afterwards, any rectangle or ellipse convertible into one, a New
Frame list that offers 16 through 512 and takes the camera there, a frame that
draws the margin every glyph has to live inside, a row of live previews at the
five sizes the icon will really be used at, a starting line weight that suits
the canvas it is drawn on rather than always four points, and Export straight to
SVG. That is the whole user ask from 2026-09-13 ("a pen that draws polygons with
hard edges and bezier curves, for building elaborate SVG icons, and SVG export")
delivered end to end in a day, in Next, behind flags.

Twenty three tasks finished. The other half of the day went to the editor
itself and to the loop that builds it.

## Reflections

**The queue is finishing user asks in a day now, and that is the thing to
protect.** The pen was asked for yesterday morning and by this morning you can
draw with it, reshape it, put it on an icon grid and export it. Nothing else in
the loop's history has turned round that fast. What made it work was that the
manager staged the epic the same hour and filed the slices small enough that each
one shipped with its own audit.

**But the ask is not actually finished, and the queue did not say so.** Three
things stand between "the pen exists" and "somebody can hand over an icon": a
path always arrives redline red with no colour on the tool bar, a path cannot be
made thicker or thinner anywhere in the app, and an icon exported to SVG has a
base64 bitmap of the white background sitting inside it. All three were filed
honestly by the runners that found them, and all three had drifted to the back of
the queue behind unrelated polish. Triage moved them to positions 8, 9 and 10, so
the ask completes before the loop moves on. That is a process lesson worth
keeping: **a task that finishes a user ask is not a follow-up, it is the rest of
the ask**, and it should inherit the ask's place in the queue rather than being
filed at the bottom like any other rough edge.

**The follow-up bar landed yesterday and it is already working, but it only
guards the front door.** It stops new near-twins at filing time. It says nothing
about the 83 tasks that were already standing when it landed, which is why this
pass applied it backwards and took ten of them out. Worth doing again: the bar
should be part of triage, not only part of filing.

**An idea worth queueing and not queued.** Every audit ends with a `rough` list,
and those lists are where most of the queue comes from. Nothing reads them
together. Three of today's four design-review findings came from noticing the
same complaint in two different audits a day apart. A small tool that groups
rough items by the words they share, and says "this is the third audit to mention
the border width slider", would find those patterns in seconds instead of by hand
once a day. Left unqueued deliberately: the loop should not build itself tooling
while a user ask is unfinished, and it is written here so the next digest can
pick it up.

## Design review

**Is the IA sound? Two bends, both in the same place: the canvas.** UX-PATTERNS
D16 governs what the app is allowed to draw on the canvas, and the icon work
walked into two holes in it.

The first is a real bend. D16 rule 3 says two guides that can be on at once must
differ in KIND, not only in colour, "two things that read alike read as one broken
thing". The icon keyline margin ships as violet dashes, and a new frame arrives
selected, so the very first thing anybody sees is two dashed rectangles side by
side meaning entirely different things. The runner noticed and lengthened the dash
rhythm, which helps, but a dash rhythm is a difference of degree. The rule does
not cover this case because the other party is not a guide at all, it is the
selection outline, and D16 has nothing to say about that
(`queue/audits/2026-09-14-icon-keylines.json`, third rough item).

The second is a gap rather than a bend. The icon previews strip is neither a
guide (it helps you place nothing) nor an annotation (it explains nothing in the
picture), and it sits on top of the ruler, which D16 names as a guide. There is no
rule for a third kind of canvas chrome, and none for what gives way when two
pieces of chrome want the same corner
(`queue/audits/2026-09-14-icon-previews.json`, first rough item).

Both folded into `two-rules-in-ux-patterns-get-scrubbed-a-callout`, which already
owned a D14 and a D16 scrub; it now carries three and is retitled to say so.

**Are we missing components or guidance? One rule, asked for four times.** The
strongest recurring finding is a control offered over something it can barely
act on, or cannot act on at all:

- Corner Radius over a group whose contents are rounded 18: the knob sits hard at
  the far left and nothing says why, which looks exactly like the old broken row.
- Corner Radius on a pill: the slider switches itself off with nothing rounded
  left to give.
- Corner Radius on a measurement: a caliper has no corners the slider reaches, so
  pulling it does nothing at all.
- Border Width on a 24 pixel icon frame: the track still runs 0 to 20 where only
  the first few steps mean anything
  (`queue/audits/2026-09-14-icon-stroke-weight.json`, fourth rough item).

Four surfaces, four separate local answers. UX-PATTERNS §4 has a settled ladder
for a control that cannot act AT ALL and no row for one whose range is clamped by
something underneath it. The rule task existed, at p3, sitting *below* two p2
tasks whose own acceptance says the answer must come from that rule. Triage moved
it up to p2 position 5 and widened it to answer all four cases by name.

**What UX issues keep recurring? The right hand panel cannot decide what to show
you.** Three of the four walks failing in the 12:47Z sweep are one cluster, and
each fails on its own as well as under sweep load: `dock-picked-first-walk` (step
73, the Arrow section never comes into view), `layers-list-follows-pick-walk`
(step 91, the Rectangle row never comes into view), and
`effects-room-for-the-one-you-opened-walk` (step 32, the Border twist reads shut
when it should read open). The fourth, `panel-start-margin-walk`, passes on its
own, so it is a sweep flake.

Three mechanisms were built separately for one job: section order, the panel
scrolling to what you picked, and the room the Effects list keeps. You answered
the question that settles all three on 2026-09-13 ("what you picked sits at the
top") and nothing has been built on the answer since. Three tasks were chasing
three symptoms of it; they are now one task, first in the queue, carrying your
answer and all four walks.

Second recurring issue, smaller: **a layer row that says the wrong thing**. A
text layer keeps its old row name after its words change; a rectangle turned into
a path is still called Rectangle. One rule, two tasks, now merged.

**Rule scrubs queued.** `two-rules-in-ux-patterns-get-scrubbed-a-callout` (D16
guide-versus-selection ink, and a home for canvas chrome that is neither guide nor
annotation) and `the-control-rules-get-a-row-for-a-control-that-c` (UX-PATTERNS
§4, a control clamped by something underneath it), promoted out of p3 because the
tasks waiting on it are not.

**Judging the icon work as a user, from the audits alone.** You can follow them
end to end: File, New Frame, pick 24 under Icons, and you are looking at a square
the size of your hand with its margin drawn on it and a live row of previews in
the corner. Press P, draw, and the line arrives at a weight that survives the
size. Three things are clumsy and all three are filed. The Pen is the only drawing
tool in the app with no colour on the tool bar, so every piece of a multi-coloured
icon is a trip to the panel. A path cannot be made thicker or thinner anywhere,
so the weight it arrived at is the weight it keeps, which is a strange thing to
say about a line-drawing tool. And the export, the last step, writes a file with a
photograph of the white background inside it. None of those is a surprise the
audits hid; they are all in the rough lists. They are, together, the difference
between a feature that demos and a feature somebody hands over, which is why they
moved to the front rather than being counted as polish.

One ceremonial thing worth naming: nothing snaps to the keylines yet. The frame
draws where the icon should go and then lets you put it somewhere else. That is
the snapping half of the icon-grid epic and it is not started, which is the right
call for one day's work, but a guide that does not pull is a guide people stop
looking at.

## Ready to try

**Draw an icon, start to finish.** Make a document, then Layer, New Frame, and
pick a size from the new Icons strip: 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 or 512. The canvas takes
you there at a zoom you can draw at. The frame arrives with the margin every glyph
in a set has to stay inside drawn on it, plus its two centre lines (View, Show
Icon Keylines turns them off). A row of previews appears in the top left showing
that frame at 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64, redrawn as you draw, each one exactly what an
export at that size would write, so a line too thin to survive 16 pixels is simply
missing from the 16 pixel chip while you can still thicken it. Anything you draw
arrives at a line weight that suits the size rather than always four points.
Audits: `queue/audits/2026-09-13-icon-frames.json`,
`queue/audits/2026-09-14-icon-keylines.json`,
`queue/audits/2026-09-14-icon-previews.json`,
`queue/audits/2026-09-14-icon-stroke-weight.json`.

**The Pen, and paths you can put right afterwards.** Press P, click for corners,
press and drag for curves, click the first point to close it. The Pen stays in
your hand for the next shape. Pick a finished path and its points are on it,
ready to be dragged, curved, broken, joined, added and removed, one undo step
each. Any rectangle, rounded rectangle, ellipse or line can become a path from the
Layer menu or the layer row menu, keeping exactly the look it had. With a grid on,
points land on the lines you can see, and Command places one exactly where the
pointer is. Audits: `queue/audits/2026-09-13-pen-draws-a-path.json`,
`queue/audits/2026-09-13-path-reshape.json`,
`queue/audits/2026-09-13-turn-into-a-path.json`,
`queue/audits/2026-09-13-pen-on-the-grid.json`,
`queue/audits/2026-09-13-pen-stays-in-hand.json`.

**Export to SVG.** Export has a fourth answer. Paths, boxes with their rounding,
ovals, lines, groups, gradients and outlined text all leave as real shapes, sharp
at any size. Before you save, the sheet names any layer that has to ride along as
a picture and says why. Caveat worth knowing before you try it: an icon drawn on a
blank canvas currently carries the white background out as an embedded bitmap, so
the file is bigger than it should be. Audit:
`queue/audits/2026-09-13-svg-export.json`.

**Cut a piece onto its own layer, and heal behind it.** Draw a marquee round
something in a screenshot and press Shift Command J. The piece lifts onto its own
layer and the space it came from fills back in with the colours that were around
it, so dragging the piece away leaves what it came off looking whole. Where the
surround is one colour the fill is exact, byte for byte; where it ramps it
matches the ramp; where it can do neither it fills flat and the pill tells you the
fill was a guess. Note the key: you asked for Command Shift X, this shipped on
Shift Command J because that is Photoshop's own key for it and Command Shift X is
Photoshop's Liquify. One line to change if you would rather have the key you
named. Audit: `queue/audits/2026-09-14-cut-to-own-layer.json`.

**A quieter right hand panel.** Pick three layers and you get controls instead of
paragraphs: seven lines of small grey prose gone from above Position & Size, 152
points of panel back, and the last section's heading on screen without scrolling.
The one thing genuinely harder to find is the unit, which used to open the
Position & Size line and now lives only on each field's hover tip. Audit:
`queue/audits/2026-09-14-panel-says-less.json`.

**Also landed, smaller:** Group and Ungroup are in the layer row menu with their
shortcuts printed, and the layers list now scrolls itself to whatever you pick.
A filled circle looks filled on the canvas at every zoom, which it did not on
about half of them. Audits: `queue/audits/2026-09-13-row-menu-group.json`,
`queue/audits/2026-09-13-layers-follow-pick.json`,
`queue/audits/2026-09-14-filled-shape-on-canvas.json`.

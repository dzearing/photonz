## Reflections

**An interrupted runner left a landmine, and it would have stopped the loop.**
When the container limits task was cut off, it had written its tests and not its
implementation, which is exactly what the test-first rule asks for. Those tests
reference `GroupLayout.limitsWidth`, `limitsHeight` and `limitsSentence`, none of
which exist, so the test target could not compile and `Scripts/test.sh` would have
failed for every task after it, not just that one. The next runner would have
spent its context debugging somebody else's half-written file. Parked it at
`queue/attic/2026-09-05-GroupSizeLimitsTests.swift.txt`, took it out of `Tests`,
and pointed the task at it. The tree is green again: 3173 tests in 275 suites
pass.

That is worth a process change rather than a note. Test-first plus interruption
means a half-finished task is a broken build for everybody, and the loop has no
way to notice. Two cheap options: the guard could check for untracked files under
`Tests/` when it resets a task, or a runner could write its tests on a branch. The
first is one command and catches this exact case. Worth doing before it happens
again, and it will.

**The answered cards changed what the queue is worth doing next.** Three tasks
came back from blocked with acceptance checklists written for a question rather
than an answer, and the read-only number one shrank from a build to a default
flip because both looks were already sitting behind a switch. Grooming those
properly was most of this pass. That is the decision system working: the cards
carried enough for the user to pick in a few minutes, and the picks were specific
enough to rewrite the work against.

**Auto layout is close to being demonstrable.** Groups hug their contents, close
around them, take room on four sides, and gaps mean visible space. What is
missing before somebody can build a real nav bar is the two tasks now at p2 seq
40 and 50: pushing contents to the two ends, and one piece taking the room left
over. Those two plus the min and max limits already at p1 are the set. Worth
saying out loud that finishing those three is what makes the epic show something,
rather than adding more around the edges.

**One thing that did not get filed and should be said anyway.** `EditorState.swift`
is 6810 lines and grew about 90 KB in a single day. The split task exists and has
been moved up to p2 seq 30, but the honest reading is that the loop is producing
code faster than it is producing structure, and the only reason that has not hurt
yet is that every task lands its logic in `PhotonzCore` where the tests are.

Filed from this pass: `one-rule-for-a-control-that-speaks-for-several-p` (p2) and
`the-stretch-menu-says-what-stretching-both-ways` (p3).

## Design review

Evidence: the twelve audits written on 2026-09-04, the five decisions resolved
today, and section 4 of `docs/design/mocks/shared/UX-PATTERNS.md`.

**Is the IA sound?** One rule bent, and the user has now ruled on it. Every
setting that belongs to the tool in your hand rather than to anything you have
picked lives in the right hand dock, so hiding the dock, which section 1 treats as
a normal thing to do, takes them all away: the Zoom Callout's Shape, the wand's
Tolerance, Measure's Snap and Show. Two audits named it independently
(`2026-09-04-callout-shape` rough 1, `2026-09-04-callout-one-home`) and both filed
it as following the house rule rather than fixing it, which is the tell that the
rule is wrong. The dock vocabulary has no home for a setting that belongs to a
tool. The answer is a new surface, a capsule above the tool bar, and it is now
task `the-settings-for-the-tool-in-your-hand-are-reach` at p2 seq 90 with the
answer written into its acceptance. Note for whoever builds it: D15 in
UX-PATTERNS says a tool's modes live in the tool, not in a row beside it, and this
capsule is a row beside it. That rule and this decision have to be reconciled in
the same task, not left to disagree.

**Are we missing components or guidance?** Yes, one, and it is behaviour rather
than a component. The Mixed look shipped and is genuinely good: one word, one
weight, in the value's own place, and a control saying Mixed still reaches every
picked layer in one undo step. What is not written down is what a control does
when it cannot speak for the selection at all, and every recent control has
answered that on its own. The Component section vanishes when you pick a second
copy (`2026-09-04-copy-own-color` rough 1). The shadow switch shows plain off for
a part-shadowed selection and explains itself in grey text underneath
(`2026-09-04-mixed-one-look` rough 4). The W field goes on showing 200 after two
labels refused it and landed on 204 (`2026-09-04-mixed-one-look` rough 5). Three
open tasks, one at p2 and two at p3, each currently has to settle the same
question inside a feature. Queued as
`one-rule-for-a-control-that-speaks-for-several-p` at p2 seq 60, deliberately
ahead of all three, and scoped as a sibling section rather than a rewrite of a
rule that is working.

One extraction candidate seen and not queued: the video crop overlay carries its
own copy of the selection handle chrome and still draws all eight handles on any
size of box, so yesterday's small-selection fix reached one of the two copies
(`2026-09-04-handles-fit-small-selections` rough 4). Leaving it is the right call
while the video epic is staged later, but it is the second surface with its own
handles and worth remembering when that epic comes up.

**What UX issues keep recurring?** Multi-selection, as above, in four audits.
Nothing else rises to a pattern: the four-point text slack that ran through three
audits earlier in the day was closed by `2026-09-04-text-box-words`, and the rest
of the rough notes are one-offs with honest reasons attached.

**Do the documented rules need a scrub?** Two, both queued rather than described.
The "What Mixed looks like" section names the read-only number as a known
exception waiting on a pending decision, and that decision was answered this
morning, so the section is now stale in the app's favour: added as an acceptance
item on `a-number-you-cannot-type-into-stops-looking-like` so the doc and the code
move together. D15 versus the tool settings capsule is the second, noted above and
carried on that task rather than filed separately, because splitting a rule change
from the change that forces it is how rules end up contradicting the app.

**Judging the focus feature as a user.** Component design is the closest thing to
finished, and the two audits from yesterday are genuinely tryable: setup, numbered
steps, and screenshots that match. Reading them as somebody who wanted to build a
button, two things are clumsy. You cannot set more than one copy at once, and it
is not that the panel is awkward, it is that the whole Component section

That is filed at p2 seq 70 and is the single thing most in the way of the feature
feeling real. And naming a colour from a copy is two moves, because the knob row

swatch to pick it (`2026-09-04-copy-own-color` rough 3, left out on purpose to
keep a 264pt dock from crowding). The second is ceremony worth living with for

## Summary

**The canvas got graph paper, and it went all the way through.** Nine tasks in a
row turned "there is no grid" into a grid you can actually build on: switch it on
with Command apostrophe, watch it thin and thicken as you zoom, place its zero
point anywhere and cap how fine it may get, snap to the lines you can actually

a chip beside the zoom, step by whole cells with the arrow keys, and draw a shape
that lands on the grid on both ends. Two of those were bug reports about the
grid flickering and disappearing, both traced to real causes and fixed with
measurements rather than guesses (the grid vanishing at zoom was not the level of
detail maths at all, it was a redraw that reset the grid to nothing on every

[grid-origin](../audits/2026-09-05-grid-origin.json),

canvas now says which one you are looking at. A Default and a Disabled button

## Reflections

**The queue is running out of focus work faster than it is being refilled, and
that is a good problem.** Eleven of the twenty seven open tasks are follow-ups
filed by the audits themselves, which means the loop is now mostly sanding what
it built yesterday. Component design end to end still has real holes (see the
design review) and those are queued, but after them the focus epic is close to
done. The next manager pass should be looking at what "create once, reuse
everywhere, override safely" still lacks rather than waiting for audits to
suggest it.

**A four hour test run is a test run nobody uses.** 188 walks at roughly 75
seconds each, and most of that is launching and quitting the app once per walk,
not the walk. Already queued as
`a-full-walk-run-finishes-in-an-hour-not-four`, and it deserves to be treated as
a feature rather than a chore: the loop already had one walk fail silently for
two days against a stale bundle because nobody ran the full set.

**A bug should have to prove itself before it goes to the top of the queue.**
The single most expensive thing in this pass was a task filed at the end of last
night's run claiming the loop had stopped rebuilding the app you playtest with.
It was not true, and it was filed at the highest priority, so it would have taken
the next task slot. Queued as
`a-bug-filed-at-the-end-of-a-task-has-been-reprod`.

**A feature idea, from watching what the audits keep saying:** the app is good at
telling you what went wrong and bad at offering the way out. A clipped layer gets
a mark and a sentence saying to move it or turn clipping off. A saved color that
cannot come along gets a pill naming the problem and pointing at nothing. In both
cases the remedy is named in words and is several steps away. A small pattern
where the notice carries its own fix would pay for itself across half a dozen
places. Started with `a-layer-its-container-cut-off-can-be-brought-bac`.

## Design review

Evidence base: the 43 audits written in the last 24 hours, the five decisions
resolved, `docs/design/mocks/shared/UX-PATTERNS.md`, and
`queue/bin/runner-prompt.md`.

**Is the IA sound? Mostly, with one rule that bent twice.** The one new shell
surface this cycle, the tool settings capsule above the tool bar, did not sneak
in: it went to the user as a decision card, and the answer is written into
UX-PATTERNS as D15 along with the cost that was knowingly accepted, that it
covers a band across the bottom of the picture where the buttons being redlined
often are. That is the vocabulary growing on purpose, which is the right way.

The bend is elsewhere. Two features in two days were asked for a guide drawn
*behind* the picture, and both shipped it drawn *in front*, each working the
reasoning out from scratch:
[canvas-grid](../audits/2026-09-05-canvas-grid.json) rough 1 ("the grid is drawn
OVER your layers, a deliberate change from the plain reading of the ask") and
[screen-columns](../audits/2026-09-05-screen-columns.json) rough 1 ("the columns
draw OVER the screen and its contents, the same call the canvas grid made a day
earlier"). Both were right. Neither wrote it down, and a grep of UX-PATTERNS and
`docs/design/overview.md` finds no rule about it, so a third person will argue it
a third time. Queued: `the-rules-say-whether-a-guide-drawn-on-the-canva`.

A second, smaller one: the Columns section needs the screen itself selected while
the Show Columns menu row acts on whatever screen your selection lives in, so the
same feature is half there depending on what you clicked last
([screen-columns](../audits/2026-09-05-screen-columns.json) rough 3). That is the
selection model disagreeing with itself and it was already queued.

**Are we missing components or guidance? Two extraction candidates, both real.**

1. *One pill, two floors.* Measurement chips and arrow captions draw from the
   same `PillRasterizer`, but each keeps its own minimum width: measurements now
   floor at 1.7 wide for every 1 tall, arrow captions still at 1.2, so a
   measurement reading "4" is a badge while an arrow captioned "1" beside it is
   a circle ([measure-chip-badge](../audits/2026-09-06-measure-chip-badge.json)
   rough 3, [caption-pill-shape](../audits/2026-09-05-caption-pill-shape.json)
   rough 3, [pill-text-centring](../audits/2026-09-06-pill-text-centring.json)
   rough 4). The queued fix moves the caption to match; the shape of the problem
   says the floor belongs to the pill, not to each caller.
2. *Who decides this number.* The treatment for a number the app works out for
   you (plain text, no box, a line saying who owns it) exists, is built as
   `GeometryReadout`, and is written up in UX-PATTERNS section 4. It has been
   applied to exactly one case. Three separate features this cycle shipped a
   typeable box for a number the flow overwrites a moment later
   ([fill-the-flow](../audits/2026-09-05-fill-the-flow.json) rough 2,
   [nav-bar-row](../audits/2026-09-05-nav-bar-row.json) rough 3,
   [words-that-wrap](../audits/2026-09-05-words-that-wrap.json) rough 3).

Worth naming on the other side: the capture history had grown a second tooltip
implementation of its own, and the fix deleted it rather than repairing it, so
there is one tooltip in the app again
([history-tooltips](../audits/2026-09-05-history-tooltips.json) rough 5). That is
the right instinct and it should be the default one.

**What UX issues keep recurring? Three patterns, not one-offs.**

1. *A number the app decides still looks like one you can type.* Three audits
   above. The rule exists; its scope does not.
2. *Nothing on screen says which level or mode you are in.* A selection band
   swept inside a group looks identical to one swept at the top level
   ([sweep-in-group](../audits/2026-09-05-sweep-in-group.json) rough 2), and a
   band inside a component copy silently falls back to the top level (rough 5). A
   grid set to Columns pulls sideways only, so the two arrow keys behave
   differently with no hint ([grid-nudge](../audits/2026-09-06-grid-nudge.json)
   rough 4). The out of view mark lives in the Layers list, so if that section is
   collapsed the canvas says nothing about a layer that vanished
   ([out-of-view](../audits/2026-09-06-out-of-view.json) rough 4). Queued as
   `the-rubber-band-shows-which-level-it-is-picking` for the first, and the
   pattern is worth a rule of its own.
3. *The app names the remedy instead of offering it.* No one click way back for a
   clipped layer ([out-of-view](../audits/2026-09-06-out-of-view.json) rough 3),
   no fix on the pill when a saved color cannot come along
   ([saved-color-not-for-that-part](../audits/2026-09-06-saved-color-not-for-that-part.json)
   rough 1). Both notices are well written and both leave you to go and do it.

**Rule scrubs queued: two.**
`the-rules-say-whether-a-guide-drawn-on-the-canva` adds the missing over versus
under rule to UX-PATTERNS beside section 3, citing the two features it comes
from. `a-bug-filed-at-the-end-of-a-task-has-been-reprod` scrubs
`queue/bin/runner-prompt.md`, which today says a great deal about proving the
work you did and nothing at all about proving a bug you file: a grep for
"reproduce" in it comes back empty, and that gap cost a top priority task slot
last night.

**Judging the focus feature as a user.** Component design end to end, read from
the audits alone: you can draw something, make it a component, drop copies,
expose four kinds of knob, give a copy its own color and its own size, add a
second version and see on the canvas which version each copy is showing. That is
a real feature and most of it reads well. Three things a first timer hits:

- **You cannot add to a bar or a card from the shelf.** Open a Nav Bar you are
  building, drag a Badge out of the Library, drop it inside, and it lands *beside*
  the bar at the top level ([nav-bar-row](../audits/2026-09-05-nav-bar-row.json)
  rough 1). The model side already works; it is the drop target that does not.
  This is the middle of the build loop, so it moved to p1.
- **The biggest fact about a copy is four sections down.** Pick a copy and its
  Component section, the one holding the Version row and the knobs, sits below
  Position and Size, Layout and the rest, off the bottom of the dock until you
  scroll.
- **The shelf always hands you the first version.** Making a disabled button is
  drop, then change Version, every time.

All three were already queued and all three now sort ahead of everything else.

## Ready to try

**A grid you can build on.** Open Photonz Next, press Command apostrophe. Set
the spacing and how bold every nth line is under Grid in the Canvas section, or
from the chip beside the zoom, which reads what the lines on screen are actually
worth at this zoom. Place the grid's zero point anywhere on the canvas and give
it a smallest cell. Draw a box and both corners land on the lines; nudge it with
the arrow keys and it steps by whole cells, rejoining the grid on the first press
if it was off it. Turn it off with the same key and the canvas is byte for byte
what it was.
[canvas-grid](../audits/2026-09-05-canvas-grid.json),
[grid-origin](../audits/2026-09-05-grid-origin.json),
[draw-on-the-grid](../audits/2026-09-06-draw-on-the-grid.json),
[grid-nudge](../audits/2026-09-06-grid-nudge.json).

**Two versions of one component.** Make a button a component, add a version, name
it Disabled, and give it the grey look. Drop copies and switch one of them to
Disabled from the Version row. The canvas prints the version after an original's
name once there is more than one, and a copy showing anything other than the
first version says so above itself. Pick several copies at once and the knobs
speak for all of them, saying Mixed where they disagree.
[component-versions](../audits/2026-09-05-component-versions.json),
[canvas-version-labels](../audits/2026-09-06-canvas-version-labels.json),

**A bar that arranges itself.** The starter Nav Bar is a Row now. Give a group a
size and clip what does not fit; tell one piece to take the room left over;
spread the contents to the two ends; set a smallest and largest size and watch a
stack lay out in the room it has. A layer the box cut off wears an orange
scissors on its Layers row and the tip names the box it went behind. Two catches
worth knowing before you try it: you cannot drag a new control off the shelf into
an open group yet, and a stretched piece still shows a width you can type into
that goes straight back.
[fill-the-flow](../audits/2026-09-05-fill-the-flow.json),

**Columns on a screen.** Select a screen, switch Columns on, set a count, gutter
and margin. Drag a layer near a column edge and it catches, freed by holding
Command. Nothing shows in an export or a copy.
[screen-columns](../audits/2026-09-05-screen-columns.json).

**Tool settings with the panel closed.** Pick the Zoom Callout with the right

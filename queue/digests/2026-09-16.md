# Daily digest 2026-09-16

## Summary

The loop had its biggest day so far and nobody has seen any of it. Seventy three
tasks finished in the last twenty six hours, across a hundred and forty nine
commits, and for forty one hours of that the Mac's screen has been locked. While
it is locked no scripted walk will run, so the full sweep has not run once, and
twenty four of the seventy two audits written in that window open their rough
list by saying there is no photograph of the app in them. The rest mostly ship
drawings made outside the window instead. Everything below shipped on unit tests
and reasoning. That is a real claim, it is just a weaker one than this much work
deserves.

What finished, by area:

- **Animating an icon became a whole feature.** A layer can be told to change a
  property over time, it says what point it turns around and you drag that point
  on the picture, a strip across the bottom shows every moving part on one cycle,
  the loop previews at the sizes an icon will really be used at, and the result
  exports as an animated SVG that a browser plays. The exported motion was
  checked against the app's own numbers at ten moments of one lap and the two
  agree to within a fifth of a degree.
- **Video editing started.** You can cut a recording into pieces and drop the one
  you do not want. This is the first video work in the app.
- **Vector work for icons filled in.** Two shapes become one, joined or cut out or
  kept where they overlap; several shapes turn into one path welded at their
  ends; a rectangle or an ellipse becomes a path you can reshape; a drawn line
  says how it ends; the Pen keeps the thickness you last chose.
- **The right hand panel got shorter three ways.** Position and size moved out of
  the panel, a number that has four sides became one row with a popout instead of
  a grid of boxes, and a Sections row at the foot lets you say which sections show
  at all. One number box now works everywhere a number is typed into a panel.
- **Components grew variants.** A component can hold a resting, hovered, pressed
  and disabled look; a shelf tile says where a component came from; a shared
  component arrives the right size in this document; the layers list says which
  row is the original and how many picked layers are inside copies.
- **Redlining picked up a lens.** A zoom callout is now the Lens set to Magnify,
  and a lens turned on its side still shows the picture straight. A measurement
  end follows your hand instead of jumping to it. Export gained WebP and a
  quality control that tells you what the file will weigh.

Three tasks became blocked and all three are waiting on the user: whether
dragging a component onto the canvas gives you the original or a copy, whether a
window should have modes you swap between, and whether the loop should keep the
Mac from locking overnight. One decision was answered yesterday, that a document
should not be locked into a kind of project.

Three tasks were dropped as no longer worth doing, two of them walks that read
the panel for controls that have since moved out of it.

## Reflections

**The locked screen is now the most expensive thing in the project, and one
click ends it.** There is a decision card waiting since yesterday asking whether
the loop may keep the Mac awake. Until it is answered, or until somebody unlocks
the screen, every feature above ships unphotographed and the sweep cannot tell a
break from a flake. Forty one hours of that has cost more than any bug in the
queue.

**The lock story runners have been told is wrong, and it has cost four of them
time.** This pass ran the check itself. The Mac really is locked right now, with
the login window covering the whole screen. Forced past the refusal, a walk ran
five steps in under four seconds: it dragged across the canvas and moved seven
grid lines, and the app was measurably busy animating while it did. So "layout
and animations stop" is not what happens. Two specific things fail instead. The
app cannot be photographed, and the names a walk looks controls up by never
arrive even though the controls are on screen at the right size with the right
tooltip. That is the exact contradiction the original bug was filed on: the step
reported no menu called Add Effect and then listed that very control, with an
empty name. The refusal is still right, because a walk cannot be trusted in that
state. The reason given for it is not, and four runners have each spent part of
a turn re-arguing it. Queued as
`the-locked-screen-message-says-the-real-reason-a`.

**The follow-up bar is working and the queue is shrinking against a rising tide.**
Seventy three tasks out, twenty four in, over the same day. Fifty five open now.
The bar's healthy number of twelve was set when the loop was slower; at seventy
three finished a day, fifty five open is well under a day of work and the
constraint is no longer queue size. It is whether the work can be verified, which
loops back to the screen.

**While the screen is locked, sequence by what can be proved without it.** This
pass put the work provable from a test bench at the front of p2 and the work that
needs a real window at the back. That is a scheduling idea rather than a feature,
and it should be undone once the screen is unlocked.

## Design review

**Is the IA sound?** One rule bent, and it bent far enough to walk back something
the user asked for by name. The right hand panel is still over-subscribed by a
factor rather than by a few points: with a piece of text picked in a three layer
document it asks for 1052 points and the tallest window this display gives it is
968. The Sections row that shipped yesterday decides which sections may ask; it
did not make anything shorter. Appearance paid for it, and its last 32 points are
now below the fold where they used to be on screen, against the user's 2026-09-07
request that Appearance and Effects are the two sections nobody should ever have
to scroll to. The panel's height has now been attacked seven separate times, all
seven done, and it still asks for more than it can have. Queued as
`appearance-is-below-the-fold-again-because-the-p`. Evidence:
`queue/audits/2026-09-15-panel-sections.json`.

A second, smaller bend: the Motion section speaks for one layer while every
section around it speaks for everything picked, so picking two layers makes it
leave. The audit names it honestly. Folded into
`the-motion-section-sits-directly-under-effects-w`, which is about to move Motion
next to Effects and will make the difference louder.

**Are we missing components or guidance?** One thing got hand-rolled twice, and
it is the order canvas chrome is read in. The point a layer turns around is
looked at after the box's own resize handles, so a point parked within about
eleven points of a corner cannot be picked up from the picture at all. A day
earlier, the same mistake in the opposite direction was found and fixed in
passing on a path's points. Two local answers to one question means the canvas
needs one written order for what a press hits when chrome overlaps. Queued as
`the-point-a-layer-turns-around-can-be-grabbed-ev`, with writing the order down
as part of it.

Counting in the other direction, the system did its job once: the four sided
popout built for padding was reused the same day for the room knob on a copy of a
component, and the audit says so and names it as where the fifth grid of boxes
should not go.

**What UX issues keep recurring?**

- *A control reaching past the design system for a colour.* A motion's From and
  To colours open the Mac's own colour window, so the colours you saved are not
  offered in the one place you are choosing two colours that have to match. This
  is a regression against a finished p1 task that settled the one picker
  question. Queued as `a-motion-s-from-and-to-colours-are-picked-in-thi`,
  including a sweep for anything else added since that reaches for the system
  panel.
- *A plate makes the words readable and does not make the plate findable.* The
  component name was fixed with a plate on 2026-09-15 and the audit measured the
  plate's own edge against a dark picture at 1.4 to 1, carried by its shadow and
  its hue rather than by lightness. The screen name is about to get the same
  treatment. Folded into `a-screen-s-name-can-be-read-over-whatever-it-sit`
  so it measures the plate as well as the lettering.
- *A count you must act on later, shown in a notice that fades.* Still open from
  the 2026-09-13 review as `what-separate-left-behind-is-still-there-after-t`,
  and still the right read: separating a dense page says 142 out and 580 left in
  a pill and then says nothing at all.
- *A mock offering what the app cannot do.* Lottie and video formats, a GIF size
  comparison, currentColor, a Fit button, a pivot tool, a blade tool over a
  multi-track timeline. Runners cut every one of these and said so in the audit,
  which is the discipline working rather than failing, so this is named as a
  pattern and not filed.

**Do the documented rules need a scrub?** Yes, and the biggest one this pass is
not in the design documents. The repo rules describe a locked screen as one where
the app is never drawn and layout and animations stop, and the reproduction above
disproves the second half. Queued with the printed message and the code comment,
as `the-locked-screen-message-says-the-real-reason-a`. The standing UX-PATTERNS
scrub picked up a twelfth rule this pass, the drop section saying what a surface
says while something is held over it, folded in from a duplicate task that was
dropped.

**Judging the focus feature as a user.** Separate into Layers is where ui-building
is being pushed hardest and it is the most honest place to look. Reading its
audits as somebody who just cropped a screenshot: it finds words well and finds
boxes badly. On a real capture of this app's own inspector panel it returns three
boxes out of a dozen rows, because a row that runs to the edge of a crop is
thrown away before anything else happens, and on a whole window capture it
returns none at all, because it wants one flat colour behind everything and a
window offers 31 percent, 17 percent and 13 percent. Somebody separating a
picture of their own app gets its text and none of its buttons. That is the
feature's central promise not landing, and it is what the two tasks now at the
very front of p2 are for. The other five open Separate tasks are all about what
happens after it works, which is the right order.

## Ready to try

Everything here is in the app and none of it has been photographed, for the
reason in the summary. The audits are honest about which pictures are real and
which are drawings made outside the window.

**Animate an icon and export it.** Pick a layer, give it a motion, drag the point
it turns around on the picture, and watch the whole thing loop on a strip across
the bottom at the sizes the icon will really be used at. Then export it as an
animated SVG and open that in a browser. Audits:
`queue/audits/2026-09-15-layer-motion.json`,
`2026-09-15-motion-pivot.json`,
`2026-09-15-motion-timing-strip.json`,
`2026-09-15-motion-loop-preview.json`,
`2026-09-15-icon-export-animated.json`.

**Cut a recording into pieces.** The first video feature: cut where the playhead
is and drop a piece you do not want. Worth knowing before you try it that the cut
lands where the playhead reads, which updates about twenty times a second, so a
cut made during playback can sit up to fifty milliseconds early. Audit:
`queue/audits/2026-09-15-video-cutting.json`.

**Make two shapes into one.** Join them, cut one out of the other, or keep only
where they overlap, and turn a rectangle or an ellipse into a path you can
reshape afterwards. Audits: `queue/audits/2026-09-15-combine-shapes.json`,
`2026-09-15-shapes-into-one-path.json`,
`2026-09-15-turn-into-a-path-wash.json`.

**Give a component its states.** A button can now hold a resting, hovered,
pressed and disabled look, and there is a guide that walks you through making
them. Audits: `queue/audits/2026-09-15-states-guide.json`,
`2026-09-15-component-properties-naming.json`.

**Two tutorial tracks, on icons and on animating one.** Five new guides on icons
and four on motion. These are the ones most worth trying with the screen
unlocked, because not one of the nine has ever been driven in a real window.
Audits: `queue/audits/2026-09-15-icons-tutorial-track.json`,

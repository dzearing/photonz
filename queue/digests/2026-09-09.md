# Daily digest 2026-09-09

## Summary

**Saved styles became a system.** Yesterday a colour could be saved under a name.
Today a text treatment can, an effect can, and any of the three can be picked up
off the Library shelf and carried onto the thing you want to wear it: onto words
on the picture, onto a row in the layers list, and, when it cannot land, onto a
refusal that says why in plain words and names the moves that would work. The
text tool even comes away holding the name you last used, so the next block you
type follows the style rather than merely matching it. That is the
create-once-reuse-everywhere epic doing what it says, and it went from one kind
of saved thing to three in a day.

Fifty nine tasks finished in the last 24 hours, across 133 commits (104 of them
product code, the rest queue bookkeeping), with 48 audit reports written. The
work fell into four bands:

- **Styles and components** (14 tasks). Text styles saved, dragged, armed and
  dropped on rows; effect styles saved and re-tuned once for every layer wearing
  them; a copy of a component now says which component it came from when you
  point at it, gets its own Opacity row back with a way to follow the original
  again, and stopped showing two rows called Corner Radius holding different
  numbers.
- **The canvas and the right hand panel** (29 tasks). One margin for every row
  and heading, one unit word (px) everywhere, Corner Radius folding like an
  effect, the Effects list keeping room for the effect you just opened, and the
  panel scrolling itself to whatever you just picked. Also an angle you can read and type, each
  corner of a shape roundable on its own, and a corner you can drag round with
  the mouse.
- **Layout** (6 tasks). A row that runs out of room wraps, a group says when its
  contents run past its edge, a container too small for what is inside it says so
  and offers to fit, and there is one way to make a piece the surface behind the
  rest.
- **Measuring** (5 tasks). Size and Gap now measure shapes you drew, not only
  screenshots; the number stands off the edge it is measuring; and while you aim
  the third click of a distance you can see the actual number under the pointer
  instead of a thin bracket.

**Started or blocked:** eleven tasks stopped and asked a question rather than
guessing, one for each card opened, among them the marquee a paint tool throws
away, the border that arrives the colour of its fill, covering a name in a
screenshot, and whether one copy of a component can wear its own text style. Two
runners failed once each and both tasks were finished on the next attempt.
Nothing is parked.

**Decisions:** eleven questions were opened and one was answered. The one
answered was Outline leaving Appearance, at 06:56 yesterday morning, and it is
the most recent answer there has been. Twelve questions are now waiting.

Seven runners have asked for a sweep since 18:46 on Monday,
`queue/bin/sweep.sh due` says one is due right now, all seven requests are still
sitting unclaimed in `queue/sweep/requested.json`, there is no run log in that

the go loop process has been up since Saturday 5 September at 06:58, and the code
that runs the sweep between tasks landed on Monday 8 September at 18:14, three
days after that process read its own script.

What is waiting on that sweep is exactly the sort of change a sweep is for:
every section header in the dock moved two points, Corner Radius grew into a

The immediate fix is a human restarting the loop (`/go`); the general fix is
queued as `a-fix-to-the-loop-itself-reaches-the-loop-that-i`, because the same
staleness silently disables every future improvement to the loop, not just this
one.

the right thing by asking rather than guessing, but at this rate the queue turns
into a waiting room. Two thoughts, and the second one is the uncomfortable one:

**The app itself.** Reading the day's audits back to back, the panel is where all

## Design review

**Is the IA sound? One rule bent, and it is worth your attention.** The dock
rules (UX-PATTERNS section 3, Reveal) say: "Ambient changes never may: a
selection moving, a document loading, a background update leave a shut header
shut and do not scroll." As of yesterday, a selection moving scrolls the dock.
`7f65ae4d`, audit `queue/audits/2026-09-09-dock-picked-first-reveal.json`, whose
own setup line reads "Nothing to turn on; this is how the panel behaves now."
There is a fair argument that clicking a layer is not ambient, since you just did
it, but the rule as written says the opposite and the code now wins by default.
Queued as `the-dock-rules-say-what-picking-a-layer-may-do-t`.

The deeper version of the same finding: **five separate mechanisms now decide
what you can see in the panel**, each built by a different task in the last three
days. The order the sections sit in, the panel scrolling itself to the picked
section, the Effects list keeping room for the effect you just opened, the height
budget that decides which section gives way, and the reveal that scrolls to a
control inside a section. Three of the twelve open questions are arguing about
this same code. The panel file has grown to 5,017 lines and took 123 of the last
week's commits.

**Are we missing components or guidance?** Two, both queued:

- *Built three times in three days:* every surface that takes a drop works out
  for itself what is being carried and in what order to ask.
  `LayersPanel.swift:1618` hand-rolls it for a layer row (style first, then a row
  reorder, then a file, with the ordering argued in comments), the canvas does it
  again across two files, and three colour targets do it a third time. The next
  task in the queue adds a fourth branch to the same chain. Queued as
  `one-place-decides-what-a-layer-row-and-the-canva`, sequenced to run just
  before it.
- *Guidance gap:* the drop rule covers what a surface DRAWS while something is
  held over it (accent edge, wash, insertion line) and says nothing about what it
  SAYS. The app now writes a sentence in three places with three different
  placements, and one open task exists because two of those sentences count at
  you ("Paints Fill on all 2 of them"). Queued as
  `the-drop-rules-say-what-a-surface-says-while-som`.

**What UX issues keep recurring?**

1. **The panel runs out of room, and it is solved one case at a time.** Five
   tasks and five audits this cycle (`2026-09-08-effects-list-fits-one-open-effect`,
   `2026-09-09-effects-room`, `2026-09-08-effect-reveal`,
   `2026-09-09-dock-picked-first-reveal`, plus the open folding bug). Each one is
   good work; together they are five answers to one question.
2. **Walks go stale when the panel changes its words.** Also five tasks in one
   day: thirteen walks looking for an Outline row that is now a Border, five more
   asking for the outline that moved into Effects, four that could not find
   Shadow, four stopped on a control that had scrolled away, and one dropped
   outright. Walks name controls by the words printed on them, so a rename is a
   bill that lands on the next feature. Queued as
   `a-walk-names-a-panel-control-in-a-way-a-rename-c`.
3. **The same disclaimer, three audits running.** Three of the day's shipped
   features end with the same paragraph: no walk can prove that a tile actually
   comes away in your hand, please check by hand. That is the day's headline
   feature handing its riskiest step to the person meant to be enjoying it.
   Queued as `prove-a-tile-really-does-come-away-in-your-hand`.

**Do the documented rules need a scrub?** Two queued, both named above: the
Reveal rule (it contradicts shipped behaviour) and the drop rule (it is silent on
the sentence, so each surface is inventing one).

**Judging the focus feature as a user.** Taking the styles audits at face value
and trying to follow them: yes, someone could try this from the audits alone. The
setup steps are concrete and most shots are real window captures. Two things are
clumsy:

- **You click a row in the layers list and the list leaves.** The panel scrolls
  to the picked section, and the audit is honest about the cost: "the panel holds
  about 1030 points of sections in a 690 point window, so showing the Text
  section means the layers list goes off the top. You click a row and the list of
  rows leaves." Clicking between layers is the single most repeated action in
  building UI. This is now the acceptance item on the retitled panel task.
- **The pick-up is unproven.** See above. It is one manual check away from being
  settled, and until it is, three features are only 90% shipped.

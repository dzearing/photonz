# Daily digest 2026-09-03

## Summary

**Photonz got a component system.** In one night the app went from having no way
to reuse anything to: layers that hold layers, screens you build on, a Library
shelf in the dock, Make Component, copies that follow their original, knobs a
copy is allowed to change, and named colors. Then it kept going and polished the
whole thing. The ladder walked end to end and has an audit written from a real
run: `queue/audits/2026-09-03-component-end-to-end.json`.

80 tasks finished, 113 commits, 50 audits written, 4 decisions asked and 8
answered. The focus moved to `ui-building` at 23:48 last night, which the split
shows: 34 of the 80 served UI building, and 37 closed out measure and redline,
the focus it replaced.

The component ladder, in the order it landed:

- **Layers can hold layers.** The document model grew a tree, and a group paints
  as one object: its blur, corners, border, shadow and fade apply once to the
  whole thing rather than to each piece.
- **Group and ungroup** (⌘G, ⇧⌘G), with the layers list turned into a real tree
  you can twist open, drag rows into, and drag rows back out of.
- **Resize a group** and everything inside scales with it, in one undo step.
- **Frames**: a screen with a size that you build on, drawn with the F tool.
  Clicking its name on the canvas renames it in place.
- **The Library**, a shelf in the right dock with four scopes, that opens itself
  and scrolls into view when the app puts something new on it.
- **Make Component** (⌥⌘K), copies that follow their original including its fade,
  blur, corners, border and shadow, and **knobs**: an original decides which
  three kinds of thing a copy may change (its wording, whether a part shows, and
  which of several shapes it is). Make Alternatives turns two shapes into a
  choice in one step.
- **Five components ship in the box** (Button, Text Field, Card, Nav Bar, Badge),
  so the first thing you do is drag one out rather than build one.
- **Named colors**: save a color under a name, reuse it, change it once and every
  layer wearing it repaints. One named color can be set on a whole selection.

Alongside that, arranging got real: **align and space evenly** over a selection
(the answer to a decision you gave at 00:45), typed **position and size** for one
layer or many, **shift-click on the canvas** to add and drop layers, **paste and
drop landing on the screen you dropped them on**, and **a blank canvas from the
File menu**. Every number field and every name field now lands on Return, puts
itself back on Escape, and hands the keyboard to the picture either way, so the
next tool letter picks a tool instead of typing into a box.

The measure and redline side, the focus until late last night, closed out its own
list: calipers that keep their number legible at the edge of the
picture, a readout you can slide along its own measurement, arrow captions that
grow out of the arrow and can be dragged where you want them, one shared placer
for every pill on the canvas, and a tool bar with grouped families, real
tooltips and consistent hover.

The loop also got better at its own job: scripted walks can now read a whole
menu bar, press a chord and prove which command it reached, and drive the real
app with Screen Recording granted, so nearly every audit written yesterday
carries a real screenshot rather than an offscreen render.

**What did not go smoothly.** A monthly spend limit stopped the runners between
17:24 and 19:00, parking one task after three failures. The handling shipped the
same day, five hours earlier, and worked exactly as designed: the refusal was
recorded as an environment failure rather than a code failure, the loop backed
off, and the parked task completed on its own at 19:29 once the limit lifted.

## Reflections

**The one thing worth saying: nothing in the app fits its contents, and every
audit knows it.** Six audits written yesterday, by six different runs about six
different features, independently arrived at the same sentence. A longer label
overflows its button. A wording override overflows its box. Widening a component
leaves its label where it was instead of centred. Shrinking a group leaves text
hanging over the edge. A frame does not lay out what is inside it. And when you
answered the alignment question you said it yourself: "We should probably also
have grid components that can be placed within a component, grid and stack."

Auto layout is in the plan as a sub epic of UI building, staged **later**, which
is why your own ask has been sitting in the queue with nowhere to be worked on.
Only you can restage an epic, so this is a decision card rather than a
reordering:
`grid-and-stack-pieces-you-can-put-inside-a-compo-six-audits-in-one-day-say-nothin`.
The recommendation is to move it up now. There are four small component fixes
queued that are each under a day, and none of them are as visible as a button
that cannot hold its own word.

**The audits are carrying the process.** Every task that shipped yesterday wrote
one, and they are unusually honest: they say what was cut, what could not be
photographed, what was changed away from the mock and why. Almost every task in
today's queue came out of a `rough` line in one of them rather than out of a
plan. That is the loop working the way it was meant to.

**Where the process is weak: the walks cannot reach the panel.** The app's centre
of gravity moved into the right dock yesterday, and the scripted walks can only
drive the picture. They cannot open a menu inside a panel or drag a tile out of
one, so five separate pieces of work had to say "covered by a test rather than a
picture". Queued as `a-scripted-walk-can-open-a-menu-in-the-panel-and`.

**A smaller idea worth having.** The starter components audit asks whether five
is the right set and whether a neutral kit is the right look. Both are cheap to
change and both are guesses right now. If the answer to either is no, saying so
on that audit is a one-click change to a table of colors and sizes.

## Design review

Checked: every audit dated 2026-09-03 in `queue/audits/`, the decisions opened
and resolved in the window, and sections 1 to 5 of
`docs/design/mocks/shared/UX-PATTERNS.md` against what actually shipped.

**Is the IA sound? Mostly, with one rule that bent.** The Library arrived as a
new panel group in the one dock, which is exactly what the vocabulary says to do
("new capability is a new group"), and the layers tree, the frames and the
component chrome all sit inside the existing surfaces. The bend is **reveal**:
`2026-09-03-library-reveal.json` says it plainly against itself, that opening the
Library on the app's behalf "does overrule a collapse you chose on purpose". The
vocabulary defines a panel group as collapsible by its own header and gives each
group its own bounded scroller, but it never says who may un-collapse one, or
whether revealing something reaches inside that inner scroller or stops at its
header. Both halves bit: the reveal scrolls the dock to the shelf and then stops,
so the tile you just made can still be under the shelf's own fold, which is
already queued as `the-component-you-just-made-is-the-one-you-can-s`.

**Are we missing components or guidance? Yes, one clear extraction.** Four
different things in the app can now quietly stop following what they came from,
and three separate audits on the same day flag the same silence: a color that was
wearing a named style unlinks with no notice
(`2026-09-03-color-styles.json`), one part of a copy stops following the moment
you drag a slider on it and only says so afterwards
(`2026-09-03-component-look.json`), and ungrouping a copy or deleting an original
detaches things with no warning at all (`2026-09-03-component-instances.json`).
The notice itself already exists as the copies-followed pill, and it is about to
be reinvented three more times. Queued as
`when-a-link-quietly-breaks-the-app-says-so-the-s`, which also absorbs the
narrower color-only task that was already in the queue.

**Recurring UX issues.**

1. **Nothing sizes itself to its contents.** Six audits: starter components,
   overrides, the end to end walk, group resize, frames, align. This is the
   headline reflection above and is now on a decision card.
2. **Things silently stop following.** Three audits, described just above.
3. **A control moves house depending on what you picked.** With one layer picked
   a color lives in the Annotation, Text or Frame section; pick a second and
   those go away and a Color section appears instead, so the color you were just
   editing is somewhere else for no reason you caused
   (`2026-09-03-multi-style.json`). The same shape shows up in
   `2026-09-03-component-choice-one-step.json`, where a new knob lands on the
   original's list rather than the panel you are looking at. Queued as
   `a-color-is-in-the-same-place-whether-you-have-on`.
4. **Claims without pictures.** Five audits admit the walk could not photograph
   what they built, always for the same reason: it is in the panel, not on the
   picture. Queued as `a-scripted-walk-can-open-a-menu-in-the-panel-and`.

**Rule scrubs queued.** One task,
`scrub-the-panel-rules-against-what-the-dock-actu`, covering three spots in
`UX-PATTERNS.md` where reality has moved past the written rule:

- **Section 3, panels and surfaces.** No rule for reveal: who may open a group
  the user collapsed, and whether revealing reaches into the group's own
  scroller. Both were decided ad hoc yesterday.
- **Section 4, selection model.** It says multi-select shows shared properties
  only, and never says a property keeps its home. That is why colors ended up in
  a different section depending on how many layers are picked without anybody
  breaking a rule.
- **Section 5, layers representation.** It promises a component instance renders
  as a collapsed group "expandable to show overrides". The app deliberately
  ships the opposite: `LayerPanelTree.panelRows` makes a copy non-openable and
  puts its knobs in the Component section. The app is probably right and the rule
  is stale, but one of the two has to move.

**Judging the focus feature as a user.** Reading
`2026-09-03-component-end-to-end.json` cold, you could sit down and follow it:
draw a control, group it, make it a component, expose its label, drop three
copies, change one, edit the original and watch three follow. That is the sitting
we wanted. The ceremony that stands out is what happens either side of the good
part. Making a component is ⌥⌘K, a chord nobody will find, and the audit itself
asks whether you even noticed the Library open. And the reward at the end is a
button whose label overflows it the moment you type a longer word. The feature
works. What it does not yet do is hold together as something you would build a
screen out of, and the reason is the same one every audit named.

Boolean operations same two shapes · four outcomes
Union
boolean · add
Keeps every pixel covered by A or B. The two silhouettes merge into one.
Subtract
boolean · A minus B
Punches B out of A. Order matters, so a swap gives B minus A instead.
Intersect
boolean · and
Keeps only the region covered by A and B. Just the overlap survives.
Exclude
boolean · xor
Keeps A and B but drops the overlap, leaving a clean hole where they met.

The idea

Booleans turn two overlapping shapes into one, four ways: Union adds, Subtract removes, Intersect keeps the overlap, Exclude drops it. The op is a property of the selected layer, so it lives in Properties, and the four outcomes browse in an overlay instead of crowding the canvas. Open the raw Pen tool →

Direction

Keep the op live by default. The sources stay underneath as child rows in Layers, so you can drag circle A, change the operation, or swap subtract order and watch the result retune, then Release to get the two originals back. Nothing is baked into pixels.

Open question

Should a boolean stay a re-tunable group forever, or offer a one tap Flatten to a plain path once you are happy? And when a subtract fully erases a shape, should the agent warn you or just leave an empty path?

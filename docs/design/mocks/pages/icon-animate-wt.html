Make an icon pulse

· usage clickthrough · one scenario

One job: the notification bell is already drawn, and it should pulse when something new arrives. The interesting part is what this is not. There is no playhead, no ruler in seconds and no track list, because an icon animation is six hundred milliseconds long and it repeats: there is no frame worth finding. Motion is a property of the layer, picked by name and tuned with four numbers, and the review is the glyph running at the sizes it will really be used, slowed down when it moves too fast to judge. Nine real clicks on ONE screen, which never re-renders below.

The app has no answer for any of this yet. PhotonzCore has no notion of time, no keyframes and no motion on a layer, the video editor is a separate surface with its own state rather than a time model a layer document could borrow, and nothing exports SVG at all, animated or still. Everything below is a proposal about what the surface should be, drawn so it can be argued with before it is built.

  1. 1

    the Bell layer

    the Layers group, at the top of the dock

    Properties fills in with this layer's Geometry and, right under it, a Motion section that currently says None. That is the whole claim of this page: a glyph that can move is not a different kind of document, it is a layer with one more property.

  2. 2

    the Motion row

    Properties, in the Motion section

    a short list of named motions opens: Pulse, Wiggle, Bounce, Spin, Breathe, and Custom at the bottom. Nobody arrives wanting to author keys. They arrive wanting the bell to pulse, so the list is the front door and keys are the room behind it.

  3. 3

    Pulse

    the motion list

    the bell starts pulsing immediately, on the artboard and on all four copies in the Real size card, and it keeps running while you carry on working. Timing controls appear under the Motion row, and the layer picks up a small loop mark so the Layers list tells you which glyphs move.

  4. 4

    0.9 in the Over field

    Properties, under Motion

    one pass now takes nine tenths of a second instead of six. The change lands on the running preview as you type, so the number and the thing it describes are never out of step. This is the whole of "editing the timing": there is no ruler to drag a key along.

  5. 5

    Ease in-out

    the Easing row

    the pulse stops growing at a flat rate. It leans out, holds at the top, and settles back, which is what makes it read as a heartbeat rather than a machine. Three named curves, no bezier editor: at nine hundred milliseconds nobody can see the difference between a hand-drawn curve and the standard one.

  6. 6

    the Repeat row, and choose Forever

    Properties, under Motion

    the pulse stops counting passes. Forever is the honest word for what a notification badge does, and it is also the thing a timeline cannot draw: there is no end to put on a ruler. Repeat being a property rather than a length of track is not a shortcut, it is the correct shape.

  7. 7

    0.25×

    the speed control on the Real size card

    everything runs at a quarter rate so you can actually see the shape of it, and the point the scale turns around appears on the artboard while you watch. This is what replaces scrubbing. You cannot usefully drag a playhead through nine hundred milliseconds; you slow the loop until your eye can keep up.

  8. 8

    at the 16 copy, and push Grows to from 115 to 124

    the Real size card, and the Motion section

    at 16 points a 15% pulse is two and a half pixels of travel, which is a shimmer rather than a pulse. At 48 it was fine. This is the review an icon needs and a video timeline cannot give you: the same motion is right at one size and invisible at another, and only the copies tell you. The keys underneath are there if you want them, sitting at 0%, 50% and 100% of one cycle.

  9. 9

    the middle key along the cycle, from 50% to 62%

    Properties, in the One cycle section

    the bell now grows slowly and snaps back, because the peak sits late in the cycle. Look at what that strip is measured in: percent of one cycle, not seconds. A loop that repeats forever has no absolute time to sit on, which is the deepest difference between this and a video document and the reason the two cannot share one ruler. Changing Over from here on stretches the whole cycle and every key keeps its place.

The idea

Motion is a property of a layer, picked by name and described by four numbers, and the keys are an escape hatch rather than the front door. Nothing about this needs a mode, a second editor, or a document that knows what time it is.

What this deliberately is not

No playhead, no ruler in seconds, no track list, no transport. An icon animation is shorter than a second and it repeats, so there is no frame worth finding. The one time-ish object on the page is the cycle strip, and it is measured in percent of a cycle, which is what a looping animation actually is and what it exports as.

A direction

Motion saved as a style is the part that would make a set feel designed rather than assembled: one attention pulse the whole family shares, changed in one place. The Styles scope in the Library already has the shape for it.

Open question

Is the named list the right front door, or does it quietly become a wall of forty presets nobody reads? The alternative is no list at all: change the glyph, and the app records the change as a key, exactly the way does. That is more powerful and it is a worse answer to "make it pulse".

Video, in the window you already have

· study · the design pass

Video brings a transport, a timeline, waveforms, transitions and captions, and the fourteen video clickthroughs draw all of it into a window with no layers list in it. This page draws the same surface into the window the app actually has. The finding is that video needs almost no new chrome: the strip across the bottom already ships for animated icons, and its own source already says video can take it as it stands. What video costs is height, and that is measured below rather than asserted. Full write-up: docs/design/video-surface.md. Arithmetic: DockWithTimeTests, eight tests.

So the expensive thing about video is not video. A clip picked in this five layer document asks the panel for 922 points, which is six points LESS than a piece of text asks for today, because the layers list is the section that gives way. The Captions section costs 163, its header plus a three row floor. What breaks the budget is the band across the bottom: it is a full width region under the canvas and the dock, so the dock loses the whole of it. On a laptop window with the strip open the panel has 453 points and is over 280 points over. That number is not video's to fix and this study does not pretend to fix it — it names it, and the first answer is the control already on screen.

03 · the correction

A row is a layer, and it is called what the layer is called

All fourteen video clickthroughs name their rows V1, V2, Gfx, Audio — a numbered track model — and delete the Layers group from the dock to make room. The timing strip that shipped for icons does the opposite, and it is right.

Three rows, three layers, same names, same icons, same order as the list beside them. The empty track is gone because nothing is in it.

The complaint behind the deletion is real and the conclusion is wrong. video.html says Layers was "a second rendering of what you had just clicked, so the dock read as having no job", and removes it. But two views of one model is the app's normal condition — canvas, layers and panel already all follow one selection — and the timeline cannot replace the list, because a video document is full of layers that do not occupy time: a background, an adjustment, a matte, a component master. Delete Layers and those have no row anywhere. There is also no lever to pull: PanelSectionVisibility.optionalSections deliberately does not contain Layers, so no mode and no switch can take it away. Layers stays, and the rows are named for the same layers.

One consequence for the shipped code, and it is the only place the strip has to grow. MotionStrip.swift says today that "a layer row is a HEADING and not a bar: the layer itself does not occupy time, the properties on it do". True of a bell that rotates; false of a clip, which is exactly a start and an end. The rule becomes: a layer draws a bar when it has an in and an out, and a heading when it does not. One rule, both jobs, no fork.

And the way out of a full panel is already on screen. The strip collapses to one row that says what you are still editing — settings-capture · 0:08 / 0:18 — which is comp-video §06 and D9, and it hands back 205 points. The app already has all three states: motionStripPhase is open, row, or none.

The idea

A document may have a duration, and a layer in it may have an in and an out. Everything else follows: a clip is a layer, a row is a layer, a property lane is a child of the layer whose property it is, and a transition belongs to the join rather than to either side. No track model, no media object, no lens.

What is new

One panel section, Captions, shaped like Measurements and optional like it. One growth in the shipped strip: a layer row draws a bar when the layer occupies time. Everything else on this page is a component that already exists.

What is not fixed

The panel is 189 points over in a video document at the largest window, and over 280 on a laptop. Video did not cause it and video does not fix it. The number is named so the build tasks do not discover it as a surprise.

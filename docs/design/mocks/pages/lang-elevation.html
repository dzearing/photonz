Elevation & glass

· Design language Design language

Depth is a ladder, not a decoration. Every surface sits at one of a few levels: stage < window < panel < popover < menu < callout. Shadow shows how far a surface floats above content, and Liquid Glass tints and blurs whatever scrolls behind a transient one, so the stack reads at a glance. Reach for it whenever you need to say "this floats over that" without a heavy container.

01

Anatomy

One exploded stack, six roles. Each step up adds either a hairline, a shadow, or glass, never all three at once.

0Stage the desktop the window floats on. Fully opaque, no shadow. 1Window the document frame. Hairline border plus the ambient window shadow the OS draws. 2Panel docked chrome inside the window. Flat, divided by hairlines, never shadowed. 3Popover a transient float: inspector detach, color picker. Real shadow lifts it off the panel. 4Menu glass tint plus blur plus the big shadow, because it covers live content. 5Callout the top rung, and the only thing allowed to sit on a float: a card anchored to something inside a popover. It cannot reuse the popover's own tokens or it reads as a hole punched in it, so in dark it lifts by growing lighter and in light by casting further. Opaque, because glass over glass doubles the blur and muddies both. Class: .elev-3.

API depth is a role, not a number you pick: surface.level is derived from what the surface is.

03

The glass treatment

Glass is for surfaces that float over live content: the titlebar, menus, the command palette. Docked chrome that never overlaps the canvas stays opaque, so it is calmer and cheaper to draw.

One glass layer, directly over content.Stack glass on glass; the second blur just muddies the first.

API glass is implied by surface.level = 2 on a transient surface, not a toggle designers flip per element.

04

States

A surface changes elevation only when its relationship to content changes. Resting flat, it lifts a hair on hover and floats fully while you tear it off to a popover, then settles back when docked.

Add the focus ring on top of elevation, never instead of it.Animate a docked panel's shadow on hover; nothing floated.

API panel.float() promotes level 1 → 2; panel.dock() drops it back. The shadow follows the state.

surface.level is read-only and derived from a surface's role. You do not set an elevation; you set what the surface is (docked, floated, modal) and the ladder resolves the shadow and glass. That keeps the same depth logic whether a person clicks or an agent issues the command.

07

Light, dark & compact

the ladder holds in every context
light · glass over bright content
Palette floats, tint stays light, shadow softens.
dark · glass over dim content
Same surface, darker tint, deeper shadow for contrast.

Light and dark are two token sets, not two designs: --chrome, --shadow, and --line all flip, so a glass menu keeps the same legibility over a white photo or a black one. Compact: in a narrow window shadows soften and the stack shortens, so stacked glass never piles up into mush; a docked panel that would have floated stays flat and folds to an icon rail instead.

The idea

Depth should be legible, not decorative. Five roles on one ladder means you never guess whether a surface floats; the shadow and the glass answer it before you read a word.

A direction

Because elevation is derived from role, we could expose a "flatten chrome" accessibility mode that drops every level to a hairline and keeps only the top rung shadowed, without touching a single component. The design system content underneath is unaffected.

Open question

Glass is gorgeous but it costs a blur pass per transient surface. On a 12-megapixel document with a menu open, do we cap live glass to the frontmost overlay and freeze the rest to a static tint, or trust the compositor to keep it under 16ms?

Collapsible sections

· Design language Design language

Nothing in the dock is a flat wall of settings. There are exactly two levels of disclosure: the panel group (.dgrp) collapses a whole panel in the dock, and a section (.section) collapses one task inside that panel's body. Both are a header with a chevron, an optional value summary, and a body. You group by task, you collapse what the current tool never touches, and you read the important value straight off a closed header. Both levels are addressable, so section("Effects").collapsed = true does from an agent exactly what a click does.

01

Two levels, one behaviour

Group collapse · section collapse

Level 1 · the panel group

A .dgrp in the dock. Collapsing it hides an entire panel and hands its height back to the groups around it. Reach for it when the whole panel is irrelevant right now: no effects on this layer, no library browsing today. Its body carries its own bound and its own scroller, so it is also the thing that keeps a long list from crowding its siblings.

Level 2 · the section

A .section inside a group's body. Collapsing it hides one task's controls while the panel stays open. Reach for it when the panel is relevant but this part of it is not: Transform when you are picking a colour, Spacing when you are choosing a typeface. This is the level that carries a value summary.

06

Rules

Do / Don't
Group by task: Layout, Fill, Type, Effects. The names a person thinks in. Group by data type: "Numbers", "Colours". Nobody browses that way. Put the one value that matters into the summary. Leave a section unnamed, or collapse it with no summary at all. Open what the active tool uses and collapse the rest, remembered per selection type. A flat wall of ungrouped rows with no headers. Make the whole header the hit target, chevron included. Nest a third level. Two levels is the whole model. Use the group level when a whole panel is irrelevant, the section level when one task is. Hide the focus ring, or make the section a different toggle from the group.
07

In the API

Both levels are addressable
group("Effects").collapsed = trueLevel 1. Collapse a whole panel group in the dock.
section("Transform").collapsed = trueLevel 2. Collapse one task inside the open panel.
section("Layout").summaryRead-only, derived from the rows. Returns "400 × 630".
inspector.for(selection)Which sections a selection gets, and which of them lead.
panel.collapseAll()What the panel menu's "Collapse all sections" runs.

Collapse state and the chosen inspector are both addressable, so an agent opens the exact section it needs before it edits, and you can watch it happen. See the pattern in place in Panels & the dock or in the editor.

What changesand what does not

Below 880px the dock itself becomes a rail plus an overlay, so the panel you summon has the full height to work with. Inside it, every section but the active one starts collapsed and the value summary does the job the open body used to. Nothing is hidden: the answer is still on the header, one tap from the controls. The two-level model does not change with width, and the summaries are what make that possible.

The idea

Grouping plus a value summary on the header lets a panel scale from a plain shape to a component instance without ever becoming a wall. You read the state collapsed and open only what you are about to change.

A direction

Let the collapsed set be remembered per selection type, not globally. Selecting a text layer restores your text layout; selecting a component restores your component layout, so each kind of object feels tuned to itself.

Open question

Should a summary ever be editable in place, nudging opacity straight from a closed Fill header? It saves a click, but it blurs the line between "read the value" and "open to change it".

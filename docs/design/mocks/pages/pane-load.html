Is the right hand pane carrying too much?

· study · measured, then answered

The side pane now holds the layers, what you picked, where it sits, what it looks like, what you added to it, the shelf you fetch from, and whatever the tool in your hand brought with it. The next thing wanting room is the timing of an animation, which does not fit a narrow column at all. So: is the pane overloaded, and what should live somewhere else? The short answer is that it is overloaded, and the timing surface is not the reason. Every number below was read out of the running app rather than estimated: a three-layer document with one piece of text picked already asks the pane for 1052 points of room, and the tallest window this machine can give it is 968. The pane does not fit today, at any window size, before animation exists. Moving the timing out is still right, for a different reason, and it fixes none of that.

1052

Points the pane asks for

Three layers, one piece of text picked, one drop shadow on it, nothing unusual. Layers 163 · Text 198 · Appearance 223 · Effects 332 · Position & Size 130.

968

Points a big window can give

At 1680 by 1000 the pane's scrolling area is 968 points tall. At a 1200 by 720 laptop window it is 688, and at 1000 by 640 it is 608. Position & Size is below the cut in all three.

2

Tenants that actually want width

Out of twenty-six sections, exactly two are shaped wrong for a narrow column: the Library shelf, and a timing surface. Everything else is a stack of labelled rows and a column suits it fine.

Three different competitions, and only one of them is about how many things live in the pane. Almost everything here competes for vertical room, and that contest is already lost: 1052 points asked for against 968 available, in a document with three layers in it. Only two things compete for horizontal room, and they are the two the pane is the wrong shape for. The tool sections compete for your attention instead: they are the right size and in the right place, they just come and go under your eyes.

The one question the motion exists to answer is now a shape. The knob's bar starts a little to the right of the bell's, and the gap between them is 90 milliseconds you can see and drag. Nothing was invented to do it: the bottom dock is a region the app's own shell has always reserved for a document with time, and it collapses the same way the side dock does. And the panel in this window still has to be scrolled, which is the finding this study keeps coming back to: moving the timing out is the right move for timing, and it is not a fix for the pane.

The timing dock does not grow with the window: it stays at the height it was left, and the canvas takes everything else. Width is what it gains, and width is the thing it actually wanted: at 1680 the 900 millisecond cycle is drawn over about 1400 points, so 10 milliseconds is 15 points and the knob's lag is a drag you can land.

And when nothing is animated, there is no timing dock at all. An icon document with no motion in it looks exactly like today's window: the bottom dock appears the first time you add a Motion entry, and disappears when you take the last one away. That is the same rule the app's own shell already states for a document with time, so it is not a new place to look, it is a place that was always reserved and never built.

What stays in the pane, and why moving it would be worse

  • Motion stays. What changes and by how much is a list you add to, entry by entry, with a switch on each: that is exactly what Effects already is, sitting directly above it. Splitting one idiom across two surfaces to save 130 points of height would buy nothing and cost the thing that makes it learnable.
  • Position & Size stays. It is five small boxes on one line. It does not want width, it wants to not be last.
  • The tool sections stay. A tool's settings belong with the tool, and the app has already settled that. What they need is to stop shifting everything under them, which is a different fix.
  • The Library is the open question. It is the only other thing in the pane shaped wrong for a column, and browsing a set of icons two tiles at a time is the symptom. It is not part of this proposal, because moving the shelf and moving the timing are two separate arguments and bundling them would get one of them decided by accident.

The honest tension. The app deliberately keeps one dock so that everything has one home, and every surface moved out of it is a second place to look. That argument is right, and it is why this study moves exactly one thing. Timing earns the exception because it is the only thing in the app whose meaning is a relationship between two layers laid out across width: you are not reading a value, you are reading a gap. No amount of height in a 265 point column shows a gap.

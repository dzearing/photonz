Overlays & menus

· Design language Design language

Overlays are how the app grows without the canvas shrinking. They float over the document, do one job, and leave, so a surface you browse occasionally costs no permanent width. The canonical one is the slide-down sheet (.sheet.down), and the most important instance of it is the capture history at ⇧⌘H, which is the app's front door. Around it sit five smaller shapes: a popover menu, the ⌘K palette, a tooltip, a toast, and the rare blocking dialog. Each is one command away, so an agent can open exactly what you can.

01

The family

When to reach for each, and how it goes away
OverlayWhen to useLifetimeDismissFocusPosition
Slide-down sheet ⇧⌘H A surface you browse occasionally and that needs room: capture history, a big catalog. Transient Esc, the close button, or picking an item. Focus moves into the sheet, then back to the canvas. Slides down from the top edge of the canvas.
Popover menu Act on the current selection, or open a short list from a control. Transient Esc, click away, or pick an item. Roving focus over items; arrow keys move it. Anchored at the pointer or at its trigger.
Command palette ⌘K Run any command by name, keyboard first. Transient Esc, run a command, or click away. Holds focus in its field; returns it after. Centred, upper third of the window.
Tooltip Name a control or show its shortcut. Transient · hover Pointer leaves, or after a short beat. Never takes focus. Above or below the control it names.
Toast Confirm something just happened. Transient · timed Fades after about four seconds, or swipe. Never takes focus; work is not interrupted. Top centre; later toasts stack downward.
Modal dialog Ask a blocking question before proceeding. Rare. Persistent · modal A button choice, or Esc for Cancel. Traps focus; the safe action is focused first. Centred on a scrim; a bottom sheet when narrow.

Rule of thumb. Reach for an overlay when the surface is browsed occasionally, and for a panel group when it is referenced constantly. Everything here is transient except the dialog, which is why the dialog is the rare one. Never stack two modals, and never put a surface in an unexplained new window.

07

Rules

Do / Don't
Use a slide-down sheet for a surface that needs room but is browsed occasionally. Give a browsed-occasionally surface a permanent slice of the window's width. Let Esc and a click away close any transient overlay. Open a surface in an unexplained new window with no way back. Give every menu item its real shortcut, right-aligned. Use a menu for navigation. Menus are for actions. Reserve the modal dialog for a genuinely blocking question. Stack two modals, or open a dialog from inside a dialog. Confirm quiet, reversible actions with a toast. Put a destructive step behind a toast that just fades away.
08

In the API

Every overlay is a command
sheet.open("history")Slides the history sheet down. ⇧⌘H and the canvas History button run this.
menu.show(at, items)Posts a context menu whose items are named commands, nothing more.
palette.open()Raises ⌘K, the fourth equal way to reach any of these.
tooltip.for("align-left")Reads a control's label and key, so the hint and the shortcut can never drift.
toast.post("Shadow added")Confirms and auto-fades. Returns nothing, because nothing is waiting on it.
dialog.confirm({title, danger})Blocks and returns the choice. The only call here that waits.

Because a menu item is a command like run("effect.shadow"), the palette, a right-click, the menu bar and the agent all reach the same action, on the same undo stack. See the history sheet in the whole shell in App shell.

The idea

Overlays are how the app adds surface without spending the canvas. The slide-down sheet is the canonical one and the history is its most important instance: the front door is always one keystroke away and costs nothing at rest.

A direction

Because each overlay is a command (sheet.open, menu.show, toast.post), the log that records a right-click also records an agent's edit. One audit trail, one undo stack, whoever drove it.

Open question

Should a toast ever carry an Undo button? A timed surface with a hidden action is easy to miss, so perhaps only reversible confirmations earn one, and everything else relies on ⌘Z.

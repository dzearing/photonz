Segmented control

· Component library Component library

One of a small set of exclusive options, with all of them visible at once. That is the whole reason it exists rather than a .select: you compare the options and hit one directly, without opening anything. It costs width to do that, so most of this page is about when not to use it. It is the system's own segmented control, drawn by macOS, not by us.

Top: the drawn control it replaced. Middle: the system control in a window in the back. Bottom: the same, in front. Photographed in the app, 2026-09-29.

01

It is the system's

Every row of side-by-side choices in the app (a panel row, a dialog, the history bar's filter, the title bar's View | Edit) is macOS's own segmented control, one continuous track, the same everywhere. macOS draws the track, the picked segment, its contrast in light, dark and Increase Contrast, and how it changes. Nothing is painted over it.

What macOS 26 draws, filmed in the app: in a window in front, the picked segment is filled with the accent colour; in a window behind, it is a grey plate. A new pick switches at once, it does not slide.

Use the system control and let macOS draw it. Draw a rail, a thumb or a glass pane of your own. That is what made the history filter's word unreadable.

Decision 2026-09-29, the user: "what happened to using the native liquid glass segmented control." Until then this page specified a drawn control: a recessed rail with a sliding pane of glass. The drawn one overshot its rail and put a white word on white glass, so it was retired the same day.

02

Sizes & forms

The system's control sizes, chosen by where it sits: small in a dense panel row and in the title bar beside the panel button, regular in dialogs and the history bar.

Fill (a panel row or popover): the control takes the row's width, in equal columns when the longest word fits an equal share, and each word's own width when it does not. Natural (in a row of other controls): equal segments as wide as the longest word.

Pictures instead of words: every picture segment is named, and that name is its tooltip, because a picture with no word is unlabelled.

Let it fill the column inside a panel, so a stack of rows aligns. Mix sizes in one panel. Pick the density once.
03

It never wraps. It becomes a menu.

When the words would have to be cut short, the control turns into a dropdown holding the same choices, and comes back when the room does. A wrapped or truncated segmented control reads as broken. Picture rows never collapse.

Let it collapse. The menu still shows every option. Ship six segments and hope the panel is wide. It never is.
04

States & keyboard

All of it is the system's. One option that cannot be picked keeps its place, dimmed by macOS, and says why in its tooltip. The whole control dims when it is disabled. Keyboard focus and arrows behave as they do in every Mac app.

Keep an unavailable option visible and say why in its tooltip. Remove a segment when it is unavailable. The row jumps.

In the app: SegmentedControl (Sources/Photonz/DesignSystem/SegmentedControl.swift) is the one place a segmented control is made, and SegmentedControlUsageTests fails the build on any other, or on the control being drawn again.

The idea

A segmented control spends width to save a click. That trade is good when there are three short options and bad when there are six, so most of the spec is a boundary rather than a style. The style is the system's.

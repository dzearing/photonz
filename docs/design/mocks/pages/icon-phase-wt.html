Make the knob lag the bell

· usage clickthrough · one scenario

Picking up where stopped. The bell already swings about the mount at its top, and the knob underneath is still sitting where it was drawn, because nothing has been asked of it. Two parts of one icon, the same motion, different timing: that is the whole page, and it is the thing a column of numbers cannot show you. A lag is a relationship between two things in time, so it needs both of them on one ruler at once. Seven real clicks on ONE screen, which never re-renders below.

None of this exists in the app yet. PhotonzCore has no notion of time, no keys and no motion on a layer, and nothing exports SVG at all. The one thing below that is more than a drawing problem is the pivot the knob borrows from the bell: the two layers turn about the same point, which the app would have to let a layer say out loud rather than each layer owning a pivot nobody else can see. That is a real modelling decision and it is called out on the Around row rather than glossed over.

  1. 1

    the menu on the bell's Rotation entry

    the Motion list, on the row itself

    the same menu an effect row has: copy it, duplicate it, save it as something the whole set can share, remove it. Copy motion is the one to take. This is the point of making motion a list entry rather than a mode: an entry is a thing you can pick up and put down somewhere else.

  2. 2

    the Knob layer

    the Layers group

    its Motion list is empty, and the bell keeps swinging while you look at it. Nothing about the bell's entry reached this layer: motion belongs to a layer the same way an effect does, which is the reason two parts of one drawing can be told apart in time at all.

  3. 3

    Paste motion

    the top of the Motion plus menu

    the knob joins in, a second Rotation entry appears under it with the same numbers, and a second bar lands on the timeline, exactly under the first. It also looks wrong, just differently from last time: the bell and the knob now move as one stamped shape, like a sticker being waved, because nothing hanging off a bell has ever moved in perfect step with it.

  4. 4

    the knob's bar to the right until the gap reads 90 ms

    the timeline, across the bottom

    and it becomes a bell. The knob is always a tenth of a second behind, so it trails through the middle and catches up at each end, which is what something hanging off a swinging object does. This is the step that is not possible in the side column. The Start field says 90 and it is the same number, but the number is not the point: the point is seeing one bar start after the other and being able to push it until it looks right.

  5. 5

    -17 and 17 into the knob's From and To

    the knob's Motion entry

    the knob swings wider than the bell it hangs from, which is the other half of "a different momentum": a lighter thing on a longer arm overshoots. Two numbers now differ between the two entries, the delay and the angle, and that is the entire difference. Nothing here is a preset and nothing had to be invented to say it.

  6. 6

    0.25×

    the speed control on the Real size card

    everything runs at a quarter rate, including the lag, so you can watch the knob cross behind the bell instead of guessing. Ninety milliseconds is under six frames and no one can judge it at full speed. This is what replaces scrubbing for something that loops: you cannot usefully drag a playhead through 900 ms, so you slow the loop until your eye can keep up.

  7. 7

    at the dashed repeats line, and at the 16 copy

    the timeline, and the Real size card

    the knob's bar runs past the line where the cycle restarts. It is still finishing its swing while the bell has already begun the next one, and that is not a bug to tidy up, it is what a lag IS in something that loops. You can see it because both bars are on one ruler. Then look at 16 points: a 17 degree swing on a one and a half unit knob is under two pixels, so at the size this icon will really be used the lag you just spent five minutes on is invisible, and the honest move is to widen it or drop the knob's entry entirely.

Why this page exists

Everything on could have been done in the side column. A lag cannot. It is a relationship between two things in time, so it needs both of them drawn on one ruler, which is the argument for a dock across the bottom rather than another panel in a column that is already crowded.

Same motion, different timing

The knob's entry is a copy of the bell's, and then two of its numbers change: it starts 90 ms later and swings five degrees further. That is the whole of what "a slightly different momentum" means, expressed in the same list, with no new vocabulary and no preset anywhere near it.

One pivot, two layers

The knob turns about the bell's mount, not about a point inside itself, because that is where it hangs from. A model where every layer owns a private origin cannot say this. It is the one thing on this page that is a real modelling decision rather than a drawing, and the Around row says so out loud.

The honest ending

Step 7 is not a victory lap. At 16 points the lag is under two pixels and you cannot see it, so the last thing the page does is admit that the careful work might have to be thrown away at the size the icon will really be used. The real-size strip is there to tell you that before you ship it, not after.

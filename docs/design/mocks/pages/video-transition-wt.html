Put a transition on a cut

· usage clickthrough · one scenario

One job, start to finish: take the cut between two clips and make it a dissolve, then tune it. The point is that the cut is selectable. A transition is not a sticker you drop between clips, it is an object that belongs to that edit point and carries a type, a length, an alignment, and a curve. Eight real clicks on ONE screen, which never re-renders below.

  1. 1

    the cut between screen-recording and b-roll.mov

    the V1 track on the timeline. Every seam between two clips carries this grabber

    the cut itself is selected, and Properties speaks for it: which clip goes out, which comes in, and that there is no transition on it yet. A cut is not a layer, so this is the only place it can have properties of its own.

  2. 2

    Add transition

    Properties, in the Transition section

    the picker opens beside the row it belongs to, showing what can go on this cut. Each tile previews the move and names its default length.

  3. 3

    Cross dissolve

    the picker

    a 0.6 second dissolve lands on the cut. The grabber becomes a marker that straddles the seam, and Properties fills in with the four things a transition actually has: type, length, alignment, curve.

  4. 4

    the marker's edge out until it reads 1.0s

    the timeline, on the cut

    the transition grows on the timeline and the Length row follows. Length is a direct drag on the thing, not a number you go and find, and the readout in Properties is the same value seen from the other side.

  5. 5

    Ease in-out

    Properties, in the Curve row

    the blend stops running at a flat rate. It holds on the outgoing shot, moves through the middle, and settles on the incoming one, all in the same 1.0 seconds.

  6. 6

    the playhead onto the cut, at 0:08

    the transport scrubber, under the canvas

    the canvas shows the halfway frame: both shots at once, the recording going and the b-roll arriving. Nothing rendered first. Scrubbing is the preview, so a transition is checked the same way any other value over time is.

  7. 7

    the Type row, and pick Dip to black

    Properties, in the Transition section

    the type changes and everything you tuned survives it: still 1.0 seconds, still eased, still centred on the cut. At 0:08 the frame is now fully black, because the middle of a dip is the bottom of the dip.

  8. 8

    Ends

    Properties, in the Alignment row

    the marker slides back so the dip finishes exactly on the cut, and the playhead has not moved: at 0:08 you are now looking at clean b-roll. Alignment decides which clip pays for the transition, and the timeline shows you the answer instead of describing it.

The idea

The cut is selectable. That one decision removes the usual transition dance: no dragging an effect from a bin and hoping it snaps, no dialog. Select the seam, and the seam has properties, because a transition belongs to the edit point rather than to either clip.

A direction

Swapping the type keeps length, alignment, and curve. That suggests a transition is a style: pick one you like, save it to Library at scope Styles, and apply it to every cut in a cut-down with one click.

Open question

Should a cut with no transition still show a grabber on every seam, or should the seam only become a target when you hover it? Always-on is discoverable and it is also a lot of small marks on a 40-clip edit.

Move a graphic from one point to another

· usage clickthrough · one scenario

One job: take a piece of clip art and animate it across the frame, from where it starts to where it lands. The whole idea is that a key is a value recorded at a moment. There is no keyframe window and no animate mode: you move the playhead, you move the thing, and Photonz writes down what changed. Eight real clicks on ONE screen, which never re-renders below.

    the Sparkle clip

    the Gfx track on the timeline

    one selection in three places: the ring on the canvas, the clip outlined on the timeline, and Properties showing this layer’s Transform and an Animate section. Nothing about animating is hidden behind a mode.

  2. 2

    the playhead to 0:04

    the transport scrubber

    the playhead lands on the first frame of the clip. A key always records the value at the playhead, so getting there is the whole setup.

  3. 3

    the keyframe button on the Position row

    Properties, in the Animate section

    Position starts animating. A diamond lands at 0:04 on a new Position lane, directly under the clip and on the same ruler. That lane is the at-a-glance tell that this layer moves.

  4. 4

    the playhead to 0:08

    the transport scrubber

    four seconds later. With one key the graphic has not moved: a single key is a value that is true everywhere, which is exactly what you want before you say where it goes.

  5. 5

    the graphic up beside the button it is pointing at

    the canvas

    moving it at a new time writes the second key for you. The lane fills in the span between the two diamonds, and the canvas draws the path it will travel. That dashed line and that lane are the same two numbers, one seen in space and one seen in time.

  6. 6

    Ease in-out

    Properties, under Between the keys

    the move stops running at a flat rate. It leans out of the first position and settles into the second, over the same four seconds. Easing belongs to the segment between two keys, so a later move can behave differently.

  7. 7

    the handle on the middle of the path, upward

    the canvas

    the straight run becomes an arc, and the Path control flips to Curved to say so. Nothing was added: the two keys are the same two keys, and what changed is how the value gets from one to the other.

  8. 8

    the playhead back to 0:06

    the transport scrubber

    the graphic sits partway along the arc, up near the top of its curve because the easing has already carried it most of the way. Every readout in Properties shows its interpolated value at that instant. Nothing renders first: scrubbing is the preview.

The idea

Animating is two keys and the road between them. You are never in an animate mode: you move the playhead, you change the thing, and the change is recorded. The lane on the timeline and the dashed path on the canvas are the same pair of values, shown in time and shown in space.

A direction

Because the path is a real object, it could be drawn first: sketch the route with the pen, then say how long it takes. That suits handwritten-looking motion far better than nudging numbers.

Open question

Dragging at a new time wrote a key without being asked. That is fast and it is also how people end up with keys they did not mean. Should auto-key be on only while a property already has one, as it is here?

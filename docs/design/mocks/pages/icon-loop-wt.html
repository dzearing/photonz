Build a spinner that never stops

· usage clickthrough · one scenario

One job: the loading spinner should turn, for as long as the thing it is waiting on takes. This is the case with no last frame in it. A spinner is not a four second animation that happens to repeat, it is one turn plus the word forever. The flow is three decisions: which part moves, what it does, and how long one turn takes. On the way it runs into the mistake everybody makes once, which is that a rotation needs a point to turn around and the obvious default is the wrong one. It uses the same two surfaces as every other animation flow, a Motion list beside Effects and a timing dock across the bottom, and the dock is where the loop stops being like anything else: the bar has no end, it has a line where the cycle starts again, and the playhead crosses it and comes back. Eight real clicks on ONE screen, which never re-renders below.

The app has no answer for any of this yet. There is no motion on a layer, no repeat, no transform origin you can choose, no timing surface of any kind and no SVG export. Everything below is a proposal, drawn so it can be argued with before it is built.

  1. 1

    the Arc layer

    the Layers group, at the top of the dock

    Properties fills in for the arc alone and the faint Track behind it stays unselected. A spinner is two shapes where only one of them moves, so the first decision is not “animate this icon”, it is which part. The dock below Properties has two lists with the same header and the same plus: Effects, which is what the layer paints, and Motion, which is what changes. The strip across the bottom already has a row for each layer, both reading none.

  2. 2

    the plus on the Motion list

    the Motion group header, the same place Effects has one

    the same list of this layer’s own properties as every other glyph: position, scale, rotation, opacity, colour, and the path ones. There is no entry called Spin, because a spinner is not a kind of motion, it is Rotation, 0° to 360°, forever, and the three parts of that sentence are three ordinary controls.

  3. 3

    Rotation

    the property list

    an entry lands in the Motion list, a bar lands on the strip, and the arc is wrong. It swings around its own middle and orbits the artboard instead of spinning in place, because the obvious default for “turn this shape” is the shape’s own center and an arc’s center is not the circle’s. Everyone meets this once. The fix is one row down.

  4. 4

    the Around row, and choose Artboard

    Motion, under the Rotation entry

    the orbit becomes a spin. A crosshair appears at 12, 12 while you are choosing, so the point it turns around is something you see rather than something you deduce from the wobble. This row is the difference between the mock being honest and the mock being pretty.

  5. 5

    800 in the Over field

    Motion, under the Rotation entry

    the bar shortens, the dashed line comes in to meet it, and the playhead starts going round faster. Over is the same field here as everywhere else, and the Repeat row underneath is what changes its meaning: with Forever on it, a length becomes a rate. That is also the honest reason this page gets a strip at all. A rate is the one number nobody can type confidently, so you set it by watching, and watching needs something to watch.

  6. 6

    Ease in out

    the Curve row

    the turn now hesitates once every pass, and the panel says why underneath the control that caused it rather than in a dialog you have to dismiss. Easing describes something that starts and finishes; on a loop with no end it just puts a stutter where the cycle joins. Nothing is blocked: you can leave it this way if that is what you meant.

  7. 7

    Linear, then look at the dashed and again line

    the Curve row, then the timing dock

    the stutter goes and the caution goes with it. Now look at the strip: there is no end mark on it, the way there is on every other animation page. The bar runs into a dashed line where the cycle starts over, and the playhead crosses that line and reappears at zero for as long as you leave it. Beside the turn length the dock says ends where it starts, which is the only thing a repeating animation must get right and the one thing the eye will not catch.

  8. 8

    at the 16 copy, turning at the same rate as the 48

    the Real size card

    a spinner is the one motion that survives being small, because the eye reads the sweep rather than the distance. Compare that with the knob on , whose whole lag turned out to be invisible at 16. One bar, one dashed line and a playhead that never stops is the entire timing surface a spinner needs, and none of it is a ruler with a last frame on it.

The idea

A continuous motion is one cycle and the word forever. It has no last frame, so the strip has nothing to end at and does not pretend to: the bar runs into the line where it starts again. One animated property covers the whole thing, and the property is Rotation like any other.

The part worth arguing about

The Around row. A rotation needs a point, the sensible-looking default is wrong for arcs, crescents and hands, and you only find out by watching it wobble. Showing the crosshair while you choose turns a puzzle into a glance.

What the dock is earning here

Not a comparison, which is what it earns on . Here it earns a rate you cannot type. Nobody knows what 800 milliseconds per turn feels like, so you set it by watching something go round, and a playhead that never stops is the cheapest thing to watch.

Open question

Should easing on a forever loop be a caution, as drawn here, or should the app simply not offer it? Cautions that are always right get ignored; controls that are quietly missing get reported as bugs.

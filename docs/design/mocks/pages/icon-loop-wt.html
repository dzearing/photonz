Build a spinner that never stops

· usage clickthrough · one scenario

One job: the loading spinner should turn, for as long as the thing it is waiting on takes. This is the case a timeline cannot draw. A spinner is not a four second animation that happens to repeat, it is one turn plus the word forever, and a ruler with a last frame on it is the wrong instrument for it. The flow is three decisions: which part moves, what it does, and how long one turn takes. On the way it runs into the mistake everybody makes once, which is that a rotation needs a point to turn around and the obvious default is the wrong one. Eight real clicks on ONE screen, which never re-renders below.

The app has no answer for any of this yet. There is no motion on a layer, no repeat, no transform origin you can choose, and no SVG export of any kind. Everything below is a proposal, drawn so it can be argued with before it is built.

Rotation on a ring arrives at 0° to 360°, Forever and Linear, because that is what you would have typed. None of it is locked: they are the answer offered, not a preset applied.

Easing fights a loop that never ends. The turn slows to a stop at 100% and starts again from nothing, so it hesitates once every turn. Easing is for a motion that begins and finishes.

Two keys and nothing between them to decide. Because the last key is the first key turned all the way round, there is no visible seam, which is the one thing a repeating animation has to get right and the one thing nobody checks by eye.

  1. 1

    the Arc layer

    the Layers group, at the top of the dock

    Properties fills in for the arc alone, and the faint Track behind it stays unselected. A spinner is two shapes where only one of them moves, so the first decision is not "animate this icon", it is which part.

  2. 2

    the Motion row

    Properties, in the Motion section

    the same list of this layer's own properties opens as on every other glyph: position, scale, rotation, opacity, colour, and the path ones. There is no entry called Spin, because a spinner is not a kind of motion, it is Rotation, 0° to 360°, forever, and the three parts of that sentence are three ordinary controls.

  3. 3

    Rotation, then set To to 360 and Repeat to Forever

    the property list

    the arc starts turning and it is wrong. It swings around its own middle and orbits the artboard instead of spinning in place, because the obvious default for "turn this shape" is the shape's own center and an arc's center is not the circle's center. Everyone meets this once. The fix is one row down.

  4. 4

    the Around row, and choose The artboard center

    Properties, under Motion

    the orbit becomes a spin. A crosshair appears at 12, 12 while you are choosing, so the point it turns around is something you see rather than something you deduce from the wobble. This row is the difference between the mock being honest and the mock being pretty.

  5. 5

    0.9 in the Per turn field

    Properties, under Motion

    the whole of the timing, in one field. Notice the label: per turn, not duration, because the thing has no duration. The preview changes rate as you type rather than after you commit, which is what makes a number this subjective possible to pick at all.

  6. 6

    Ease in-out

    the Curve row

    the turn now hesitates once every pass, and the panel says why underneath the control that caused it rather than in a dialog you have to dismiss. Easing describes something that starts and finishes; on a loop with no end it just puts a stutter at the seam. Nothing is blocked: you can leave it this way if that is what you meant.

    the Curve row

    the stutter goes and the caution goes with it. A One turn section is now showing what the motion actually is: two keys, 0° and 360°, and a loop check that says it ends where it starts. That check is the only thing a repeating animation must get right, and it is the thing nobody catches by eye.

    the Real size card

    a spinner is the one motion that survives being small, because the eye reads the sweep rather than the distance. Compare that with the pulse on , which vanished at 16 and had to be made bigger. Nothing in this flow had an end, a ruler or a playhead in it, and nothing was missing.

The idea

A continuous motion is one cycle and the word forever. There is no duration to give it and no last frame to draw, so a timeline has nothing to measure. Two fields and one animated property cover the whole thing, and the property is Rotation like any other.

The part worth arguing about

The Around row. A rotation needs a point, the sensible-looking default is wrong for arcs, crescents and hands, and you only find out by watching it wobble. Showing the crosshair while you choose turns a puzzle into a glance.

A direction

The loop check could do more than confirm the ends match. It could catch the real seam bugs: a value that drifts a fraction each pass, a motion whose duration is not a multiple of a sibling's so two spinners slowly fall out of step.

Open question

Should easing on a forever loop be a caution, as drawn here, or should the app simply not offer it? Cautions that are always right get ignored; controls that are quietly missing get reported as bugs.

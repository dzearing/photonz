Draw a checkmark on

· usage clickthrough · one scenario

One job: when something succeeds, the success badge should draw itself rather than appear. Two things are different here from the other animation flows. Nothing moves, turns or scales: what changes is how much of the stroke has been drawn, which is a motion only a vector tool can offer and the one Photonz is closest to being able to do, because it already has the path and can measure it. And it is the scenario where order is the whole craft: the ring draws, then there is a 180 millisecond hold, then the check draws, then the fill comes up underneath it. 1.20 seconds end to end, which is slow for a badge and deliberately so, because three beats have to be separable before anybody can judge whether to tighten them. Every part carries an explicit start, and step 4 shows on purpose what happens when they do not. Eight real clicks on ONE screen, which never re-renders below.

The app has no answer for any of this yet. There is no motion on a layer and no SVG export, animated or still. The nearest thing that does exist is the vector model itself: this motion needs the length of a path and the direction it runs in, and both are things a pen tool knows. It is the cheapest of the four to build and the last one anybody would think of.

  1. 1

    the Ring layer

    the Layers group, at the top of the dock

    Properties opens with a Path section, and the first thing in it is a number nobody typed: this stroke is 56.5 units long. That measurement is the whole reason the next step is possible, and it is the sort of thing only a drawing tool has lying around. Below Properties sit two lists with the same header and the same plus: Effects, which is what the layer paints, and Motion, which is what changes.

  2. 2

    the plus on the Motion list

    the Motion group header, the same place Effects has one

    the same list of this layer's properties as every other glyph, with one heading that only a drawn shape gets: Path, holding Path points, Stroke width and Dash. Everything above that heading moves the shape around. These change the shape itself, which is a different kind of thing and worth a line between them. Drawing a stroke on is not a preset called Draw on, it is Dash going from all gap to all dash, and it is only offered because the path knows how long it is.

  3. 3

    Dash, and set Over to 420

    the property list, under Path

    the ring draws itself round and the timing dock at the bottom gets its first bar: 0 to 420, drawn where it happens. The check and the fill are hidden for the moment so there is one thing to look at. Nothing moved, turned or scaled. Repeat came up as Once, unlike every other motion in the app, because a success mark that keeps redrawing is a success mark nobody believes; the preview replays it so you can watch it, which is a different thing and the dock says so at the right hand end.

  4. 4

    the Check layer, then Dash again, over 260

    Layers, then the plus on Motion

    this is the version somebody builds on their first try, and it is here on purpose. Both motions start at 0, so the badge draws its ring and its tick over the top of each other and reads as a scribble rather than as a mark being made. The dock shows exactly why: two bars with the same left edge. Nothing is broken and nothing warns you. It is just wrong, and the next field is the whole fix.

  5. 5

    600 in the Start field

    Motion, under the Dash entry

    the ring finishes at 420, nothing happens for 180 milliseconds, and then the check draws. That pause is the whole difference between a badge that is being made and a badge that is being dumped on you, and it is a thing you can point at: the dock marks it as a dashed box in the check's lane, labelled with its own number. This is the step the scenario exists for. Everything before it was setup.

  6. 6

    the Fill layer, then Position, from 0, 18 to 0, 0, starting at 860

    Layers, then the plus on Motion

    the fill rises into the badge from underneath, arriving as the check lands. It is one property moving: the fill is a rectangle clipped to the ring, and what changes is where that rectangle is. Not a fade, not a scale, not a colour, not three of those at once, which is what makes it read as the badge filling rather than as something being switched on. Three bars now, left to right, and the total reads 1.20 seconds.

  7. 7

    Draw a curve, and pull the first handle down

    the Curve row, at the bottom of the named list

    all three beats now start quickly and settle, which is how a pen actually moves and how a thing that has weight actually arrives. The named curves are the front door and this is the rest of it: the readout is cubic-bezier(.2, .7, .3, 1), the same four numbers that leave in the file. The little sketch on each Motion row redraws, so you can see without opening anything that the three entries are sharing one curve.

  8. 8

    at the 16 preview, top left, while it runs

    the Real size panel on the artboard

    the sequence survives being small and it stops being the same sequence. At 16 there are not enough pixels in the stroke for the eye to follow a tip, so the ring reads as a wipe; the hold and the fill still land, which is why the badge still says success rather than saying nothing. Worth knowing before it ships into a toolbar: the beats are the part that scales, the drawing is not.

The idea

Drawing a stroke on animates the path, not the shape's position. It needs the path's length and its direction, which a vector tool already has and a video editor never would. Of the four animation scenarios this is the cheapest to build and the easiest to forget exists.

The craft

Three beats, three explicit starts, and a hold you can point at. The badge is 1.20 seconds because each beat has to be separable before anybody can argue about tightening it. The fill arrives by one property moving, which is the difference between a mark being made and a state being switched.

A direction

The dock could stagger a whole group in one gesture: pick eight bars of a chart, say 40 milliseconds apart, and let it lay the starts out. That covers most of what people would otherwise open a full timeline to do, and it is the same Start field being written many times.

Open question

Three lanes fit. Where does one cycle stop being enough, and what replaces it? A document-length timeline is the obvious answer and it drags the whole video surface in behind it. A nested sequence keeps the cycle but makes people think about grouping. Compared against the video flows on .

One app, four jobs: modes you can swap

· decided 2026-09-20 · the chain first, then the arrangement

Answered by the user on 2026-09-20: build modes, and pay the video bill too. They picked the option that takes the whole chain rather than modes alone, so a recording is no longer a separate little editor: it is a layer in an ordinary document with an in and an out. That half has since shipped and was checked in the running app on 2026-09-22: an icon drawn on a grid, saved as a component, dropped onto a recording's timeline and given a stretch of time, with nothing exported and nothing imported at any step. What is left to build is the mode chip and the presets themselves, described in the recommendation at the foot of this page.

The question used to be whether a document should know what kind of thing it is. It should not, and that answer has not changed. What changes is the thing built on top of it: a mode is a preset of what the WINDOW is showing, and you swap it as freely as you change the zoom. Nothing about the document moves when you do, nothing is stored in the file, and everything a mode folds away is one visible click from coming back. The hard part is not the tidy default, it is the moment you reach for a tool that is not in front of you, so the way back is drawn here before the tidy pictures are. And the test that decides whether modes are lenses on one app or four apps in a trench coat is drawn first of all: draw an icon, put that icon in a component, put that component on a video timeline, in one document, with no export and no import at any step, then change the icon and watch all three change.

One honest wrinkle, stated rather than hidden. A mark drawn to read at 24 points, blown up to 190 on a 1080p frame, is a 24 point drawing at eight times scale: the stroke goes up with it and the thing reads chunkier than the icon does. That is correct behaviour for a vector and it is still not what you wanted. The fix is the one the icon epic already has in hand, a component with a variant per size band, and it is not a mode question, so it is named here and left to the icon work.

The bill

Video has to stop being a separate editor for any of this to be true

This is the cost attached to the direction, not a detail. It is worth paying, and it is not small.

Today a recording opens into its own window with its own state (VideoEditorState, VideoEditorView), and that editor knows nothing about layers, components or the document model. It is a good single-clip editor: trim, speed, crop, save. But there is no layer list for a component to be a row in, so nothing can be dragged onto its timeline, and no amount of panel arrangement fixes that. The chain above needs a recording to be a LAYER in an ordinary document whose extent in time the timeline draws.

What paying it buys
What it actually costs
The chain, and everything shaped like it: a title that is a real text layer with your text styles, a callout that is the same component as the one on the screen you shipped, an icon animated on the same timing strip the video uses. One undo stack, one selection, one export.
A recording becomes a layer type with a duration, the timeline becomes a view over layers rather than over clips, and the trim, speed and crop the video editor does today have to be re-expressed as things you do to that layer. The video work already queued (cutting and arranging) would be built on the new footing rather than the old one, which is cheaper than building it twice but is a real re-aim.
It also collapses a second window, a second set of shortcuts and a second mental model that a person currently has to learn separately.
Playback performance is the risk to watch: a composite that has to be right at 30 frames a second is a harder bar than a still canvas, and the renderer's target today is a still document.

Say the honest alternative out loud too: keep video separate and the chain stops at two links. You could still draw an icon, put it in a component, and use that component anywhere in a design document, which is most of the value, and the video editor would keep taking finished pictures. Everything else on this page works either way. The chain is the reason to pay, and it is the user's call whether to.

Where the switch lives, and why it is not a row of tabs. It is a quiet chip in the window's status, beside the document's name, opening this list: the place you already look to find out what this window is set to. Three tabs in the title bar would read as navigating between three applications, which is the exact impression this whole direction is trying to avoid, and the study ruled that shape out for good reason. A popup reads as state. It is also on ⌃1 … ⌃5 and in View ▸ Mode, so the swap never needs the pointer.

The list is not four hardcoded entries. Pixel art is on it because somebody wrote a mode down, not because anybody changed the app, which is section 6. Show everything is the panic button: one click and every folded thing comes back, in every mode, and it is the first item under the line so it is findable when you are annoyed.

Nothing here is stored in the document. Two windows onto the same file can be in two different modes, which is genuinely useful (the icon frame open in Icon mode on one screen while the promo is in Video mode on the other), and neither window is telling the other what the file is.

The risk

Reaching for something that is not in front of you, five ways it goes right

A preset that hides a tool is indistinguishable from a broken app in the second you reach for that tool. This is the section the prototype exists for.

Why it does not feel broken
You reach for the measure tool in Icon modeand it is not in the bar
Press I and the measure tool is simply armed, with a quiet line on the canvas: "Measure is folded away in Icon mode. It is in the tool bar's overflow menu." Nothing was disabled, so the muscle memory works first.
A mode never takes a keystroke away. Shortcuts and the native menu bar hold every tool in every mode, so the worst case is a tool you cannot see rather than a tool you cannot use, and the app tells you where it went.
You go looking with the pointerand there is no Measure button

It is a place you have already learned for a different reason, so nothing new is being taught. A folded tool is one click away and it is visibly one click away.
A panel section is missingMeasurements, say
The Panels button at the foot of the dock lists every group with a tick beside it, and the list inside the panel says in words "You turned this off" rather than leaving a blank where a section was.
Both already exist: the dock's own pane manager is standard chrome on every window in the study, and PanelSectionVisibility.Reason ships the wording that tells a section you folded from a section this document has nothing for. "Where did it go" has a literal answer on screen.
You want it back for goodwithout leaving the mode
Turn it on in the list and it stays on. The chip then reads Icon, edited, and Reset this mode is under the same popup.
Your change belongs to you, not to the preset. A mode you have bent is remembered bent, and nothing quietly re-folds it the next time you swap back.
You want the window you had a second agothe panic case
Show everything in the mode popup unfolds the lot, in one click, and swapping back to the mode you were in restores exactly the arrangement you left.
Proved rather than promised: ModeIsAPresetTests pins that a mode is a set of per-section choices, that swapping away and back lands on the identical set of sections, and that handing the lot back to automatic gives the window of somebody who never touched a mode.

The escape hatch problem, and why this one is not the same trap. The old objection to hiding was that a "show all tools" switch turns the bar into two states of the app to learn. What makes this different is that nothing a mode does is a new place: folded tools go to the overflow menu that already exists, folded sections go to the rail and the list that already exist, and every single thing stays in the native menu bar the whole time. The mode is not a second state of the app; it is a starting arrangement of the one state, and the arrangement was always adjustable by hand. That is also why this is worth doing rather than being a gate in disguise: the app already shipped the by-hand version, and a mode is just a name for a set of those choices you would otherwise make one at a time.

Scope

Exactly what a mode may fold, and what it may never touch

Stated rather than implied, because "it hides some things" is how a feature becomes unpredictable.
Part of the app
May a mode fold it?
The rule
Panel sections
Yes, nine of them
Only the ones already marked optional: library, libraryItem, measurements, motion, placement, columns, arrange, component, shadow. That list is a fact about the running app, not a proposal.
The layers list
Never
It is not in the optional list, and the code refuses to write a choice for it. This is what makes every mode visibly the same app rather than four.
The section for what you picked
Never
Pick a path and you get Path; pick a measurement and you get Measurement. A mode that could fold this could leave you holding something with no way to change it.
Appearance and Effects
Never
Every layer has them and every job wants them. Folding them would be hiding the point of the panel.
Tool groups in the bar
Yes, into the overflow
By group, never one tool out of a group, so the bar keeps the same shape. It is the same overflow menu a narrow window already uses, with one honest change: that button only appears today when the bar runs out of room, and a mode that folds tools has to make it permanent, because there is now always something in it.
Shortcuts
Never
Every tool keeps its key in every mode. Pressing it arms the tool and says where it lives.
The native menu bar
Never
The macOS menus stay complete in every mode, so the whole app is always one menu away and Help ▸ Search always finds it. This is the safety net the whole idea rests on.
The timeline
Folded, not removed
It appears because the document has time, never because of a mode. A mode may fold it to a lip at the foot of the window; the lip stays visible and says what it is.
The document
Never, in any sense
No layer is hidden, no value is changed, nothing is written to the file. Pinned by ModeIsAPresetTests: the same document answers the same under every mode.
Open set

What a mode is made of, and a new one written out: pixel art

Four hardcoded modes would be four information architectures in a costume. This is the whole of a mode, and every line of it points at something the app already has.
Mode · Pixel art
Name and icon
Pixel art, grid icon, fifth on the list, ⌃5.
Panel sections folded
Measurements, Placement, Columns, Component, Shadow. Kept: Layers, the picked layer's section, Appearance, and Motion, because sprites animate and folding Motion would be wrong here even though it looks like a design thing.Written as the same per-section choices the panel already stores.
Tool groups in front
Select · Marquee · Pencil · Fill · Eyedropper. Everything else folds into the overflow menu.The pencil is the existing brush with its own defaults, not a new tool.
A new document starts at
32 × 32, zoom 1600%, transparent.Same mechanism as the sizes already in New Frame.
What the canvas shows
Grid at one pixel, and magnification that shows squares rather than smudges past 2×.Both exist: CanvasGridStore with its settings panel, and magnifyNearest in the renderer, which already mirrors the canvas rule past 2×.
What NEW work starts as
Pencil at 1 pixel, hard edge, no antialiasing, snapped to the pixel grid.A default for the next stroke you make, the same way the pen already starts at 2 points inside a 24 point icon frame.
What it is NOT allowed to say
"Draw everything in this document without antialiasing." That would reach back into work that already exists and change how it comes out, which is the line below.

Three of the five ingredients of a pixel art mode are already running in the app, which is the strongest evidence that a mode is an arrangement rather than a new subsystem: the grid is settable per document with its own store and settings panel, the renderer already switches to nearest-neighbour magnification past 2× because it assumes you are inspecting pixels, and an icon frame already changes what a stroke drawn inside it comes out as. Pixel art needs a pencil default and a 32 point size on the list. Nobody has to touch the editor to describe it.

A mode MAY
A mode MAY NOT
Fold what the window offers. Panel sections, tool groups, the timeline's lip. Nothing is removed and everything says where it went.
Change how existing work comes out. Switch to Pixel art with a smooth curve already on the canvas and that curve stays smooth. If it re-rendered hard-edged, the document would be in a mode, and swapping would no longer be free.
Set a default for the NEXT thing you make. The pencil starts at one pixel, the new frame starts at 32 square. This is the same kind of thing as the tool remembering its last size.
Touch anything in the file. No layer values, no visibility, no flags, nothing written on save. A mode is remembered per window, next to the zoom.
Change what you are LOOKING at. The grid, the magnification rule, the keylines. These are view settings, exactly like zoom, and nobody thinks the zoom changed their document.
Take a capability away. Every tool keeps its shortcut and its menu item. A mode moves things out of the front row; it never makes them unavailable.

The case that tests the line, drawn out. You are in Pixel art, drawing a 32 square sprite with the pencil. You swap to Design mode to borrow a shape from a component. Every pixel you drew stays exactly as it is, because it is a drawing, not a mode's opinion. What changes is that the grid goes back to whatever the document's grid was, the magnification stops snapping to squares, and a new shape you draw comes out smooth. Swap back and the sprite is still the sprite. If this were not true, a mode would be a document type wearing a better name, and the whole direction would have quietly turned back into the thing that was rejected.

The one place this gets argued is antialiasing, because a person who wants pixel art wants hard edges on what already exists too. The honest answer is that hard edges are a property of the LAYER, one you apply and can undo and can see in the panel. A mode can arm it for new work; it cannot reach backwards. That leaves one thing to build that this page is not proposing, a hard-edge switch on a raster layer, and it belongs to the drawing work, not to modes.

The shelves

The chain starts with a drag, so it matters which shelf you drag off

Settled by scope: History is everything you have ever made, the Library is what this document contains. Drawn in full on the companion page.

In section 1 the bell was dragged off the Library, because the icon was drawn in that document and the Library is the document's own shelf. Had it been drawn last month in another file, it would have come off History, the global shelf that opens over everything with no window in the way. The split is by how far the shelf reaches, not by what the thing is, which is why a screenshot from ten minutes ago and a component you built in June can sit on the same shelf without arguing.

One consequence worth naming here because it is a bill like section 2's: the Library's Media scope shows the global capture folder today (LibraryPanel), which under this model belongs to History. Media in the Library should become this document's own media, and the global captures should be reachable where every other global thing is. That is a move, not a new feature. Before and after

What happens to a document made before modes, and what mode a new one starts in

The short version: nothing happens to the old ones, because there is nothing in them to happen to.

A document from before modes opens in whatever mode that window is in, exactly like a document from before opens at whatever zoom you left the window at. There is no mode stored in a file, no missing value to guess, no dialog in front of a file somebody just double-clicked, and no compatibility promise to keep forever. This is the single biggest practical difference between a mode and the project type this replaces: the type had to be written into the format, and a format is a promise you cannot take back.

A new document starts in the mode the window was already in, unless what you did says otherwise. New ▸ Icon opens in Icon, because you just said the word. A capture opened from History opens in Redline, because that is what a capture is for. A recording opens in Video. Everything else opens where you left off, which for most people is one mode most of the time. Nobody is asked a question at the front door, which was the other half of the old proposal and is not needed once the choice is this cheap to change.

And somebody who never finds modes at all loses nothing. The default arrangement is the one the app has now, with the sections it already shows automatically. A mode is something you opt into when you notice you keep folding the same four things.

Recommendation

Build modes as named presets. Pay the video bill only if the chain is the point.

Three slices, each judgeable on its own. The first two are small and stand alone; the third is the expensive one and is a separate yes.
1

A mode is a named set of the choices that already exist

The panel already stores a per-section yes/no and can say in words why a section is missing. A mode bundles those with a set of tool groups and a couple of view defaults, and puts a chip in the window's status to swap them. Small, and it makes the by-hand feature that just shipped worth having.

2

The way back ships in the same slice, not after

Shortcuts always work and say where the tool went, folded tools sit in the overflow that already exists, the panel list says "you turned this off", and Show everything is one click. Without these, a mode is a bug report.

3

Modes are written down, not coded, and pixel art proves it

If the four we ship are the only four possible, they are four applications. Pixel art is the test: describable as a record, three of its five ingredients already in the app, and nobody opens the editor to add it.

What is deliberately NOT in this recommendation. Nothing is stored in a document, so there is no migration and no format promise. No tool is removed; the native menus stay complete in every mode. And the video work was the expensive sentence on this page, and it has been answered yes and paid for. Video is no longer a separate window: a recording is a layer with a start and an end, and the three-link chain at the top of this page runs in the real app. Modes are what is left.

Cut a recording apart and place the pieces

· usage clickthrough · one scenario

One job: get rid of a fumble in the middle of a take without losing the voiceover over it. Split, trim, move, snap. The thing worth watching is that nothing is ever destroyed: a split makes two layers that read the same recording, and a trim only moves an in point. Eight real clicks on ONE screen, which never re-renders below.

The idea

Cutting is non-destructive by construction. A clip is a layer with an in and an out, so a split is two layers pointing at one recording and a trim is a number changing. Nothing is copied, nothing is deleted, and undo is never a rescue mission.

A direction

Detaching audio is so often the right first move that it could be the default for a screen recording, with the two layers linked until you break the link. The risk is a link people cannot see.

Open question

Dragging a piece left closed the gap by hand. Should a ripple delete (⌥⌫) that closes everything downstream be the taught path instead, and if so, what tells you which tracks it will move?

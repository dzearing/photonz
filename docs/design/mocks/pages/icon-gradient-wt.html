Paint an icon with a gradient

· usage clickthrough · one scenario

One job: take the cloud upload glyph off the grid and make the color version of it, the one that ships as an app icon. The interesting part is that a gradient here is a set of numbers, not a mood. Its type, its angle, each stop's color and each stop's position are all values you can name, so the angle snaps to 15 degrees the way the drawing snapped to whole units, a stop snaps to the midpoint and to its neighbours' spacing, and a position is typed as 58 rather than dragged to about there. Then the whole paint gets a name and the rest of the family follows it. There is one paint control on this page and it moves between slots. Eleven real clicks on ONE screen, which never re-renders below.

  1. 1

    the Fill swatch on the plate

    Properties, in the Paint section

    the paint editor opens on #5B3AD9, the flat color the plate starts on. This is the only color control in Photonz: the same popover handles a solid and all three gradient types, and it moves to whichever swatch you click rather than each slot growing a dialog of its own. The quiet 24 unit grid is still on the artboard, carried over from the glyph document.

  2. 2

    Radial, in the four-way type switch

    the paint editor, along its top

    the ramp and the direction pad appear, and the plate becomes a gradient seeded from the color it already had rather than from a stock preset. That matters: you never lose the color you were working from. The four thumbnails are outcomes, not words, so each one shows what your paint would look like as that type.

  3. 3

    the pivot on the direction pad up and to the left

    the paint editor, in the gradient row

    the highlight moves to 32%, 22% and the plate reads as lit from above, the way a physical object would be. The handle on the artboard moves with it, because the gradient's geometry belongs on the canvas as much as in the panel. The origin is stored as percentages of the plate, so it holds at 1024 and at 16.

  4. 4

    the outer stop, then HEX, and paste #1B1140

    the paint editor, on the format switch under the field

    the far end of the ramp becomes the exact brand ink rather than something dragged close to it. That field also takes rgb(27, 17, 64) and hsl(255 58% 16%), because a brand color usually arrives as text pasted from a spec, and retyping it by eye is how a family drifts apart.

  5. 5

    + on the ramp, then set the new stop to 58%

    the paint editor, on the stop row under the ramp

    a third stop lands and the falloff stops looking like a lens flare. Dragging it snaps to the midpoint, to the other stops, and to even spacing between them, the same idea as the drawing grid one page over, and the card above the ramp takes the position as a typed number. So this stop is at 58, not somewhere in the high fifties.

  6. 6

    Save style, and call it Brand / plate

    the paint editor's footer

    the whole paint, three stops and an origin and all, becomes one named thing in Library › Styles, and the Fill row now shows the name instead of a value. A style does not care whether it holds a solid or a gradient, which is exactly why the picker refuses to treat those as two different features.

  7. 7

    the Glyph swatch

    Properties, one row below Fill

    the picker slides to the glyph slot and comes back to Solid, because that is what the glyph is painted with. It brought its recents and its shades with it, so the plate colors you were just working from are one click away. This is the same popover, not a second one: there is exactly one color surface in the app.

  8. 8

    the Linear type, then hold ⇧ and drag the pad round to 135°

    the paint editor, in the gradient row

    the glyph gets a soft top-left to bottom-right ramp, and the angle lands on exactly 135 because shift snaps it to 15 degree steps. That is the same idea as the drawing grid: a family whose gradients all run at one angle looks deliberate, and one where they nearly do looks careless. The handle on the artboard shows the axis the ramp actually runs along.

  9. 9

    the Check section, and the size strip at the top of the canvas

    Properties, under Style

    two gradients meeting each other is the one place an icon quietly fails, so the check takes the worst point under the glyph rather than an average: 4.9:1, out where the plate is lightest and the glyph is at its palest. The picker's own readout answers a simpler question, contrast against white, and both are worth having. The strip shows the icon at 16, 32, 64 and 128, and the 16 is the one that decides whether the gradient was worth having at all.

  10. 10

    Brand / plate onto the second artboard

    Library › Styles, onto cloud-download on the canvas

    the sibling takes the whole paint at once, origin and stops together, and the style now reads 2 uses. Nothing was copied: both plates point at one definition, so moving the light or retyping a stop moves both icons. That is the difference between a family and two files that happen to look similar.

  11. 11

    All sizes · 16 to 1024

    the Export button in the title bar

    every size is rendered from the paint rather than downscaled from the 1024, so the 16 gets its own honest gradient instead of a blurred copy of a big one. The SVG option keeps the gradients live, which means whoever consumes the icon can still point it at a different style. Two icons, one paint, one definition.

The idea

A gradient is a set of values, not a mood: a type, an origin, an angle, and a list of stops that each have a color and a position. Once it is values, the same precision the drawing grid gave the glyph applies to the paint. The angle snaps to 15 degrees, a stop snaps to the midpoint and to its neighbours, and a position is typed. And because it is values, it can have a name, which is the only reason a family of icons stays a family.

A direction

The gradient could be edited entirely on the canvas, with the ramp drawn along the axis handle and the stops draggable in place, so the panel becomes the numeric view rather than the only view. Photoshop and Figma both make you look away from the artwork to move a stop. The risk is a handle that covers the thing it is painting, which is why here the handle shows geometry and the ramp stays in the panel.

Open question

Should a style be able to hold a paint per appearance, so Brand / plate carries a light-mode and a dark-mode ramp under one name? It would make one style do what two nearly-identical ones do today, and it is how tokens already work. The catch is that an app icon does not switch with the system, so the same style would mean different things in different places.

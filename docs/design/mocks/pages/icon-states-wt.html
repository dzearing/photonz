Animate a toggle between two states

· usage clickthrough · one scenario

One job: the bookmark should fill in when you save something, and empty out when you unsave it. This flow has no time axis in it at all. You are not authoring motion over a duration, you are saying what the icon looks like when it is off, what it looks like when it is on, and how long the change between them takes. So the canvas holds two artboards, neither of which ever moves, and the crossing is shown in the preview where it belongs. It is also the flow with the biggest hole in it, because an SVG on its own cannot feel a tap, and the page says so rather than drawing a control that would have to be invented. Seven real clicks on ONE screen, which never re-renders below.

The app has no answer for any of this yet. A layer has no states, there is no transition between two drawings of the same thing, and nothing exports SVG. The nearest thing that does exist is component variants, which is the argument this page is really making: icon states and component states are the same idea and should not be built twice.

An SVG file cannot feel a tap. A picture dropped into an img tag has no events, so a tap-driven toggle can only be handed over as two states plus a class to swap, and whoever receives it does the swapping. The app can hand over the crossing; it cannot hand over the tap.

    Properties shows this glyph's Appearance, and the header above it says in Off. There is already exactly one state and it is the drawing you have: nothing has been added, the panel is just naming what was always true.

  2. 2

    the + on the States group, and name it On

    the States group header

    a second artboard appears beside the first, identical to it, and the panel now lists two states. The new one starts as a copy on purpose, so it can only ever differ where you make it differ. Nothing on screen is a track or a ruler: a toggle is two drawings.

  3. 3

    the Fill row, and choose Same as the stroke

    Properties, under Appearance

    the right-hand artboard fills in and the left one does not, because you were standing in On when you changed it. The States list now reads 1 difference, which is the honest count of what separates the two drawings and the thing you check when a toggle starts behaving oddly.

  4. 4

    0.22 in the Takes field

    Properties, in the new Crossing section

    the number lands on the arrow between the two artboards, and the preview starts crossing back and forth at that rate. That arrow is the entire animation. There are no keys, because with two states and one duration there is nothing left to say.

  5. 5

    Ease out, then 14 in Overshoot

    Properties, in the Crossing section

    the bookmark now swells slightly past full size and settles, which is the difference between a toggle that acknowledges you and one that merely changes colour. Those two controls are the only place taste enters this flow, and they are the two a person will actually fiddle with.

    This is the hole, and the app says so instead of pretending. Photonz can hand over the crossing; it cannot hand over the tap. Saying that here beats letting somebody discover it after the export.

  7. 7

    at the 16 copy, crossing back and forth

    the Real size card

    the two artboards never moved, and they should not: they are the drawings. Only the preview animates, which is what keeps the states editable while the crossing is running. At 16 the fill carries the whole message and the overshoot is barely visible, which is an argument for leaning on colour rather than on scale in small toggles.

The idea

A toggle is two drawings and a crossing. No keys, no cycle, no duration to lay out, because with two states and one number there is nothing left to describe. The artboards are the states and they never animate; the preview is the only thing that moves.

The consolidation worth having

This is component variants wearing an icon's clothes. The same list, the same "differs in this state" count, the same crossing. If the app builds icon states and component states separately it will have built the same feature twice and they will drift. See .

What is missing, plainly

The trigger. An SVG in an image tag has no events, so the tap belongs to whoever receives the file. The app can export the two states and the crossing; it cannot export the tap, and any mock that draws a working On tap control is lying.

Open question

Should a crossing be one number for both directions, as drawn, or two? On is usually meant to feel quicker than off. Two numbers is more truthful and it doubles the controls on a surface whose whole appeal is that it has almost none.

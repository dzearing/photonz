Animate a picture

· prototype: set a moment, change things, it tweens

For review. Today a picture starts with the plus and a list of properties, then a form of From, To, Start and Over. Here there is nothing to choose: pick a layer, press Add Key, and from then on any change to that layer keys itself at the playhead and the app tweens between. It is the video editor's key model and the video editor's timeline, slimmed for a picture. The whole key flow below really works, so try it on the bell. Two things it borrows from video are named and not drawn: Path, and Draw a Curve.

Never by surprise

The Badge has no keys, so dragging it moves it for the whole picture, the way it always has. Only a layer you pressed Add Key on keys itself, and on that layer a hollow diamond stands at the playhead saying where the next change lands. Your change fills it in.

Only what changed

Add Key alone adds no rows. Drag and Position appears; fade and Opacity appears, keeping its old value at the moments you did not touch. A row you never changed never shows up.

Clicks and keys

Click a key to go to it and pick it, then change the layer. Delete takes it away, drag slides it in time, ⌘Z undoes. Click the line between two keys to pick that stretch and set its Curve. Drag the blue repeats mark to change the length.

Where the strip livesThe video timeline, at the bottom, slimmed: ruler, one row per animated layer, a row under it per changed value. One bar above with previous key, play, next key, the time, Repeat and the timeline toggle.Same place and same reading as video. No scrubber (the ruler is right there), no volume, no track tools: a picture has nothing to cut or hear. K clashes with the Lens, so it is on the review card.
How a picture becomes animatedThe first Add Key opens the strip with a length of 2 seconds, repeating, and the playhead at 0:00. It stays a picture: same Image chip, same layers, same tool bar. Delete the last key and the strip leaves; it is a still again, and ⌘Z brings it back.Nothing to set up and no new document. Two seconds holds a move and a fade. Repeating, because that is what an animated SVG does; the Repeat button turns it into play once.
Showing that a change will keyOnly a layer with keys keys itself. On that layer: a hollow diamond at the playhead on its line, the change landing in it with a small pop, the diamond and the time in the size pill while your hand is on it, a solid diamond on the panel row, and a diamond on the layer in Layers.Every signal is where the eye already is, and the canvas gets nothing permanent (placement contract). Pressing Add Key is the one and only switch, so there is no record mode to forget.
EasingOne row: Between the keys ▸ Curve, Ease in out to start with, plus a right-click on the line between two keys. A new key takes the curve of the stretch it lands in. The full list and Draw a Curve sit underneath, as on video.One question with one answer (D17). Most people never open it and still get a soft move.
Where Animate a property goesThe small arrow beside Add Key, under the heading Advanced, and Animate on the right-click. It adds a key at the playhead, in the same model. The plus and the From, To, Start and Over form are no longer offered; motion already saved keeps playing.It is for the person who wants one value keyed without changing it, never the first step.
Grabbing the layerA press on a layer always moves the layer. The pivot is drawn only while the Rotation row is picked.Today, once a layer rotates, a drag from its middle moves the pivot and not the layer. Found trying this on the real app, and it would break the first thing anybody does.

Selection & canvas

· Design language Design language

Selection is how you tell the app what you mean, and it is shown in exactly three places at once: the ring and handles on the canvas, the highlighted row in Layers, and the contents of the Properties group. Click in any of the three and the other two follow, which is what makes one object read as one selection instead of three coincidences. Drag empty canvas for a marquee, hold Shift to add. Every selection is also a value an agent can name, so select("Headline") from a command lands you in exactly the same state as a click.

Read it left to right. The frame on the canvas, the highlighted Hero card row in Layers, and the Properties group titled Frame are the same fact stated three ways. Nothing selected shows the document's properties; one layer shows that layer; a multi-select shows only what the selection shares. That is the habit the app teaches: look right to see what the current selection can do.

02

One frame, every size

Specimen · 8px to a whole card, same markup

The frame shows bounds, never the object's shape. That is what makes one model work everywhere. A frame that traced the shape had to carry a large corner radius for cards, and below about 52px that radius exceeds half the box, so the browser clamps it into a circle: an 8px square read as a round ring and a 6px hairline read as a stadium. It also sat 9px outside the object, so an 8px selection was three times wider than the thing it selected. Ten pages had each hand-tuned their way around that, between them inventing seven radii and five insets.

So the frame is constant — a 2px hairline radius on the bounds — and the handles adapt instead. Under 44px they shrink and step outside the frame so they cannot cover the object. Under 16px they go away: a 7px handle on a 6px object is not grabbable, and you resize it from the Properties fields. The frame alone still says "this is selected".

03b

Proposal · cleaner handles and a 3D mode

for review Specimens · not shipped yet

Try it live on the card in section 01 at the top: click the cube beside the rotate knob, then drag the edge pills (Shift for 15° steps, Escape to leave). Why. On a selected card today the frame is hard to look at: square-cornered grips, a stem that runs into the rotate knob, labels sitting on the grips, and three big coloured 3D arrows plus a crosshair that cover the card and its neighbours. This section proposes the fix in four parts. Parts 1 to 3 are polish to the frame everyone sees. Part 4 turns 3D into a mode you switch on from a button by the rotate knob, and offers three ways to draw it.

1 · Grip cornersshown at 4×

Every grip is 9px with a 2px corner, the same softening as the frame. The guide already says 2px; the app draws them square and has to match.

2 · The stem stops at the knobshown at 4×

The stem runs from the frame's top edge to the knob's outer edge and stops. The knob is filled with the panel colour, so nothing shows through it.

3 · Nothing sits on a griplabels and badges
Default

The name and size tag goes below the frame when the frame has a rotate knob, because the knob owns the space above. A component's badge and variant name sit above the top-left corner with a 6px gap, never on the corner grip.

4a · The 3D button, offbeside the rotate knob

A 16px chip, 8px to the right of the knob and centred on it: panel fill, hairline edge, a small cube. Same family as the grips, so it reads as part of the frame, not a toolbar. Tooltip Turn in 3D. Nothing 3D is drawn until it is on.

4b · The 3D button, on3D mode

On: the chip fills with the accent, the frame follows the tilt, and the grips change job (option A below). Click it again, press Escape, or pick another layer to leave 3D mode. The turn stays; only the tools go.

Three ways to draw 3D mode. All three stay inside the design language: one accent, hairline strokes, panel-filled grips with 2px or round corners, nothing bigger than the knob except where the option needs it, and no coloured arrows.

A · Tilt from the edgesrecommended

How it works: drag the top or bottom pill to tip the card forward or back, the left or right pill to turn it. The knob still spins it flat. Drag the small pivot to move what it turns around, and Option-drag the pivot to push it nearer or further. Shift snaps to 15°.
Good: it adds nothing outside the frame, so it can't cover neighbours. The grips stay where your hand already is, and the frame shows the real tilt.
Cost: no resizing while in 3D mode (the corners hide), which is also what tells you the mode is on.

B · A small trackballfree tumble

How it works: drag inside the ball to tumble the card any way, drag its rim to spin it flat. Shift keeps to one axis.
Good: one control does every direction, which is how pro 3D apps work.
Cost: the ball covers the middle of what you're turning, and on a small object it covers most of it. Free tumbling is also hard to aim at an exact angle.

C · Axis rings off the cornerprecise

How it works: a 34px badge just outside the top-right corner. Drag the flat ring to tip the card, the upright ring to turn it, the outer ring to spin it. The card itself only moves.
Good: exact, one axis at a time, never on the card.
Cost: your hand leaves the object to use it, and it still needs room past the corner, which a card near the canvas edge doesn't have.

ComparedA · adds nothing past the frame, hand stays on the object, exact by edge · B · covers the centre, free but loose · C · exact, but off the object and needs space past the corner
RecommendationA, with the 3D button and parts 1 to 3. It fixes every complaint (size, overlap, look) without a new kind of control. Typed angles stay in Properties for exact values.
Keyboardnone proposed for the button. Rotate and 3D live where you click. Escape leaves 3D mode.

Not a state the selection can be in. The selected object keeps its frame; the other one, under the pointer, gets a 1px outline and no handles — that is what a click would grab instead. Hovering the selected object changes nothing, because its frame has already said so.

07

Rules

Do / Don't
Mirror the selection across canvas, Layers, and Properties, every time. Highlight a row in Layers without ringing the object on the canvas. Frame the bounds. A rectangle, always, whatever shape the object is. Round the frame to match the object's corners. Below ~52px the radius exceeds half the box and the browser clamps it into a circle. Give every bounds ring the same 2px hairline radius — selection, instance, marquee, hover, lock. Let one of them keep a card-sized radius. A 16px ring reads as an object of its own, so you cannot tell the ring from the thing it is ringing. Show the hover outline on the object a click would grab next. Draw a hover outline on something already selected. Its frame has answered the question; a second ring is noise. Let the handles adapt to the size, never the frame. Draw grab handles on a locked object, or four handles on something too small to grab. Show the measure tag while resizing and hide it at rest. Leave guides or gap tags on screen after the drag ends. Ring a component instance in purple and everything else in accent. Snap silently. Always show the guide and the number it chose. Give the group frame the only handles in a multi-select. Invent a second selection colour or a second frame style per page.

In the API

A selection is a value
select("Hero card")Selects by name. All three places update, exactly as if you had clicked.
select(["A","B"])A multi-select, which draws one group frame and a count.
selection.boundsWhat the measure tag reads, in logical points.
node.transform(w, h, rotate)What a handle drag runs. One command per drag, so one undo step.
node.locked = trueDrops the handles. A locked node still answers select() for inspection.
align(ids, "center")What the align control in Properties runs, and what an agent runs for you.

Because a selection is a named value, an agent can say what it is about to touch before it touches it, and you can watch the ring land on the right object. Measuring the same selection is the Measure tool.

The frame does not change when the window does. What changes is around it: the dock becomes a rail plus an overlay, so the canvas keeps the room the selection needs, and the tool bar folds its overflow into more. Handle hit areas grow while the handles stay the same size on screen, and the measure tag stays with the object rather than docking somewhere else. Press the panel toggle to slide Properties in over the canvas and confirm the row and the ring still agree.

The idea

One selection model everywhere, shown in three places at once. Whether you grabbed a shape, a text layer, or a component instance, the ring, the row and the panel behave the same, so the canvas is predictable the moment you click.

A direction

Selection could carry semantic hints: pick a row of cards and Properties offers "distribute evenly" or "make a component" at the top, the same commands the agent would run, surfaced where you are already looking.

Open question

How aggressive should snapping be? Strong snaps feel crisp but fight a precise nudge. Do we hold a modifier to suspend snapping, or learn a per-document threshold from how often you override it?

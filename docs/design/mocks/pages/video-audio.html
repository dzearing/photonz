In the API

// every mixer action is one call an agent can make track("music").duckUnder("vo", {amount:-8, ms:300}) track("music").fade({inS:1.5, outS:1.5, curve:"ease"}) track("vo").gain(0).solo()
The idea

Audio rides the same time axis as the video timeline, so a mixer is not a separate app. Levels, fades and ducking are just automation over time, drawn where you can see and grab them. The music envelope you watch dip is the exact curve the render reads.

One selection, three places

Pick a lane and the meter on the canvas, the row in Layers, and the channel strip in Properties all follow. The source files sit in Library, scope Media, so audio is not a special case with its own bin.

A direction

Ducking is a relationship, not a baked edit. Point the music at the voiceover and it follows, so re-timing the voiceover re-computes the dip. The same idea drives sidechains, auto-fades on cuts, and loudness targets for export.

Open question

Should ducking default on the moment a voiceover lands on a track, or stay an explicit choice? Smart defaults feel magic until they surprise, and audio surprises are the worst kind.

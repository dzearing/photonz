Draw an icon on the grid

· usage clickthrough · one scenario

One job: draw a new cloud upload glyph that belongs in a set of 199 others, which means it has to agree with them about everything measurable. Same 24 unit box, same 20 x 20 live area, same keylines, same 1.75 stroke. The interesting part is that you never type a coordinate and you never nudge one. Snapping puts every edge, every center and every pen point on a whole unit as you draw, symmetry is a command rather than a second careful drag, and a boolean turns four construction shapes into the one path that gets exported. Eleven real clicks on ONE screen, which never re-renders below.

  1. 1

    the Grid row, and choose Icon · 24 units

    the Grid & snap group, at the top of the dock

    the artboard rules itself into 24 units and the dashed 20 × 20 live area appears inside it. That inset is the set's contract: every one of the 199 glyphs already in the library keeps its drawing inside it, so the whole set sits at one optical size.

  2. 2

    Keylines

    the Show row, in Grid & snap

    two more guides land on the artboard: the 18 × 18 square keyline and the 17.2 circle keyline. A square glyph fills the square, a round one fills the circle, and the two then look the same size even though they are not the same size. That is the trick the whole set is built on.

  3. 3

    Grid under Snap to

    Grid & snap, second section

    every intersection lights up as a target, and Keylines and Geometry come on with it. From here the cursor is attracted to whole units, to the two keylines, and to the edges and centers of anything already on the artboard. Nothing is forced: holding ⌃ suspends snapping for one drag.

  4. 4

    the Rectangle tool across the middle of the artboard

    the floating tool bar, under the canvas

    the drag lands on 9, 10 and stops at 15, 13, and Properties reads 6 × 3. Not 6.04 by 2.97, and no arrow keys afterwards: the pointer could not stop anywhere else, because the grid is what it is attracted to. The readout beside the cursor is the target it is currently locked to.

  5. 5

    the Ellipse tool out from the intersection at 12, 8

    the tool bar, next to Rectangle

    a circle of diameter 8 centered on 12, 8. An ellipse snaps by its center and its diameter, not just by the corners of its box, so a circle you meant to be even is even. The center also caught the artboard's vertical center line, which is why it is on x = 12 rather than near it.

  6. 6

    one more circle, starting from the base's top-left corner at 9, 10

    the artboard

    diameter 6, centered exactly on the corner of the rectangle. That was a Geometry snap, not a grid one: the shapes you have already drawn are targets too. Draw only this one, because the other shoulder is not going to be a second careful drag.

  7. 7

    Mirror across center, or press ⇧⌘M

    the Layers panel menu

    the shoulder is copied to 15, 10, reflected about the artboard's center line. That is exact by construction rather than by aim, which matters because a set of icons is looked at side by side and a half unit of asymmetry is exactly the sort of thing a person sees without being able to say why.

  8. 8

    Union, with all four shapes selected

    Properties, in the Path section

    the seams disappear and the four rows in Layers collapse into one path called Cloud, bounded by 6, 4 to 18, 13. Whole units again, because the union inherited them from its inputs. It stays live too: the four shapes are still in there, so you can push a shoulder later and the outline follows.

  9. 9

    the Width row, and pick 1.75 with round caps

    Properties, in the Stroke section

    the hairline becomes the drawing. 1.75, round caps, round joins, aligned to the center of the path: the same four values the other 199 glyphs use, saved as the style Glyph / stroke 1.75. Pick the style instead and it is one click, and if the set is ever redrawn heavier every glyph moves together.

  10. 10

    the Pen, then click 12, 20 · 12, 16 · 10, 18 · 14, 18

    the tool bar, then the artboard

    four clicks, four whole units, and the shaft sits on x = 12 because the artboard's center line is itself a snap target. The head's two arms are symmetric for the same reason the shoulders were. This is the point of the whole exercise: an arrow that is half a unit off center is what makes a hand-drawn glyph look hand-drawn.

  11. 11

    Check against the set

    Grid & snap, on the Fit row, with the whole glyph selected

    the drawing measures 12 × 16 inside the 20 × 20 live area, with margins of 4 · 2 · 4 · 2, and the strip at the bottom of the canvas shows it at the sizes it will actually be used: 16, 24, 32, 48, on both a light and a dark ground. Everything the panel says is a number, so "does this match the set" has an answer instead of an opinion.

  12. 12

    Add drawing to the set, or press ⇧⌘A

    the Library group header, on the + menu

    cloud-upload joins the Assets scope beside cloud, upload and download, and exports as a 24 × 24 template SVG with the stroke left as a stroke rather than outlined, so whoever consumes it can still restyle it. The set is 200 glyphs and every one of them still agrees about the grid.

The idea

Icon work is arithmetic wearing a drawing tool. Every question a glyph has to answer to join a set is a number: which box, which live area, which keylines, which stroke. So the grid is not a backdrop, it is what the cursor is attracted to, symmetry is a command rather than a careful second drag, and every readout in Properties is a whole unit because snapping put it there. Nobody typed a coordinate on this page.

A direction

The fit report could grade the glyph against the set rather than against the template: compare its ink coverage and its optical center to the 199 that already exist, and say "this reads about 8% heavier than the set average" instead of only "it fits". That is the check a person actually does by squinting at a contact sheet, and it is the sort of thing a machine is better at.

Open question

Where should snapping stop? Grid, keylines and geometry cover almost everything, but real sets also want tangency, equal spacing between siblings, and matching a neighbouring glyph's optical weight. Turn all of those on at once and the cursor starts guessing. Is the answer a priority order, or one modifier key that promotes a target while you drag?

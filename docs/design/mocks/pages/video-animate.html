Animate with the overlay

· type transform values beside the object, and animate only what you touch

Move, resize, turn or tilt a layer and a small glass bar appears beside it with the numbers to type into and a Reset. Click a key on the timeline and the same bar opens beside the key, holding that property's value there. A layer's track lists only what is animated: change something on a layer that has keys and it joins; pick a row and press Delete and it leaves. The graph under a row takes points you add and segments you pick, and every property behaves the same way.

Try it

Drag the title to move it, a corner to size it, the knob above it to turn it, and the knob's flat or tall arc to tilt it. The bar appears under it: type +40 into X, press Tab, type 12 into the angle. Reset puts size, turn and tilt back; position stays. Pick another tool, press Esc or click empty canvas and it goes.

Keys

Click a diamond on the Opacity row: the playhead jumps to it and the bar opens beside it. Type 40. Click a diamond on the clip's key line: the bar opens on the canvas instead, ready for you to drag or type; whatever you change there is keyed at that moment.

Only what is animated

The track lists only Position and Opacity. Click Opacity's name and press Delete: it leaves, and the title keeps the opacity it had. Right-click the clip's key line for Animate to bring it back, or move the playhead and turn the title: Rotation joins on its own.

Where the bar sits

· never on the handle in your hand, never off the canvas

One rule, applied in order: under the object, 12 points below its frame; above it (over the turn knob) when you are dragging a bottom handle or there is no room below; slid sideways so it stays 12 points inside the canvas; and never over the tool bar. It follows the object while you drag and keeps the side it chose when the drag began, so it never jumps under your pointer.

The same bar, opened from a key

· one property, its value at that key, and Reset

Click a key on any row and the bar opens above the key, never over it: the property's name, its value in its own units, and Reset to the property's default. Return or Tab commits, Esc closes. Easing is not repeated here: it stays in the timeline bar while keys are picked, and on the right-click of a key or a segment.

The graph: points you add, segments you pick

· in the row's own lane, on the timeline's clock

Right-click a row and choose Show Graph (or click the curve at the end of its name): the row grows and draws the value over time, lined up with the playhead. Click a segment to pick it and its two handles appear; drag them to shape the ease, or right-click it for the easing list. Double-click the line (or Option-click it) to add a point there. Drag a point to move it in time and value; Shift keeps it to one direction.

Hover

Resting on the line lights up the segment under the pointer, so you can see what a click will pick. Holding Option shows a hollow point where a click would add one.

Pick

A picked segment turns blue and shows its handles. Shift-click adds more segments or points. The timeline bar's Easing then names the curve and sets it for everything picked.

Units

The value axis is in the property's own units and fits itself to the curve. Position plots the distance travelled, so its points move in time only; type its X and Y in the key's bar.

Joining

On a layer that has keys, any change keys itself at the playhead, from the canvas, the bar or the panel. A property that was not animating joins with its old value kept at the layer's other key moments, so the change belongs to that moment only.

Leaving

Click a row's name to pick the row, then Delete: its keys go in one undo step, the row leaves, and the property keeps the value it had at the playhead.

Adding back

Right-click the clip's key line (or the clip) for Animate, grouped as the panel is: Transform, 3D, Look, Effects. It puts a key at the playhead and the row appears. The same list is Clip ▸ Animate in the menu bar.

What a Photoshop user expects

Free Transform shows X, Y, W, H and the angle as numbers while you handle the layer, with a link between W and H. The bar is that, beside the object instead of at the top of the window.

What a Premiere or After Effects user expects

After Effects' U: only the properties that have keys. A diamond per key, a click to pick it, Delete to remove. The graph editor's segments, handles and easing presets, in the row instead of a separate window.

Open question

When a change on a layer with keys is keyed: always, only while a record switch is on, or only on a key moment. The decision card asks; this page draws the first.

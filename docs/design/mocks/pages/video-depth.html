Depth (2.5D)

· flat layers that turn toward you and move back and forth in space

Any layer (a title, a picture, a shape, a group, a clip) can turn about its sideways and upright axes and move nearer or further away, seen through one camera with real perspective. That is all 2.5D is: Keynote's and Apple Motion's kind of 3D, flat cards in space, not modelled objects. It adds handles to the turn knob you already use, one short section to the panel, and nothing to the tool bar.

Where each control lives

Canvas: the turn knob grows two arcs (tilt and swing) and the pivot crosshair grows a depth arrow. Panel: a 3D section, four rows, each keyable. Tool bar: nothing new, because turning is not a tool you pick. Layer menu: Reset 3D Rotation, Bring Nearer, Send Further. Right-click a layer: the same three.

Try it

Rest on the knob above the title, then drag the flat arc sideways to swing it or the tall arc up and down to tip it. Drag the knob itself to turn it flat, as today. Drag the arrow at the crosshair up to push the title away. Hold Shift to step in 15° turns. The panel follows.

On the timeline

A turned layer is just an animated layer: its keys are marks on its clip, and opening the track lists one row per property it animates (Rotation Y, Depth). Nothing marks it as 3D there; the canvas already shows the tilt.

Three ways to turn a layer in 3D

· pick one on the decision card

Each canvas below holds a big layer and a small one. Click either to pick it, then turn it. All three share the depth arrow on the crosshair and the panel rows; they differ only in how you grab the turn.

A

Arcs on the turn knob

The knob you already turn with grows a flat arc (swing) and a tall arc (tip) when the pointer rests on it. No mode, no new key to learn, and the arcs stay the same size on a tiny layer. Hardest part: the arcs are small targets, so they get a generous hit area.

B

Edges you tip, in a 3D frame

LaunchShip it faster.
NEW

Click the turn knob to switch the frame to 3D: the side handles become tabs. Pull the top tab down and the top comes toward you; push a side tab in and that side swings away. Feels like holding a card by its edge. Costs a mode (click the knob or press Esc to leave it), and on a small layer the four tabs crowd together.

C

Rings around the pivot

LaunchShip it faster.
NEW

Final Cut Pro's and Motion's 3D control: a red ring tips, a green ring swings, a blue ring turns flat, all centred on the pivot. Familiar to anyone who has used those apps and the most precise, but it is a second turn control beside the knob, and on a small layer the rings cover it completely.

Out of scope

Extruded type, lights, materials, shadows cast between layers and imported 3D models. Layers stay flat; a group turns as one flat card.

What a Photoshop or Final Cut user expects

Photoshop's 3D is gone, so its users bring Keynote's habits: the knob, the panel numbers. Final Cut users expect rings (design C) and Rotation X and Y rows, which the panel has under the same names.

Keys

Every 3D row takes keys the way every other row does: the diamond on the row sets or removes a key at the playhead, and the timeline draws it on the clip.

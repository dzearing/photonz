Ask · the agent surface

· Surfaces & primitives Component library

A box you type into, and changes you watch happen. That is the whole promise, and everything here protects it. The conversation is made of exactly two things: short lines the agent says, and work cards that say what it is doing. Ask is also the ⌘K command palette, so a sentence and a command land in the same place.

It is called a work card because that is what it tells you. "Plan card" would promise a plan agreed up front, and the list changes as it goes. "Tool step" is the row inside it. So: one card per stretch of work, .step rows within — collapsed by default, because while it runs you want one line that keeps telling you what is happening, not a list to watch.

01

Anatomy of a work card

A card per stretch of work. Collapsed by default, and its header is a live sentence rather than a label — that single line is how you follow a run without reading anything. Open it and you get the receipt, readonly: the way to change your mind is to say so in the composer, which is right underneath.

Keep the collapsed line current. It is the only thing most people read. Let the title reflow to two lines. A card that twitches on every step is unwatchable.

API a card is a list of the same commands the UI issues: layer.set("headline","fontSize",44). There is no agent-only command, which is why the receipt can be rendered — and why After/Before is possible at all.

02

Four states, one composer

Idle, busy, steering, done. The composer is present and live in all four — it is the only control that never changes, because the answer to "this is not what I wanted" is always the same: say so, here. The send button's label is the only thing that moves.

Say a real failure in prose. The card is the receipt, not the announcement. Print ⏎ queue · ⇧⏎ interrupt beside the field. Two hints read as two controls and match nothing else in the app.

Interrupting is the Stop on the card, next to the thing being interrupted — not a modifier on the send button. Stopping keeps every step that already landed.

03

Work → steer → new work

Steering leaves a seam, and the seam has to be visible or the transcript lies about itself. It is three entries, not one card mutating: the work that was running stops and says so, your message lands as its own turn, and the new work opens a new card. Scroll back later and you can see why the second card exists.

Let the stopped card keep its steps. Those changes really did happen. Rewrite the running card in place. It erases the fact that you intervened.

The delay is the honest part. Between saying it and seeing it there is a queued acknowledgement, then a stop, then a re-plan. Each gets its own line rather than being smoothed into one, because a user who sees nothing for two seconds assumes they were ignored.

04

Left, because it is an input

The conversation docks on the left, opposite the inspector. It is where you are typing — an input, next to the tool rail and the reading edge — while the right dock is an output you consult. Stacking a tall conversation on top of Layers and Properties made every group there fold, which is a lot of panel to lose for a chat.

Take the width from the inspector and rail it. The canvas keeps its size. Shrink a panel past the point it is usable. Float it instead.

Same node, three placements. The chat moves; it is never duplicated, so there is no second transcript to keep in step. Hitting Ask while it is docked floats it back rather than opening the empty overlay it was moved out of.

The calls this page makes

QuestionAnswerWhy, and what it gives up
What is the card called?Work card, .workIt says work is happening, then what work happened. "Plan card" promises a plan agreed up front; the plan changes. "Tool step" is the row inside it.
Open or closed?Closed, with a live titleWhile it runs you want one line that keeps telling you what is happening. Costs a click to see the list — the rarer need.
Is the list editable?No, readonlyPer-row controls invite the accident that makes an agent feel broken. Changing your mind is a sentence in the composer, which is always right there.
Toggle, or a diff view?An After/Before switch on the cardA diff is a second surface to build and to learn, and you cannot glance at it. The switch flips in place and the row values swap emphasis. Costs a warning line so a stray flip never reads as failure.
Per step or per card?Per cardOne switch cannot leave the canvas half-agreeing with the report. Costs the ability to keep four of five changes — that is what "ask again" is for.
Can you type while it works?Always. The button says QueueDisabling the composer while busy is the most common mistake in this class of UI.
What does steering look like?Stop, your message, a new cardThree entries in the order they happened. Costs vertical space, and buys a transcript that explains itself a week later.
One line or many?Grows to five, then scrollsA design change is a sentence, not a search term. Enter sends; Shift+Enter is the newline.
Pictures?Paste or drag one in"Make it look like this" is the most natural thing a designer says. Shots come from capture history or the clipboard and ride along as chips.
Float or dock?Float by default, dock left on demandMost asks are one sentence and want the biggest target. Supervising wants the canvas visible, so the same chat moves left and the inspector rails.

In the API every step is a command the UI can also issue, and the card is the transaction that wraps them: history.perform(work). That is what makes the receipt renderable and After/Before possible — if the agent had a private path into the document there would be nothing to list, and nothing to put back.
